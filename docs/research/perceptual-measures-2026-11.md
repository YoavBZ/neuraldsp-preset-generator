# Auditory models lead an untested field

*Research, 2026-11-03, for the ML session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was installed or run.*

No open measure has been tested against listeners on guitar amp or effect setting changes. So every ranking here is a prediction from how a model works, backed by tests on other music. On that basis, the best partners for the judge are two auditory models, **HAAQI** (MIT) and **PEAQ's separate outputs** (LGPL), plus **reverb cues from an impulse response estimated between the two renders**. Both models score about 0.84–0.89 against listeners on other music. Their design should shrink small EQ steps and enlarge reverb, the opposite of the judge's errors, though HAAQI may over-score input gain. The impulse-response cues are the only reverb measure checked against listeners on real music mixes. Embeddings track reverb but also treble EQ, so they are unlikely to fix the tone errors. The judge already orders time effects fairly well (0.72). Its fault is the exchange rate *between* kinds of change.

## The judge misses decay, not bands

The ear's ratings are F44 in the [pipeline fix plan](../pipeline-fix-plan.md): one listener, one single-note lead DI, two presets (PR12 clean, SW50R crunch), 26 blind level-matched pairs, 24 of them scored once the two catches are set aside. With 24 pairs, the judge's Spearman of 0.43 has a 95% interval of about 0.02–0.72 (Fisher interval). So the variants (0.43–0.50) cannot be told apart. Agreement also differs by preset: 0.71 on PR12 clean, 0.07 on SW50R crunch. A 0.40 reverb mix moves the direct-to-reverberant ratio (D/R) from dry to near 0 dB (estimate). There its just-noticeable difference (JND) is smallest, about 2–3 dB ([Larsen et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2677334)), so the change spans many JNDs. Perceived reverb also depends on decay time ([De Man et al. 2017](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/items/274cc3b0-af9d-4da7-81a6-8204cf48e65f)). The judge hides this three ways: it averages over mostly note-on frames, removes the wet render's extra loudness and ignores forward masking (inference). For steady changes, it weighs all 64 bands equally above its floor (40 dB under the loudest band; [measuring-closeness.md](../measuring-closeness.md)). A loudness model would instead shrink changes in weak bands, such as the top end (inference).

Cambridge's loudness programs (row 4's note) go to the user to check.

**1. HAAQI.** Fitted to music with filtering, clipping, compression and noise, it reached r = 0.970 ([Kates & Arehart 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4849486/)). Independent tests gave 0.84 on coding and 0.92 on pre-echo ([Torcoli et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11438)). It is the Cadenza challenges' main metric ([CAD1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05799)). Spectral-shape differences move its score by at most about 0.16 of its range (my arithmetic), so the treble step should shrink. But it weighs clipping heavily, so input gain may score large. Read the four `raw` sub-terms, with one fixed `level1`. Run both directions, in case silence selection drops a dry reference's tail ([haaqi.py](https://github.com/claritychallenge/clarity/blob/main/clarity/evaluator/haaqi/haaqi.py)).

**2. PEAQ outputs.** Skip the overall grade, a codec "annoyance" scale that will probably bottom out on a large reverb. On ODAQ music, the noise-to-mask ratio scored 0.89, low-pass 0.95, and pre-echo 0.90 ([Dick et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00742)). Pre-echo, a temporal smear, is the closest tested condition to reverb. AvgLinDist, an EQ-type loudness loss, scored 0.84 alone on music with its mapping re-fitted ([Delgado & Herre](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01467)). It needs the advanced version, which only GstPEAQ implements openly. GstPEAQ fails strict conformance but tracks listening scores at 0.98 on 16 test items ([DAFx-15](https://www.ntnu.edu/documents/1001201110/1266017954/DAFx-15_submission_12.pdf/532752d5-8e95-41da-bd5c-5ddccd5ab2e7)). Whether it prints each output is unconfirmed ([GstPEAQ](https://github.com/HSU-ANT/gstpeaq)). PyEvalAudio offers the two-output "2f-model" (0.90 on coding) in NumPy ([repo](https://github.com/peladeaucome/PyEvalAudio)). PEAQ has two known failures here. The advanced version did not rise steadily with added hum, hiss and clipping ([He et al.](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/details/zPYxwsZQRNgBM4XY2Azt4A)). And some outputs skip the first 0.5 s, much of a 1–2 s note ([Kabal](https://www.mmsp.ece.mcgill.ca/Documents/Reports/2002/KabalR2002v2.pdf)).

**3. Impulse-response cues.** Estimate the impulse response that turns render A into B from their cross-spectrum. Then read its tail level, early decay time (EDT) and clarity (C80). On real mixes, reverb loudness plus EDT predicted "too wet" better than loudness alone (p = .005) ([De Man et al.](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/items/274cc3b0-af9d-4da7-81a6-8204cf48e65f)). Scale the cues by JNDs: EDT 5%, C80 1 dB ([Christensen et al.](https://odeon.dk/pdf/ISRA2013_Paper_The_ISO_3382_parameters_Can_we_simulate_them_Can_we_measure_them.pdf)), D/R 2–3 dB. Non-linear differences (drive, tremolo, modulated delay) break it, so gate it by A–B coherence. Or render a true impulse response through the plugin's time block. Most delays (100–800 ms) exceed music's 30–50 ms echo thresholds ([Litovsky & Shinn-Cunningham](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/shinn/publications/pdfs/2001/2001jasa_litovsky.pdf)). So echo level against masking should decide audibility (inference). A cheap partner is the judge over the 50–500 ms after each note ends (inference). F44's DI plays throughout (its 3 s tails changed no score), so it has few such windows; the fresh page needs a DI with gaps.

**4. Loudness and modulation distance.** torch-amt ports the Glasberg–Moore loudness models and the Dau and King modulation filterbanks, with no published checks ([docs](https://torch-amt.readthedocs.io/en/stable/)). LoudnessModel is a NumPy version of Moore's 2016 model ([repo](https://github.com/MalcolmSlaney/LoudnessModel)). No tested difference measure uses them, so this row is the project's own. Cambridge's programs show no terms, and a case study mentions fees ([REF](https://impact.ref.ac.uk/casestudies/CaseStudy.aspx?Id=19712)), so the user should check them.

**5. AFx-Rep.** Trained with random gain on 63 plugins, including GuitarSet audio, it tracks reverb settings across content at ρ 0.945–0.969, the strongest reverb evidence for any embedding ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)). It also tracks treble EQ (0.92) and drive (0.94), so it will probably repeat the tone error. Its authors say it "does not work well" for guitar tone matching.

**6–7. MERT and CLAP.** A per-pair MERT distance has the best music evidence of any embedding: 0.69 against source-separation ratings, versus 0.37 for spectral error ([arXiv 2604.20270](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20270)). At 24 kHz it misses the top octave. CLAP's mean-and-spread statistics did best on timbre ratings ([Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07764v1)), but CLAP encodes level strongly, so loudness-match first ([Deng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15900)). Round 4 set these encoders aside because they match notes, not processing ([round 4](round-4-audio-ml.md)). Here both renders share one DI, so the notes cannot drive the distance; whether the encoders are sensitive enough to processing is untested.

**8–11 and excluded.** ViSQOL's scores need not rise with the difference ([ViSQOL v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09584)), and v3 trailed its predecessor on music ([Torcoli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11438)). CDPAM is speech-trained and scored 0.22 on music ([PAMT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04985)). MoSQITo's values are untested as differences ([MoSQITo](https://github.com/Eomys/MoSQITo)). FAD on PANNs scored weakest on effect models (−0.303, [Comunità et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14405)). Fx-Encoder++ (CC BY-NC, 1.6 GB) is untested on guitar or knob steps ([repo](https://github.com/SonyResearch/Fx-Encoder_PlusPlus)). PEMO-Q needs paid MATLAB ([Oldenburg](https://uol.de/en/mediphysics/downloads/pemo-q)). FAD, KAD and MAD compare sets of clips, and PAM, NOMAD and SRMR rate one clip alone. The PREV reverberance model moved 2% with a tenfold change in reverb time, but more than fivefold with a 40 dB change in level ([Lee et al. 2017](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/123888/4/Leeetal2017.pdf)).

## ODAQ checks the code; Osses and TONE3000 check the answer

No open dataset rates knob changes on guitar. **ODAQ** (42 listeners, 10,080 scores, solo acoustic guitar included; CC BY, BY-NC or CC0) publishes each metric's correlation ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10405774); [add-on](https://zenodo.org/records/13377284)). Reproducing NMR's 0.89 checks a PEAQ or ViSQOL setup before it touches guitar. **Osses's reverberance set** (24 listeners, 8 instruments in 8 rooms, audio included; CC BY 4.0) tests whether a row orders reverb amount as listeners do ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7601160)). **TONE3000's A2 test** (105,842 ratings of 37 guitar and bass captures; CC BY 4.0) is the closest steady-tone check ([repo](https://github.com/tone-3000/a2-mushra-data)). But only ratings are posted, and its differences are capture errors, not knob steps. **Comunità et al.'s** ratings of drive and compressor models are not stated as released, so ask; the audio is public ([ToneTwist](https://github.com/mcomunita/tonetwist-afx-dataset)). Nothing open covers delay or cab changes.

## Two corrections, and where sources disagree

[A-closeness.md](round-4/A-closeness.md) needs two corrections. **MoSQITo has no fluctuation-strength module**: its metrics are loudness, roughness, sharpness, speech intelligibility and tonality ([tree](https://github.com/Eomys/MoSQITo/tree/master/mosqito/sq_metrics)). SQAT (MATLAB, GPL-3.0) has one ([SQAT](https://github.com/ggrecow/SQAT)). **HAAQI's 32 bands stop at 8 kHz**; 12 kHz is only its sample rate's limit. A-closeness's proposed sharpness term would also push the wrong way, since on F44's page the judge already weighs treble well above the ear.

The sources disagree in places. This round's embedding notes call Open-Amp unusable for lacking a licence file, but the project rule accepts "no licence stated". It is untested on knob changes anyway. D/R JNDs are 2–3 dB in Larsen and Reichardt, but 5–6 dB in Zahorik and De Man. Snippets about He et al. conflicted, so this report follows the archive abstract. No licence text was found for Kabal's PQevalAudio (C and MATLAB) or PyEvalAudio; the rule treats "no licence stated" as fine. PEAQ patents were not checked.

## What to try first

1. **Declare before scoring:** rows, preprocessing (loudness match, one calibration, each tool's rate, both directions) and reading. Read F44's three Spearman figures (overall, within the effects, within drive, tone and cab), per preset, and the inversions by F44's declared rule (14 for the judge). Suggested screen: fewer inversions than the judge, with within-effects agreement not clearly below 0.72. These 24 pairs only screen; the test is F44's proposed fresh blind page (another DI, other presets), with the rows declared before it is scored.
2. **No downloads first:** the release-window judge and the impulse-response cues in JNDs.
3. **HAAQI sub-terms and PEAQ outputs**, once PEAQ reproduces ODAQ's numbers.
4. **One loudness or modulation row and one embedding row** (AFx-Rep or MERT).
5. **Independent checks:** Osses for reverb order, and TONE3000 if its audio is available. Then listen to new pairs where rows disagree most, adding delay, chords and a second DI.

## Conclusion

Stop looking for one better metric; put each kind of change on its own perceptual scale. Auditory models have the best support for spectral change, and impulse-response cues for reverb. Drive has the least. The best published distortion-effect correlations are only about 0.5–0.6 ([Comunità et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14405)), and HAAQI may over-score input gain, so that is where the next listening trials belong.
