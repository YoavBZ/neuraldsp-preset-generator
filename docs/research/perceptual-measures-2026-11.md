# Auditory models lead an untested field

*Research, 2026-11-03, for the ML session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was installed or run.*

No open measure has been tested against listeners on guitar amp or effect setting changes. So every ranking here is a prediction from how a model works, backed by listener tests on other material. On that basis, the best candidates beside the judge are two auditory models, **HAAQI** (MIT) and **PEAQ's separate outputs** (LGPL), plus **reverb cues** read from the two renders (an impulse response estimated between them, or the PREV model). On codec, filtering and pre-echo tests the two models reach about 0.80–0.93 against listeners (Pearson; HAAQI 0.69 on source separation). Their design suggests they count small EQ steps less and reverb more than the judge does, though HAAQI may over-score input gain. Embeddings respond to reverb, treble and drive settings, but whether they weigh one kind of change against another as the ear does is untested. On F44's answers the judge already orders the effects fairly well among themselves (0.72, on 12 pairs). Its fault is the exchange rate *between* kinds of change, which F45's headphone recheck confirmed.

## Why the judge may under-count reverb and over-count treble (inference)

The ear's ratings are F44 in the [pipeline fix plan](../pipeline-fix-plan.md): one listener, one single-note lead DI, two presets (PR12 clean, SW50R crunch), 26 blind level-matched pairs, 24 of them scored once the two catches are set aside. With 24 pairs, the judge's Spearman of 0.43 has a 95% interval of about 0.02–0.72 (Fisher), and ties among the three answers make that approximate. The variants (0.43–0.50) differ by far less than this; only a paired test over the same 24 pairs could separate them. Agreement also differs by preset: 0.71 on PR12 clean, 0.07 on SW50R crunch.

F44 was heard on phone speakers. F45 re-heard 13 items on headphones (11 pairs chosen for their inversions, one of them twice, and the catch). Inversions by F44's rule rose from 14 to 27 (21 distinct pairs). 5 of the 6 large effects re-heard were heard clearly or very, and so was crunch drive +10 dB, while the three treble steps and clean drive stayed the same or barely different. So F44's treble and reverb readings stand, and its drive reading holds on the clean amp only. F45 suggests the revised judge needs a term for what time effects add (energy and decay after notes, repeats) and less weight on steady treble.

**Reverb.** The page level-matched each pair, as the judge does, so loudness does not explain the gap. The judge compares spectra frame by frame, mostly while notes play. Its frame-by-frame part does see tails, but it has no explicit measure of decay time or space, only spectral change per frame, and under F44's continuous line the reverb overlaps the next notes (inference). Just-noticeable differences (JNDs) for the direct-to-reverberant ratio (D/R) are a few dB: 2–3 dB in [Larsen et al. 2008](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2677334), 5–6 dB in Zahorik and De Man. Perceived reverb also depends on decay time ([De Man et al. 2017](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/items/274cc3b0-af9d-4da7-81a6-8204cf48e65f)). JNDs alone may over-predict, though: reverb mix 0.15 was heard as the same on clean, so masking by the playing notes may limit how much of the tail is heard (inference).

**Treble.** The judge scores only bands within 30 dB of the reference's long-term peak (18–56 of its 64 on K1's halves), weighs each scored band equally in dB, and clamps each frame 40 dB under the reference's loudest band in that frame ([measuring-closeness.md](../measuring-closeness.md)). So a few dB in a weak upper band inside the scored set counts as much as in a loud one. A loudness model with spread of masking might count such changes less. But the project's exploratory Zwicker loudness row, which has spread of masking, and its CDPAM row both put the three treble steps in their top 3, against the ear (F45; `judge-ear/explore/`). A Moore-type model, or HAAQI's ear model, is untested here. Both of the judge's own parts (tonal and frame by frame) also put the treble steps high.

## Ranked shortlist

**L** = listener test on other material (codecs, filtering, separation, mixes), never guitar setting steps; **R** = recovers one effect's own setting value, which shows sensitivity, not agreement with listeners; **P** = predicted from the design. All compare two signals except MoSQITo and PREV, which rate one signal; their values would be subtracted. Every row fits the project's licence rule ([ML plan §5](ml-plan-2026-10.md#5-rules-for-every-step): open, non-commercial or no licence stated is fine), GPL and LGPL code included, except PEMO-Q (paid MATLAB). Cambridge's loudness terms (see row 4) go to the user to check.

**1. HAAQI.** Fitted to music with filtering, clipping, compression and noise, it reached r = 0.970 ([Kates & Arehart 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4849486/)). Independent tests gave 0.84 on coding, 0.92 on pre-echo, 0.80 on band-limiting (where PEAQ's overall grade reached 0.95) and 0.69 on source separation ([Torcoli et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11438)). It tracked added hum, hiss and clipping ([He et al.](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/details/zPYxwsZQRNgBM4XY2Azt4A)). It is the Cadenza challenges' main metric ([CAD1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05799)). In the code, the linear (spectral-shape) term adds at most 0.162 to the score ([haaqi.py](https://github.com/claritychallenge/clarity/blob/main/clarity/evaluator/haaqi/haaqi.py); my arithmetic), which suggests a treble step would count less than in the judge. That is a design argument, not a bound, and the same hope already failed for the project's Zwicker loudness and CDPAM rows (F45): spectral change also reaches the nonlinear terms through the ear model's compression, and on a crunch preset the treble knob changes the distortion itself. HAAQI weighs clipping heavily, so input gain may score large. Read its four `raw` sub-terms, with one fixed `level1`. It drops frames where the reference is silent, so with a dry reference it drops the wet render's tail; run both directions.

**2. PEAQ outputs.** Skip the overall grade, a codec "annoyance" scale that will probably bottom out on a large reverb. On ODAQ, the noise-to-mask ratio (NMR) scored 0.89 overall, 0.90 on low-pass and 0.93 on pre-echo (Pearson; [Dick et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00742)). Pre-echo, a temporal smear, is the closest tested condition to reverb. AvgLinDist, an EQ-type loudness loss, scored 0.84 alone on music with its mapping re-fitted ([Delgado & Herre](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01467)). It needs the advanced version, which only GstPEAQ implements openly. GstPEAQ fails strict conformance but tracks listening scores at 0.98 on 16 test items ([DAFx-15](https://www.ntnu.edu/documents/1001201110/1266017954/DAFx-15_submission_12.pdf/532752d5-8e95-41da-bd5c-5ddccd5ab2e7)). Its element's `console-output` property prints every output; the `peaq` command-line tool turns it off ([GstPEAQ](https://github.com/HSU-ANT/gstpeaq)). PyEvalAudio offers the basic version and the two-output "2f-model" (0.90 on coding) in NumPy ([repo](https://github.com/peladeaucome/PyEvalAudio)). One known failure: the advanced version did not rise steadily with added hum, hiss and clipping ([He et al.](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/details/zPYxwsZQRNgBM4XY2Azt4A)). Some outputs skip the signal's first 0.5 s ([Kabal](https://www.mmsp.ece.mcgill.ca/Documents/Reports/2002/KabalR2002v2.pdf)), which matters only if clips are cut per note.

**3. Reverb cues.** Estimate the impulse response that turns render A into B from their cross-spectrum. Then read its tail level, early decay time (EDT) and clarity (C80). On multitrack mixes, reverb loudness plus EDT predicted "too wet" better than loudness alone (p = .005) ([De Man et al.](https://dspace.library.uvic.ca/items/274cc3b0-af9d-4da7-81a6-8204cf48e65f)); the A-to-B estimate itself has never been tested on renders. Scale the cues by JNDs: EDT 5%, C80 1 dB ([Christensen et al.](https://odeon.dk/pdf/ISRA2013_Paper_The_ISO_3382_parameters_Can_we_simulate_them_Can_we_measure_them.pdf)), D/R a few dB. Non-linear differences (drive, tremolo, modulated delay) break the estimate, so gate it by A–B coherence, or render a true impulse response through the plugin's time block. [Lee et al. 2017](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/bitstream/10453/123888/4/Leeetal2017.pdf) cross-validated two reverberance models against listening data. PREV rates reverberance from audio; EDTN needs an impulse response, so here it would read the estimated A-to-B one. They suggest EDTN for music and PREV for speech, and PREV for relative changes over a small level range, as on F44's level-matched pairs. But PREV moved only 2% with a tenfold change in reverb time, and more than fivefold with a 40 dB change in level. In the reverberance set below, listener ratings correlated with a PREV model's estimates (Osses et al. 2020, JASA Express Letters 148(1), EL93; the [dataset record](https://research.tue.nl/en/datasets/raw-data-and-stimuli-for-assessing-the-perceived-reverberation-in/) names the model). Most delays (100–800 ms) exceed music's 30–50 ms echo thresholds ([Litovsky & Shinn-Cunningham](https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/psychology/shinn/publications/pdfs/2001/2001jasa_litovsky.pdf)), so echo level against masking should decide audibility (inference). A cheap partner is a **release-window judge**: the judge scored only over the 50–500 ms after each note ends (inference). F44's DI plays throughout (its 3 s tails changed no score), so it has few such windows; the fresh page needs a DI with gaps.

**4. Loudness and modulation distance.** torch-amt ports the Glasberg–Moore loudness models and the Dau and King modulation filterbanks, with no published checks ([docs](https://torch-amt.readthedocs.io/en/stable/)). LoudnessModel is a NumPy version of Moore's 2016 model ([repo](https://github.com/MalcolmSlaney/LoudnessModel)); its licence is BSD-2, but its README also says the model is "provided free for any research purposes". Cambridge's own programs show no terms, and a case study mentions fees ([REF](https://impact.ref.ac.uk/casestudies/CaseStudy.aspx?Id=19712)), so the user should check Cambridge's terms. No tested difference measure uses these models, so this row would be the project's own.

**5. AFx-Rep.** It was trained with random gain on 63 plugins, GuitarSet audio included. In ST-ITO, an optimiser using it recovered reverb settings at ρ 0.945–0.969, treble EQ at 0.92 and drive at 0.94, across content ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)). That shows it responds to each setting, not how it weighs a treble step against a reverb step, which is untested. The ST-ITO authors say their system "does not work well" for guitar tone matching.

**6–7. MERT and CLAP.** A per-pair MERT distance has the best listener evidence of any embedding on music: 0.69 against source-separation ratings, versus 0.37 for spectral error (Spearman; [arXiv 2604.20270](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.20270)). At 24 kHz it misses the top octave, and a probing study finds it discards loudness but reads reverb time well, on speech ([arXiv 2607.03806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03806)). CLAP's mean-and-spread statistics did best on timbre ratings ([Tian et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.07764v1)), but CLAP encodes level strongly, so loudness-match first ([Deng et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15900)). Round 4 set these encoders aside because they match notes, not processing ([round 4](round-4-audio-ml.md)). Here both renders share one DI, so the notes cannot drive the distance; whether the encoders are sensitive enough to processing, and weigh it as the ear does, is untested.

**8–11 and others.** ViSQOL's scores need not rise with the difference ([ViSQOL v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.09584)), and v3 trailed its predecessor on music (0.69 against 0.78; [Torcoli et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11438)). CDPAM is speech-trained and scored 0.22 on music ([PAMT](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.04985)). MoSQITo's values are untested as differences ([MoSQITo](https://github.com/Eomys/MoSQITo)). FAD on PANNs scored weakest on effect models (−0.303, [Comunità et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14405)). Fx-Encoder++ (CC BY-NC, 1.6 GB) is untested on guitar or knob steps ([repo](https://github.com/SonyResearch/Fx-Encoder_PlusPlus)). Zimtohrli, a two-signal psychoacoustic measure on ODAQ's list, was not assessed. PEMO-Q needs MATLAB ([Oldenburg](https://uol.de/en/mediphysics/downloads/pemo-q)), a paid tool: in development it needs the user's yes unless the user already pays for MATLAB, and the product may not depend on it (§5). SQAT also needs MATLAB. FAD, KAD and MAD compare sets of clips, and PAM, NOMAD and SRMR rate one clip alone.

## ODAQ checks the code; Osses and TONE3000 check the answer

No open dataset rates knob changes on guitar. **ODAQ** (42 listeners, 10,080 scores, solo acoustic guitar included; CC BY, BY-NC or CC0) publishes each metric's correlation ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/10405774); [add-on](https://zenodo.org/records/13377284)). Reproducing NMR's 0.89, or ViSQOL v3's 0.77, checks a PEAQ or ViSQOL setup before it touches guitar. Its items are coding and separation degradations (dialogue enhancement included), and its figures are Pearson where F44's are Spearman. **Osses's reverberance set** (24 listeners, 8 instruments in 8 rooms, audio included; CC BY 4.0) tests whether a row orders reverb amount as listeners do ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/7601160)). It has no dry version and its ratings are absolute, so a two-signal row needs a chosen reference room. **TONE3000's A2 test** (105,842 ratings of 37 guitar and bass captures; CC BY 4.0) is the closest steady-tone check ([repo](https://github.com/tone-3000/a2-mushra-data)). But only ratings are posted, and its differences are capture errors, not knob steps. **Comunità et al.'s** ratings of drive and compressor models are not stated as released, so ask; the audio is public ([ToneTwist](https://github.com/mcomunita/tonetwist-afx-dataset)). Nothing open covers delay or cab changes.

## Corrections, and where sources disagree

[A-closeness.md](round-4/A-closeness.md) is kept as written; two of its claims are corrected here. **MoSQITo has no fluctuation-strength module**: its metrics are loudness, roughness, sharpness, speech intelligibility and tonality ([tree](https://github.com/Eomys/MoSQITo/tree/master/mosqito/sq_metrics)). SQAT (MATLAB, GPL-3.0) has one ([SQAT](https://github.com/ggrecow/SQAT)). **HAAQI's 32 bands stop at 8 kHz**; 12 kHz is only the limit of its 24 kHz sample rate.

Its sharpness term, which round 4's synthesis also proposes, needs a narrower reading. On F44-type steps (an amp's treble knob, render against render), it would push the wrong way, since the judge already weighs those above the ear. Its original reason still stands: against real recordings, a +12 dB octave at 8 kHz costs the judge only +0.10 dB, because those bands fall outside the scored set ([measuring-closeness.md](../measuring-closeness.md)). Both can be true.

The sources disagree in places. This round's embedding notes call Open-Amp unusable for lacking a licence file, but the project rule accepts "no licence stated"; it is untested on knob changes anyway. D/R JNDs are 2–3 dB in Larsen and Reichardt, but 5–6 dB in Zahorik and De Man. Snippets about He et al. conflicted, so this report follows the archive abstract. No licence text was found for Kabal's PQevalAudio (C and MATLAB); the rule treats "no licence stated" as fine. PEAQ patents were not checked.

## What to try first

1. **Declare before scoring:** the rows, the preprocessing (loudness match, one calibration level, each tool's rate, both directions) and the reading. Read F44's three Spearman figures (overall, within the effects, within drive, tone and cab) per preset, and the inversions by F44's declared rule on both answer sets, reported apart: the phone (F44; 14 for the judge) and the headphones (F45; 27, or 21 distinct pairs). Caveats: the phone's 14 rest on 3 answers (7 on crunch reverb 0.40 alone), so a row that simply ranks those pairs higher passes; F45's 11 pairs were chosen for their inversions, and pairs not re-heard could form new ones; and the within-effects 0.72 rests on the phone's 12 effect pairs (95% interval about 0.23–0.92). A suggested screen, fixed in the declaration: fewer inversions than the judge on both sets, and within-effects Spearman of at least 0.5 on the phone answers, the only full set. These pairs only screen; the test is F44's proposed fresh blind page (another DI, other presets), with the rows declared before it is scored.
2. **Nothing to install first:** the release-window judge and the reverb cues in JNDs.
3. **HAAQI sub-terms and PEAQ outputs**, once the PEAQ setup reproduces ODAQ's NMR figure.
4. **One loudness or modulation row and one embedding row** (AFx-Rep or MERT).
5. **Independent checks:** Osses for reverb order, and TONE3000 if its audio is available. Then listen to new pairs where the rows disagree most, adding delay, chords, a DI with gaps and a second DI.

## Conclusion

One hypothesis follows from this: give each kind of change its own perceptual scale (auditory models for spectral change, reverb cues for time effects) rather than look for one better metric. It has limits. Joining the scales needs a rule, which is a weight, and F44 says nothing on its page fits a new weight without its own check. And a candidate render against a recording differs in every kind at once, so the difference cannot be split by kind; the scales would have to be read together. The shortlist's single measures are the simpler test of the same idea. Drive has the least support: the best published correlations on distortion-effect models are about 0.5–0.6, and on overdrive only about 0.36 ([Comunità et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14405)), and HAAQI may over-score input gain. So drive, on chords as well as single notes, is where more listening would tell the most.
