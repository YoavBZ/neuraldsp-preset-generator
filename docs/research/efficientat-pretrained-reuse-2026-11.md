# Screen EfficientAT frozen, from its middle blocks

*Research, 2026-11-06, for the ML models and scoring review session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

*Tags: **[M]** measured and published; **[R]** measured in this repo; **[I]** inferred; **[none]** no evidence found. Every test design and pass bar below is an untested proposal by the researchers, not a published protocol. This report builds on PR #394's report, [`docs/research/model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md`](model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md), on the latest results, [`docs/model-comparison-results.md`](../model-comparison-results.md), and on [`docs/research/proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md`](proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md) and [`docs/research/perceptual-measures-2026-11.md`](perceptual-measures-2026-11.md). It does not repeat them. The researchers' working notes are not in the repo.*

**Recommends:** test EfficientAT once, frozen, read from its middle blocks beside the mel, with the plugin-to-real transfer read (T1) as the key test. The two gains that made it strong on AudioSet are distillation from a 9-model PaSST ensemble (+5.3 to +5.7 mAP) and an ImageNet start (+1.2 to +1.6). Both need AudioSet's 2M clips, and we get them only by using the weights [M, I]. What carries to 520 labels is how the weights are read. Pooled middle blocks plus a time-averaged log-mel beat the network's own final embedding by 10 points on HEAR. On music tasks the log-mel alone beat every network-only feature set in the paper's Table II: 88.3 against 74.7 at best [M]. Outside the authors' own work, a frozen EfficientAT beat a mel summary mainly where time structure matters (synth LFO rates). It tied on static spectral colour. In both independent cases with plentiful task data, it lost by 17 to 27 points to a net trained on the task [M]. Tone and cab are mostly static colour, so the comparison's mel ridge is the right control, and a tie is the likeliest result [I]. No published work reads amp, drive or effect settings with EfficientAT, with style statistics of a pretrained CNN, or with tagger classes [none]. The test is cheap: zero renders, about an hour of CPU rebuilding, minutes of network time, and one checkpoint of about 20 MB. It also needs torchaudio, which is the user's call [R, I].

## Recommends: try these first, in order

Each row is read before the next starts. "Clears" means the paired 90% interval's lower end is above 0, read by `learn/reads_v3.py` on held GOAT, as in [PR #394](model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md). The player-clustered check is descriptive, reported beside it as in PR #394: a pass that vanishes when clustered by player is only a lead [I]. The comparison's paired cab intervals imply an SE of about 0.035–0.04, so a cab gain of about +0.06 clears; drive and tone need about +0.07 to +0.09 [R, I].

## 1. EfficientAT's own papers: distillation and the readout matter, width barely does

The MN paper and the DyMN paper vary the training recipe at full AudioSet scale. Distillation dominates. Channel SE (+1.7), coordinate attention (+2.3 over a static MN without SE) and an ImageNet start (+1.2 to +1.6) come next. A coarser hop or fewer mels cost up to 2.4. Every other training choice moved mAP by about a point or less ([MN paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04772v3); [DyMN](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15648v1)) [M]. Neither paper ablates strides, input length, AudioSet subset size or balanced sampling. Neither compares frozen with fine-tuned use of the same model on the same task [none]. The lesson that carries is the authors' embedding paper, whose result needs only the weights. Pooled blocks B5, B11, B13 and B15 (472-d at mn10), plus the time-averaged 128-band log-mel, beat every other feature set in its Table II, and removing any one of those blocks lowered the score ([embedding paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.01879v2)) [M]. Width gains above mn10 are small both on AudioSet and downstream. mn10 to mn30 added 1.1 mAP and 0.4–1.4 points on the fine-tuned tasks [M, I, from the DyMN paper's Table I]. Channel and frequency SE, and coordinate attention, are the only architecture ideas we could graft onto our own CNN. They are hypotheses here, since every gain was measured with distillation on 2M clips [I].

## 2. Independent uses: wins on time structure, ties on colour

Independent uses are few and mostly in scene classification. EfficientAT has no published use on audio effects, amp settings, guitar timbre or perceived quality [none]. The closest is Combes et al. 2025, read from its figure. There the family's lead over a time-averaged mel comes almost entirely from LFO-rate controls, while filter cutoff, resonance and reverb wet tie at 0.997–1.000 ([figure](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pcmbs/synth-proxy_audio-model-selection/main/assets/audio_model_eval_results.png); [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07635)) [M]. Against PaSST and CLAP it ranked a little ahead on synth sweeps [M]. Against PANNs it led in the authors' own HEAR run, where the recipes differ, and in one independent tagging task, against the small CNN6 [M]. No independent study puts it beside BEATs under the same probe [none]. Both independent cases with plentiful task data show frozen EfficientAT losing to a net trained on the task [M]. That is our plugin-render situation, which is why only transfer (T1) can make the case [I].

## 3. Few-label reuse: middle layers, frequency kept, frozen first

The published practice points one way on every point that matters here. Read a middle layer, or a middle and a last one fused, never the last alone. On CNN14 the tasks most like ours (NSynth timbre, Surge synth sounds) gained 7.8 to 22 points from adding a middle layer. The last layer won only on tasks like the pretraining labels ([Niizumi 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.08138)) [M]. Keep the frequency axis and pool over time with mean and std [M, I]. Start frozen. At 520 labels, frozen probes are a strong baseline in the published size studies, and fine-tuning wins only with more data [M, I]. If frozen fails but transfer looks possible, tune batch-norm scales and biases next, not the whole net [I]. Learned layer weights are a poor guide to which layer is good, so choose depth inside the folds, per group of controls [M, I]. No published work regresses an audio-processing parameter from a pretrained encoder with under 1,000 labels [none].

## 4. Style statistics: mean and SD, not Gram, and only as an arm

No published work reads effect or amp settings from Gram or channel-variance statistics of a frozen pretrained audio CNN. None compares them with the top embedding on any regression target [none]. The indirect evidence leans mildly towards channel mean and SD from early and middle blocks, which step 1's taps already compute [I]. The variance half carries most of any second-order gain, and channel correlations add almost nothing on top of mean and SD [M, I]. The effect encoders that work all pool first-order, by their code [M]. Mean MFCCs kept far more processing information than the top embeddings of general nets ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)) [M]. Deep-feature distances track listeners weakly unless trained on listener judgements. They were validated by correlation with ratings on speech, distortions, textures and timbre, never on knob steps [M, none]. So the judge stays as in PR #394's section C and [`docs/research/perceptual-measures-2026-11.md`](perceptual-measures-2026-11.md) [I].

## 5. Taggers: nobody has used them to pick windows or read effects

No published work uses a pretrained tagger to choose windows where one instrument is exposed, for tone or effect estimation [none]. No published work uses AudioSet's Electric guitar, Distortion or Reverberation outputs as features for those tasks either [none]. Tagger-chosen windows do help separation training where the target is often masked [M]. Class outputs carried less than the embedding on music stems [M]. Instrument detectors find distorted guitar far more easily than clean, so an exposure score would favour windows that already sound driven [M, I]. In the repo, the true dominance map led the plain mix by 5 readable heads, and stem-based maps fell behind it (F58 in [`docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`](../pipeline-fix-plan.md)). Its audit found the true map not ahead when clustered by band (p 0.072), with mean knob error unchanged [R]. So a tagger score earns a place only if it tracks the true map, which step 3 tests at no cost [I]. EfficientAT's MN models output clip-level classes, so frame scores need sliding windows; a frame-level model is a separate download ([#20](https://github.com/fschmid56/EfficientAT/issues/20)) [M].

## Practical facts: rebuild the audio at 32 kHz

The weights fix the front end, and the project's stored features do not match it. The comparison read everything from stored mels ("nothing here needs audio", [`docs/model-comparison-plan.md`](../model-comparison-plan.md)), on backed units, not stems ([`docs/model-comparison-results.md`](../model-comparison-results.md), Limits) [R]. Mixes and stems are stored only as uint8 96-band mels, except F52's 770. The clean targets and other guitars are stored as audio, so mixes rebuild on the CPU at zero renders ([methods report](proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md)) [R]. So no bakeoff input can feed the weights, and every arm needs its audio rebuilt [I]. Computing the taps on the fly avoids storing audio. About 16,800 mixes at 32 kHz would take roughly 10–20 GB, against [ML plan §5](ml-plan-2026-10.md)'s rule to keep 30 GB free [I].

## Conclusion

EfficientAT's comparisons teach less about networks than about reading them [I]. The big published gains came from data and teachers we cannot have [M, I]. What carries is a readout that stays close to the spectrum, and every independent result says that is where a mel summary is already strong [M, I]. Either likely outcome is useful [I]. If T0 ties and T1 fails, a pretrained AudioSet CNN is not the lever for tone and cab at this label count. The effort then belongs in exposing the guitar and in the fresh bands PR #394 counts (27–45 for a 0.05 gain). Only those bands can separate models the score now cannot [R, I]. If T1 passes on cab, that is a development lead, not a finding. BKS and GOAT "have been used to choose many times", and T0's primary was chosen from a GOAT contrast [R]. It would need confirming on a set nobody has chosen on: N6's capture set (read once, by F59), or L2, the fresh real-song test, which waits for the user's yes [R, I]. Only then would partial tuning and a score read earn their cost [I].
