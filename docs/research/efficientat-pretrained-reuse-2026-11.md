# Screen EfficientAT frozen, from its middle blocks

*Research, 2026-11-06, for the ML models and scoring review session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

*Tags: **[M]** measured and published; **[R]** measured in this repo; **[I]** inferred; **[none]** no evidence found. Every test design and pass bar below is an untested proposal by the researchers, not a published protocol. This report builds on PR #394's report, [`docs/research/model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md`](model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md), on the latest results, [`docs/model-comparison-results.md`](../model-comparison-results.md), and on [`docs/research/proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md`](proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md) and [`docs/research/perceptual-measures-2026-11.md`](perceptual-measures-2026-11.md). It does not repeat them. The researchers' working notes are not in the repo.*

Test EfficientAT once, frozen, read from its middle blocks beside the mel, and let the plugin-to-real transfer read decide. The two gains that made it strong on AudioSet are distillation from a 9-model PaSST ensemble (+5.3 to +5.7 mAP) and an ImageNet start (+1.2 to +1.6). Both need AudioSet's 2M clips, and we get them only by using the weights [M, I]. What carries to 520 labels is how the weights are read. Pooled middle blocks plus a time-averaged log-mel beat the network's own final embedding by 10 points on HEAR. On music tasks the log-mel alone beat every network layer: 88.3 against 74.7 at best [M]. Outside the authors' own work, a frozen EfficientAT beat a mel summary mainly where time structure matters (synth LFO rates). It tied on static spectral colour, and it lost by 17 to 27 points to models trained on plentiful task data [M]. Tone and cab are mostly static colour, so the comparison's mel ridge is the right control, and a tie is the likeliest result [I]. No published work reads amp, drive or effect settings with EfficientAT, with style statistics of a pretrained CNN, or with tagger classes [none]. The test is cheap: zero renders, minutes of network time and one checkpoint of about 20 MB [I].

## Try these first, in order

Each row is decided before the next starts. "Clears" means the paired 90% interval's lower end is above 0, read by `learn/reads_v3.py` on held GOAT, with the player-clustered check beside it, as in [PR #394](model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md). The comparison's paired cab intervals imply an SE of about 0.035–0.04, so a cab gain of about +0.06 clears; drive and tone need about +0.07 to +0.09 [R, I].

Cab is the primary for both T0 and T1, where PR #394 put drive first in T0. Cab is the one group where a learned body has already beaten the mel at this n: `W-ridge − M-ridge` +0.136 [+0.080, +0.191] [R]. One primary across both tests also keeps the candidate count low [I]. A tie in T0 does not close the route. The case for a frozen encoder rests on transfer, which only T1 tests [I].

| `forward()`'s 960-d embedding or the 527 logits as the reader | On HEAR music they score 67.6 and 55.9, against 88.3 for the time-averaged mel ([embedding paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.01879v2)) [M] |
| Wider MN (mn20–mn40) or DyMN first | Frozen quality barely moves above mn10: HEAR 86.2 rises to about 87, and in Combes mn04 to mn40 span 0.011, less than one SD [M]. dymn10 adds 0.6 mAP for 2.2× the weights, and no frozen MN-against-DyMN test exists [M, none]. Each is another download |
| End-to-end fine-tuning before T1 | Nothing in this family is tested at 520 labels [none]. | Core ML, MLX or TorchScript exports for the product now | A TorchScript mobile export of mn10 gave wrong labels, and the issue closed with no fix ([#26](https://github.com/fschmid56/EfficientAT/issues/26)) [M]. The third-party ports are unverified [none] |

## 1. EfficientAT's own papers: distillation and the readout matter, width barely does

The MN paper and the DyMN paper vary the training recipe at full AudioSet scale. Distillation dominates. Channel attention and an ImageNet start come next, at 1.2 to 2.3 points, and a coarser hop or fewer mels cost up to 2.4. Every other training choice moved mAP by about a point or less ([MN paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.04772v3); [DyMN](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.15648v1)) [M]. Neither paper ablates strides, input length, AudioSet subset size or balanced sampling, and neither compares frozen with fine-tuned use of the same model on the same task [none]. The lesson that carries is the authors' embedding paper. Its result needs only the weights: pooled blocks B5, B11, B13 and B15 (472-d at mn10), plus the time-averaged 128-band log-mel, beat every single layer. Removing any one of those blocks lowered the score ([embedding paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.01879v2)) [M]. Width gains above mn10 carried downstream only weakly: mn10 to mn30 added 1.1 mAP but only 0.4–1.4 points on the fine-tuned tasks [I, from the DyMN paper's Table I]. Channel and frequency SE, and coordinate attention, are the only architecture ideas we could graft onto our own CNN. They are hypotheses here, since every gain was measured with distillation on 2M clips [I].

## 2. Independent uses: wins on time structure, ties on colour

Independent uses are few and mostly in scene classification. EfficientAT has no published use on audio effects, amp settings, guitar timbre or perceived quality [none]. The closest is Combes et al. 2025, read from its figure. There the family's lead over a time-averaged mel comes almost entirely from LFO-rate controls, while filter cutoff, resonance and reverb wet tie at 0.997–1.000 ([figure](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pcmbs/synth-proxy_audio-model-selection/main/assets/audio_model_eval_results.png); [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07635)) [M]. Against PaSST and CLAP it ranked a little ahead on synth sweeps [M]. Against PANNs it led in the authors' own HEAR run, where the recipes differ, and in one independent tagging task, against the small CNN6 [M]. No independent study puts it beside BEATs under the same probe [none]. Every independent case with plentiful task data shows frozen EfficientAT losing to a net trained on the task [M]. That is our plugin-render situation, which is why only transfer (T1) can make the case [I].

## 3. Few-label reuse: middle layers, frequency kept, frozen first

The published practice points one way on every point that matters here. Read a middle layer, or a middle and a last one fused, never the last alone. On CNN14 the tasks most like ours (NSynth timbre, Surge synth sounds) gained 7.8 to 22 points from adding a middle layer. The last layer won only on tasks like the pretraining labels ([Niizumi 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.08138)) [M]. Keep the frequency axis and pool over time with mean and std [M, I]. Start frozen: 520 labels sit at the measured crossover between a frozen probe and fine-tuning [M, I]. If frozen fails but transfer looks possible, tune batch-norm scales and biases next, not the whole net [I]. Learned layer weights are a poor guide to which layer is good, so choose depth inside the folds, per group of controls [M, I]. No published work regresses an audio-processing parameter from a pretrained encoder with under 1,000 labels [none].

## 4. Style statistics: mean and SD, not Gram, and only as an arm

No published work reads effect or amp settings from Gram or channel-variance statistics of a frozen pretrained audio CNN. None compares them with the top embedding on any regression target [none]. The indirect evidence leans mildly towards channel mean and SD from early and middle blocks, which step 1's taps already compute [I]. The variance half carries most of any second-order gain, and channel correlations add almost nothing on top of mean and SD [M, I]. Effect encoders that work all pool first-order. Mean MFCCs kept far more processing information than the top embeddings of general nets ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)) [M]. Deep-feature distances track listeners weakly unless trained on listener judgements. They were validated by correlation with ratings on speech, distortions, textures and timbre, never on knob steps [M, none]. So the judge stays as in PR #394's section C and [`docs/research/perceptual-measures-2026-11.md`](perceptual-measures-2026-11.md) [I].

## 5. Taggers: nobody has used them to pick windows or read effects

No published work uses a pretrained tagger to choose windows where one instrument is exposed, for tone or effect estimation [none]. No published work uses AudioSet's Electric guitar, Distortion or Reverberation outputs as features for those tasks either [none]. Tagger-chosen windows do help separation training where the target is often masked [M]. Class outputs carried less than the embedding on music stems [M]. Instrument detectors find distorted guitar far more easily than clean, so an exposure score would favour windows that already sound driven [M, I]. In the repo, only the true dominance map helped, and stem-based maps hurt (F58 in [`docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`](../pipeline-fix-plan.md)) [R]. So a tagger score earns a place only if it tracks the true map, which step 3 tests at no cost [I]. EfficientAT's MN models output clip-level classes, so frame scores need sliding windows; a frame-level model is a separate download ([#20](https://github.com/fschmid56/EfficientAT/issues/20)) [M].

| Question | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Tagger-chosen windows elsewhere | SED-mined 2 s anchors against random windows: +0.44 dB on MUSDB18, +0.62 dB on Slakh, −0.33 dB on AudioSet ([Kong 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07447)) [M]. Vocal segments: about +15% relative in artist ID ([Berenzweig](https://www.aes.org/e-lib/browse.cfm?elib=11147)) [M, abstract] |
| Detection on mixes | Mean AUC 0.78 at 1 s to 0.81 per track; distorted guitar about 0.85–0.89, clean about 0.67–0.70 ([Gururani](https://ismir2018.ismir.net/doc/pdfs/275_Paper.pdf)) [M, figure] |

## Practical facts: rebuild the audio at 32 kHz

The weights fix the front end, and the project's stored features do not match it. The comparison read everything from stored mels ("nothing here needs audio", [`docs/model-comparison-plan.md`](../model-comparison-plan.md)), on backed units, not stems ([`docs/model-comparison-results.md`](../model-comparison-results.md), Limits) [R]. Mixes and stems are stored only as uint8 96-band mels, except F52's 770. The clean targets and other guitars are stored as audio, so mixes rebuild on the CPU at zero renders ([methods report](proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md)) [R]. So no bakeoff input can feed the weights, and every arm needs its audio rebuilt [I].

## What the approval covers

The user approved testing EfficientAT, and the comparison plan lists the mn10 weights among the downloads it waited on ([`docs/model-comparison-plan.md`](../model-comparison-plan.md)) [R]. Read narrowly, the approval covers fetching the MIT code and the one mn10_as checkpoint, and running steps 0–3 frozen at zero renders on CPU or MPS [I]. Anything beyond that stays the ML session's and the user's call [I]:

- other checkpoints (mn04, mn20, any DyMN, the frame-level model);
- any new package install, torchaudio included, if it is not already on the Mac;
- fine-tuning, which the plan lists apart from the download;
- third-party Core ML or MLX ports;
- any shipping of EfficientAT in the product.

## Conclusion

EfficientAT's comparisons teach less about networks than about reading them [I]. The big published gains came from data and teachers we cannot have [M, I]. What carries is a readout that stays close to the spectrum, and every independent result says that is where a mel summary is already strong [M, I]. Either likely outcome is useful [I]. If T0 ties and T1 fails, a pretrained AudioSet CNN is not the lever for tone and cab at this label count. The effort then belongs in exposing the guitar (F58's true map) and in the fresh bands PR #394 counts (27–45 for a 0.05 gain), which alone can separate models the score now cannot [R, I]. If T1 passes on cab, it would be this project's first feature space that reads real cabs from plugin rows alone: W reads them backwards and the plugin-trained mel ridge at about zero [R]. Only then would partial tuning and a score read earn their cost [I].
