# Keep htdemucs_6s until a clean challenger appears

Evidence tags used throughout: **[V]** verified (a researcher opened the source and read the claim, or it is in this repo's committed docs); **[I]** inferred (reasoning from verified facts); **[U]** unverified (search snippet, third-party claim, or memory). "MVSEP" means the community guitar leaderboard at mvsep.com (30 one-minute tracks; "guitar" = acoustic + electric). "Project SNR" means the median SNR of the guitar stem against the part's own amp track on the 10-s crop, from the 2026-10-08 separator-upgrade test ([plan](../separator-upgrade-plan.md), [results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)).

**Bottom line up front.** No open, licence-clean separator available today gives a clearly better electric-guitar stem than htdemucs_6s for this project, so the separator should stay as it is for ML plan step 3. The best published guitar separator, BS-RoFormer SW, leads htdemucs_6s by **+3.8 dB** on MVSEP, but its weights have no known licence, and the one model derived from it (X-LANCE) inherits that problem. The only challenger the project can arguably use, Mega-53, was already tested on 61 development parts and gained only **+0.5 to +0.6 dB in paired median SNR**, almost all of it on the 7 single-guitar parts (+4 dB there, +0.3–0.4 dB on the 54 multi-guitar parts), while removing much of the guitar's top octave. The gap between the published +3.8 dB and the project's +0.5 dB is mostly not a disagreement: on single-guitar parts the project sees the leaderboard's margin, and the leaderboards never ask a model to separate one guitar from other guitars, which is what the project's multi-guitar parts require. The project's SNR metric also compresses gains at low values, so part of the shortfall is likely a units effect. The lever that matters is choosing among guitars, not swapping networks, and no open model splits electric from acoustic or lead from rhythm well enough to do it. A cheap re-test is still worth declaring, mainly to re-score stored stems under a fairer, paired, band-clustered bar with spectral guards, and to be ready if a cleanly licensed model (for example a released SCNet6) appears.

## Leaderboards promise +3.8 dB; the project's own parts delivered +0.5 dB paired

### Three families of guitar numbers, none matching the project's target

Only three kinds of guitar-specific evidence exist, and none of them uses the project's reference signal [V]. The **MoisesDB academic line** scores separators on MoisesDB's un-mastered multitracks. The MoisesDB paper itself gives htdemucs_6s a guitar SDR of **3.07 ± 1.81 dB mean, 3.16 dB median** on the 88 songs that contain all six stems, below the oracle ideal ratio mask at 5.28 dB, and warns that "other plucked" instruments bleed into guitar ([MoisesDB, arXiv 2307.15913](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.15913)) [V]. On the later 48-song test split, a December 2025 paper reports median SNR of **2.4 dB for htdemucs_6s, 6.3 dB for SCNet6 and 7.0 dB for SCNet6(L)**, trained only on the 144 MoisesDB training songs ([QSCNet, arXiv 2512.15532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.15532)) [V]. That +4.6 dB is the only like-for-like, paper-grade comparison of a non-Demucs guitar separator [I], but the researchers could not confirm that its weights are public [V], and anything trained only on MoisesDB carries its CC BY-NC-SA licence question ([moises-db](https://github.com/moises-ai/moises-db)) [V]. A 2024 ensemble study measured htdemucs_6s at 4.38 dB SNR / 2.59 dB SDR on a 71-song MoisesDB subset ([arXiv 2410.20773](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.20773)) [V].

The **MVSEP guitar leaderboard** is community-run and not peer-reviewed: 30 one-minute tracks at 44.1 kHz, references hidden server-side, and "guitar includes acoustic guitar as well as electric guitar" ([MVSEP custom leaderboards](https://mvsep.com/quality_checker/custom_leaderboards)) [V]. On it, **BS-RoFormer SW scores 9.01 dB** at maximum overlap and 8.97 dB at overlap 2, against **5.25 dB for htdemucs_6s** run with shifts 5 and overlap 0.75 ([MVSEP guitar leaderboard](https://mvsep.com/quality_checker/leaderboard/guitar/?sort=guitar)) [V]. MVSep's own model table reuses the same set (SW 9.05, Demucs 6-stem 5.22) ([MVSEP algorithms p2](https://mvsep.com/en/algorithms?page=2)) [V]. The anonymous 2026 entries near the top (8.98–8.99) are unpublished and none beats SW [V]. The **third family**, model pages from MVSep and ZFTurbo's training repo, adds nothing independent: ZFTurbo's pretrained table lists no guitar SDR for any checkpoint, and MUSDB18-HQ has no guitar stem, so every SCNet, BS-RoFormer, MDX23C or DTTNet number on MUSDB is for "other" only ([pretrained_models.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/docs/pretrained_models.md)) [V]. No source reports cSDR or BSS Eval v4 numbers for guitar, and the Sound Demixing Challenge 2023 appears to have scored four stems only [U].

Absolute values do not carry across sets: htdemucs_6s scores 5.2 dB on MVSEP and 2.4–3.2 dB on MoisesDB [V], and the project's 1.11 dB median sits below every published htdemucs_6s figure [V]. That is expected, because the project's reference is a single electric amp track, and every other guitar, acoustic part or organ in the stem counts as error [I].

### Why the project measured a third of the published margin

The project's 2026-10-08 test ran four eligible rows (Mega-53's electric-guitar head, X-LANCE, the two chained, and a pan rule) against htdemucs_6s on sets 1–2 (28 parts, 11 bands) and set 3 (33 parts, 11 bands), with a pre-declared bar of **+2 dB median SNR on both sets** ([plan](../separator-upgrade-plan.md)) [V]. Every candidate beat htdemucs_6s, and usable parts (SNR ≥ 1 dB) rose from 14 to 20–22 of 28 and from 12 to 20–21 of 33, but the median gain was only **+0.76 to +1.24 dB**, so nothing passed ([results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. The paired figure is smaller still: **+0.50 dB on sets 1–2 and +0.61 dB on set 3** for Mega-53 [V]. An independent reviewer re-scored all 61 parts with separate code and reproduced the medians and counts [V].

Four effects explain why this is so far below MVSEP's +3.8 dB, and the first one dominates. **First, the target differs.** On the 7 single-guitar parts the gain was about **+4 dB paired** (median 5.86 → 9.96 dB), which matches the leaderboard margin; on the 54 multi-guitar parts it was +0.3 to +0.4 dB "because a 'guitar' stem still holds the other guitars" ([results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. Leaderboards reward a model for keeping all guitars, while the project penalises it for exactly that [I]. **Second, the metric compresses.** If the project's least-squares gain scales the estimate, its SNR equals 10·log10(1 + SI-SDR in linear units); then 1.11 → 2.32 dB SNR is roughly **−5.4 → −1.5 dB SI-SDR (+3.9 dB)**, and set 3's 0.63 → 1.48 dB is **−8.1 → −3.9 dB (+4.2 dB)** [I]. In the units the literature uses, the difference of medians would then be close to MVSEP's margin, and the old "+2 dB from 1.11" bar would have meant about +5.6 dB SI-SDR, which was nearly unreachable on multi-guitar parts [I]. Whether the gain scales the estimate or the reference is decided by the scoring script on the Mac, which is not in git, so this point is unverified until someone reads it [V gap]. **Third, the paired statistic is the honest one:** a difference of medians (+1.21 dB) overstated the paired median gain (+0.50 dB) by more than double [V]. **Fourth, conditions differ:** the project feeds mono 30-s instrumental contexts with vocals already removed, from sessions that carry no pan, while MVSEP feeds stereo one-minute mixes [V]. 

The upshot for decision-making is that both numbers are right about different questions. A better network roughly quadruples the energy ratio on a lone guitar, but it cannot tell one of the song's guitars from another, and 54 of the 61 development parts sit in multi-guitar mixes [V].

### The edge-loss problem that SNR hides

Mega-53's median band error against the amp track is **−9 to −14 dB below 63 Hz, −15 dB at 8 kHz and −36 dB at 12.7 kHz**, against −2 to −3 dB, −6 dB and −18 dB for htdemucs_6s, which is why its active LSD is worse (8.56 vs 5.47 on sets 1–2) "as BS-RoFormer-SW's was" ([results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. X-LANCE kept the shape better (LSD 4.40 vs 7.40 on single-guitar parts) [V]. SNR and SDR are nearly blind to where in frequency the error falls; BSS Eval even "completely forgives" any 512-tap filter, low-pass included ([Le Roux et al., arXiv 1811.02508](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.02508)) [V]. For this project the missing top octave is not cosmetic: step 3's arm A compares the stem with full-band renders, so a stem missing 8–13 kHz energy would push the search toward darker settings [I]. The observation that SW sends guitar content above about 7 kHz into its drums stem was not found in any repo doc or note, so it remains unverified [U]; the proposed test below measures it directly.

## Only htdemucs_6s and Mega-53 survive the open-licence rule

### Licences: one clean model, one tolerable, the rest flagged

Demucs code is MIT in both Meta's archived repository and the maintained fork, and the 4.1.0 release (11 July 2026) moved weights to Hugging Face as safetensors; those model repos carry **no licence field**, so the weights rest on the project-level MIT statement ([adefossez/demucs README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adefossez/demucs/main/README.md), [HF HTDemucs-6s](https://huggingface.co/api/models/adefossez/HTDemucs-6s)) [V]. The data behind the guitar and piano stems is undocumented, and htdemucs was "trained on MusDB + 800 songs", with MUSDB18 itself restricted to non-commercial use ([Zenodo 1117372](https://zenodo.org/records/1117372)) [V]. The project already treats this as acceptable, and it is the lowest-risk guitar model available [I].

Every community guitar checkpoint is flagged. **BS-RoFormer SW** is orphaned: the original uploader's account was deleted, the current host declares `license: unknown`, and one re-host states "the original trainer is unknown and no license was stated" ([enerjazzer](https://huggingface.co/enerjazzer/BS-ROFO-SW-Fixed), [lumabeat](https://huggingface.co/lumabeat/bs-roformer-sw)) [V]. Two mirrors tag it MIT without citing a source, and an MIT-licensed inference package warns explicitly not to treat its code licence "as a grant for commercial use of this checkpoint" ([bs-roformer-infer README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openmirlab/bs-roformer-infer/main/README.md)) [V]. becruily's guitar Mel-RoFormer has no card or licence, and his newer models are CC BY-NC 4.0 ([HF API](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=becruily)) [V]. chenCFD's guitar model was trained on MoisesDB ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/chenCFD/Guitar_Source_Separation/main/README.md)) [V]. Banquet's code is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([arXiv 2406.18747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18747)) [V]. **Mega-53** sits in between: its release in ZFTurbo's MIT repository carries no weights licence and no training-data statement ([release v1.0.21](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/releases/tag/v1.0.21)) [V], which the project accepted, but strictly it is "unstated", the same footing as htdemucs_6s with less provenance [I].

### Mac support: everything runs, but complex STFT still goes to CPU

All candidates run on Apple silicon through PyTorch, with the same known gap: complex-number STFT and iSTFT. Demucs moves them to CPU by design ("mps doesnt support complex numbers") and uses MPS for the rest ([htdemucs.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adefossez/demucs/main/demucs/htdemucs.py), [docs/mac.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adefossez/demucs/main/docs/mac.md)) [V]. ZFTurbo's `inference.py` selects MPS automatically and retries STFT on CPU on error; FlashAttention and `torch.compile` are CUDA-only ([bs_roformer.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/models/bs_roformer/bs_roformer.py), [inference.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/inference.py)) [V]. python-audio-separator (MIT, 0.47.0 on 27 Aug 2026) probes MPS and falls back per op, requires PyTorch ≥ 2.13 on Apple silicon, and offers fp16 for RoFormers ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator/main/README.md)) [V]. The research notes disagree on whether its bundled model list includes SW: one note found "BS Roformer SW by jarredou" in `models.json`, another found no guitar entry by name ([models.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/nomadkaraoke/python-audio-separator/main/audio_separator/models.json)) [V conflict]; since SW's licence excludes it anyway, this does not change the answer. One inference package removed MPS support outright in September 2026 [V].

Runtime evidence on Macs is thin and mostly third-party. The firmest numbers are the project's own, on CPU with 4 threads on a shared machine (so wall times are inflated): per 30-s context, **htdemucs_6s 15 s wall (39 CPU s), Mega-53 two heads 102 s (315 CPU s), X-LANCE 87 s (193 CPU s)** ([results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. Per minute of audio, that is roughly 30 s, 204 s and 174 s of wall time [I]. A third-party site reports htdemucs_6s at 1.6 s per 7-s clip on an M4 with MPS, about 14 s per minute ([stemsplitter.github.io](https://stemsplitter.github.io/research/model-comparison/)) [U]. SW reportedly takes 2.75 s for a 12-s excerpt in a GGUF/Metal C++ port (about 14 s/min) ([FractalSurfer card](https://huggingface.co/FractalSurfer/BS-RoFormer-SW-6stem)) [V claim], and about 120 s/min under PyTorch MPS on an M2 [U]. SW's fp32 checkpoint is 699 MB (about 175 M parameters) against htdemucs_6s's 55 MB [V]. The practical conclusion is that a RoFormer-class guitar model costs roughly **5–8× htdemucs_6s's CPU time** on the project's machine (193–315 CPU s against 39 per context) [I], which is affordable for a one-off test but matters for a product pipeline.

### Maintenance: battle-tested is not the same as maintained

Meta archived its Demucs repository on 1 January 2025; the author's fork is "the officially maintained Demucs", but he is "not actively working on Demucs anymore", and 4.1.0 is the first release in about three years ([facebookresearch/demucs README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/demucs/main/README.md), [PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/demucs/json)) [V]. Low activity is acceptable for a frozen model with a pinned, bit-exact pipeline (`learn/separate.py` "reproduces a stored stem bit-exactly") [V]. ZFTurbo's training repo and audio-separator are active, with releases in August 2026 [V]. SW is orphaned, becruily's guitar model has not been updated since May 2025, and the UVR GUI looks stale, with about 1.5k open issues and no reachable LICENSE file ([UVR repo](https://github.com/Anjok07/ultimatevocalremovergui)) [V].

## No open model splits electric from acoustic or lead from rhythm reliably

The only public dataset with a guitar sub-taxonomy is MoisesDB (acoustic, clean electric, distorted electric) [V]. On it, Banquet reaches median SNR of **0.7 dB for clean electric, 2.4 dB for distorted electric and 1.8 dB for acoustic** guitar, and its authors note the model is "somewhat insensitive to fine differences in timbre" ([Banquet, arXiv 2406.18747](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18747)) [V]. QSCNet reports clean electric 3.6, distorted 4.0 and acoustic 1.3 dB median SNR, beating Banquet on both electric classes, but it was trained on coarse "guitar" and has no confirmed open weights ([arXiv 2512.15532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.15532)) [V]. In both papers **distorted electric is the most separable guitar**; the hardest is clean electric in Banquet and acoustic in QSCNet [V]. From Banquet's numbers, clean and edge-of-breakup parts may produce worse stems than high-gain ones [I].

Mega-53 has dedicated `electric-guitar` and `acoustic-guitar` heads. MVSep reports 8.15 dB for the electric head on its internal validation, and notes that "individual models for each instrument generally produce better results than this multimodel" ([MVSEP algorithms p2](https://mvsep.com/en/algorithms?page=2)) [V]. On the project's parts, the electric head was not consistently better than Mega-53's plain guitar head (2.32 vs 2.12 dB on sets 1–2, 1.48 vs 1.54 dB on set 3) [V], so an electric-only head does not solve the multi-guitar problem, because most of the other guitars are electric too [I]. The MVSEP electric-only scores (3.81 for Mega-53's head, 1.91 for an MDX electric model) are low because the reference contains acoustic guitar, not because those models are worse [I].

Lead versus rhythm separation has no credible open method. The only academic attempt found is a short 2025 Japanese conference paper that splits two electric guitars by timbre (clean vs distorted) with a three-layer BLSTM on a 4-stem separator's "other" output, trained on about 2.9 h of material, with no released code and absolute scores only in a figure; mixtures with other instruments scored about 1.3 dB lower than guitar-only ones ([FIT2025 E-035](https://www.ieice.org/publications/conference-FIT-DVDs/FIT2025/data/html/program/pdf/E-035.pdf)) [V]. Moises sells acoustic/electric and lead/rhythm models but publishes no metrics or code ([Moises blog](https://moises.ai/blog/moises-news/new-guitar-separation-models/)) [V]. Meta's SAM Audio accepts text prompts like "guitar" but reports no guitar SDR, and a generative separator that re-synthesises timbre is risky for tone matching ([arXiv 2512.18099](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18099v1)) [V for the paper; I for the risk].

The project's own simulated-pan experiment points to the more promising lever. With each guitar given a random pan, an oracle choice of left, centre or right gained **+2.6 dB paired on sets 1–2** and lifted multi-guitar parts from 1.45 to 2.50 dB median, far more than any network swap; but the automatic "most energy" rule made multi-guitar parts worse, because the loudest side is usually another guitar ([results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. The development sessions carry no pan (96% mono guitar tracks), so this could only be simulated [V]; commercial stereo mixes do carry pan, so a user picking the side by ear from three short clips is plausible in the product [I], though no published evaluation of pan-based guitar extraction exists [V gap].

## Tricks add tenths of a decibel; choosing among guitars adds decibels

### Ensembles, shifts, overlap and context

Measured gains from inference tricks are small and consistent. **Ensembling** two similar-quality guitar models added +0.16 to +0.35 dB over the best member on MVSEP's set (BS-RoFormer 7.16 + Mel-RoFormer 7.02 → 7.51), but the single SW model at 9.05 beat every ensemble ([MVSEP algorithm 17](https://mvsep.com/algorithms/17?lang=en)) [V]. ZFTurbo's guidance is to average waveforms (`avg_wave`) and only ensemble models of similar quality, because a weak member lowers the result ([ensemble.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/docs/ensemble.md)) [V]. On the project's parts, chaining X-LANCE after Mega-53 did nothing, because a mixture-to-guitar model is close to the identity on a guitar stem [V]. **Shifts** gave about +0.3 dB on the original Demucs v2 ([arXiv 1911.13254](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.13254)) [V] but essentially zero for htdemucs_ft (other 5.84 vs 5.84 dB at 10 shifts vs 1, at ten times the cost); raising **overlap** from 0.25 to 0.95 gained only +0.10 dB on "other" ([arXiv 2305.07489](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07489v2)) [V]. The project's shifts 0 and overlap 0.25 therefore cost it at most about 0.1–0.2 dB [I]. **Longer context** helps at training time (3.4 s → 7.8 s segments raised HT Demucs from 8.17 to 8.70 dB overall; extending to 15 s during fine-tuning added nothing) ([arXiv 2211.08553](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.08553)) [V], but at inference HT models are capped at 7.8-s segments ([demucs README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adefossez/demucs/main/README.md)) [V], so the 30-s context mainly protects the crop from edge effects [I]. Its value has never been measured on project data; `learn/separate.py --crop-only` makes that a free anchor [V].

### Vocals-first cascade: already built into the development test

A vocals-first cascade raised becruily's guitar model from 5.29 to 5.63 dB on MVSEP (+0.33 dB) [V], and the MDX23 cascade's large gains appeared only where it fixed a domain mismatch ([arXiv 2305.07489](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.07489v2)) [V]. The project's development contexts are rebuilt from the session **without vocal tracks**, which is an oracle cascade [V]. That makes development stems optimistic relative to a product that receives a full mix with vocals, which the songs check already flagged ("the stems are optimistic") ([songs-check results](../songs-check-results.md)) [V]. Any new test should report a with-vocals condition rebuilt from the same sessions, so the product-facing cost is visible [I].

### Fine-tuning on the project's multitracks: feasible on paper, expensive on a Mac

Fine-tuning is the only route that targets the project's real problem, one guitar against the others, because the project's sessions supply exactly that pairing [I]. The tooling exists: ZFTurbo's repo fine-tunes from a checkpoint (`--start_check_point`), supports LoRA, and accepts per-stem and real-mixture datasets; Demucs supports `continue_from` with a fine-tune variant ([MSST README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/README.md), [demucs training.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/facebookresearch/demucs/main/docs/training.md)) [V]. Published gains are modest: per-source fine-tuning added +0.25 dB overall to HT Demucs (and slightly hurt "other", 6.42 → 6.32), and going from 150 to 800 training songs added +0.31 dB ([arXiv 2211.08553](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.08553)) [V]. Cross-song **remixing** is the augmentation that matters most: HT Demucs lost 0.7 dB without it [V], and ZFTurbo's repo already offers mixture-side MP3 compression, EQ and distortion augmentation ([augmentations.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/docs/augmentations.md)) [V].

The Mac is the bottleneck. ZFTurbo's training path picks CUDA or falls back to CPU, with no MPS branch and CUDA-only mixed precision ([model_utils.py](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/utils/model_utils.py)) [V]; Demucs's training docs point to a CUDA environment [V]. A short fine-tune on an M-series Mac would mean CPU training or patching for MPS with CPU fallbacks for complex ops, and would take hours to days per run [I]; a rented CUDA GPU is the realistic route, with inference back on the Mac [I]. The data is the 61 development parts used by the separator test (65 classed clean and crunch parts in the ML plan), roughly 1–3 h of isolated guitar, which is a fine-tuning set, not a training set [I]. The closest precedent, the two-guitar BLSTM, used about 2.9 h [V]. Three constraints make this a later step rather than a step-3 input: the starting checkpoint must be licence-clean (htdemucs_6s, or Mega-53 with its unstated status) [I]; evaluation must be leave-band-out across development parts, never the held-out sets [V rule, ML plan]; and Banquet's finding that separators are insensitive to fine timbre differences suggests that separating two distorted electric guitars in one song will stay hard even with in-domain data [I].

## A two-hour re-test with a paired, band-clustered bar

The previous test was well run, so the next one should change only what it got wrong: a difference-of-medians statistic, a bar in a compressing metric, no spectral guard, and no downstream check. The design below is a proposal [I] that follows the project's rules: a committed `docs/<topic>-plan.md` before any stem is computed, a ledger entry in `docs/dev-test-ledger.json` before results exist, band-clustered statistics, open weights, and no held-out parts ([ML plan](../research/ml-plan-2026-10.md)) [V rules]; checking the licence at the original upstream rather than a re-host is a proposed tightening [I]. It must be declared as a **second development use** of the same 61 parts already used on 2026-10-08 [I].

**Step 0, before declaring the bar (minutes, no separation).** Read the Mac scoring script to fix whether `snr_db` applies the gain to the estimate (then SNR = 1 + SI-SDR, linear) or to the reference (then it is SI-SDR) [I]. Then compute the band-level standard deviation of the paired difference from the stored `sep2/manifest.json` and log it as a power computation, not a test [I]. With about 22 bands, a +1 dB paired effect is detectable at 80% power if that band-level SD is near 1–1.5 dB; a +0.5 dB effect needs 25–56 bands and is not detectable here [I].

**Material and candidates.** Sets 1–2 and set 3 (61 parts, about 22 bands), stratified single (7) against multi (54), by gain class, and with and without MUSDB18-train songs [V parts; I strata]. The baseline is the stored htdemucs_6s stems. The eligible candidate is Mega-53's `electric-guitar` stem, re-scored from stored stems at zero separation cost, plus any newly found model with a verified open licence [I]. One new, cheap candidate is worth adding: a **fixed crossover blend**, Mega-53 below about 6 kHz and htdemucs_6s above, built from the two stored stems; it is a fixed filter rather than a per-song correction, so the product could ship it [I, untested]. Anchors are reported but never eligible: the mixture crop (negative anchor), htdemucs_6s with `--crop-only` (what context is worth), and SW and X-LANCE as descriptive rows [I]. A with-vocals rebuild of the contexts is reported for htdemucs_6s and the leading candidate [I].

**Pre-declared switching bar.** A candidate replaces htdemucs_6s only if every one of these holds [I; thresholds are proposals to check against the stored htdemucs_6s vs Mega-53 spread before declaration]:

| Gate | Condition |
|---|---|
| 1. Signal gain | Band-weighted mean paired SI-SDR gain on the 10-s crop **≥ +1.0 dB** over all 61 parts, 90% band-cluster interval excluding 0; and the multi-guitar stratum's interval lower bound **≥ −0.25 dB** (no harm on the majority case). Report the old SNR beside it |
| 2. High-frequency guard | Median octave band error at 8 and 16 kHz **no more than 3 dB worse** than htdemucs_6s's; 4–12 kHz band SI-SDR paired lower bound **≥ −1 dB**; guitar energy above 6 kHz leaking into other stems (for multi-stem models) **not more than 0.1 higher** as a fraction |
| 3. Low-end guard | Median band error at 63 Hz no more than 3 dB worse than htdemucs_6s's |
| 4. Downstream non-inferiority | Judge (`flat`) ranking of step 4's menu renders against the stem vs against the amp track: band-weighted Kendall τ not lower than htdemucs_6s's by more than **0.02**, and pick regret not worse by more than **0.01** log ratio (mirrors arm A's 0.01 margin). Zero new renders if step 4 keeps its renders as audio |
| 5. Licence | Code and weights MIT, Apache, BSD, CC0 or CC BY at the original upstream, not a re-host |

With k eligible candidates, intervals widen to 1 − 0.10/k, or one primary candidate is declared and the rest are descriptive [I]. Ties go to the best downstream regret, then the lowest CPU time [I]. Stability checks (leave-one-band-out, each set separately, single vs multi) are reported, not required [I]. A rebuilt-DI coherence check is reported but does not gate, because the project has already shown coherence can rise while picks get worse ([residual-di results](../residual-di-results.md)) [V].

**Expected outcome and cost.** Mega-53 as measured would fail gate 2: its 8-kHz error is 9 dB worse than htdemucs_6s's and its 12.7-kHz error is 18 dB worse [I from V numbers]. So the realistic outcomes are either "no switch" again, now with a fairer reason on record, or a pass for the crossover blend if it keeps most of Mega-53's single-guitar gain while restoring the top octave [I]. Scoring stored stems takes minutes; a genuinely new RoFormer-class candidate adds about 1.5–1.7 h of CPU separation for 61 contexts on the project's Mac, about 2–3 h end to end [I from V per-context timings].

## Conclusion

The question "which separator is best" has a clear published answer, BS-RoFormer SW, and a clear project answer, htdemucs_6s, and the two do not conflict. Published benchmarks measure how well a model finds all guitars; the project needs one guitar out of several, and on that task every current network, open or not, is capped by the other guitars in the stem. The project's own single-guitar parts already reproduce the leaderboard's roughly +4 dB margin, which means the separators are not the weak link on easy parts and cannot be the fix on hard ones. Two loose ends deserve attention before anyone concludes the matter: the project's SNR may be compressing gains that SI-SDR would show as near-leaderboard size, and the high-frequency loss of RoFormer-class stems is a real downstream risk that SNR alone would never catch.

For step 3, both routes should keep starting from htdemucs_6s with its current settings, and report single-guitar and multi-guitar parts separately so that the separator's ceiling is visible in the results. The higher-value research bet is not another separator but a way to pick the right guitar: user-chosen pan regions on real stereo mixes, or a separator fine-tuned on the project's own "one guitar versus the others" pairs, run on rented CUDA hardware. Watch for an openly licensed release of SCNet6 or a successor trained on licence-clean data; that is the one development that could change this verdict, and the pre-declared test above is ready for it.
