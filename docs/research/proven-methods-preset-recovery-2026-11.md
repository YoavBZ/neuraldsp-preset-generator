# Adapt lightly, recast drive, check by ear

*Research, 2026-11-04, for the ML models and scoring review session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

No published method recovers amp or effect settings from a full commercial mix, and none has been tested on a guitar amp plugin with real recordings, so nothing below is proven on this problem. The closest real-recording evidence is one vocal study (65 MedleyDB vocals, 365 fitted presets). There, 16 listeners rated plain parameter regression lowest. Nearest-neighbour retrieval in MFCC space tied the best search, search with a preset prior left 25% less spectral excess than regression, and ST-ITO as published did worse than regression ([Yu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11315v1)). The big wins elsewhere (flow heads about 2× better than regression, proxy or PNP loss terms 7–38%, reward training 17–38% out of domain) all came on synthesizers with tens of thousands to ten million renders, no input signal and synthetic targets, a regime the project does not have. Drive is the one topic where several studies agree on a mechanism: distortion shows only above the amp's breakup point, every published estimator fixes the input level, and clean-versus-breakup detection is near-solved on isolated guitar. No study tests few-label adaptation for audio regression, and no listener-validated measure has been tried on amp-knob steps. The two changes the evidence best supports are these. **First, take the real-amp correction with a light touch**: interpolate the stored plugin model W with its fine-tuned copy FT, or tune only its bias or head. This costs zero training and about 8–16 minutes of renders to test. **Second, recast drive** as a breakup class plus one effective-drive value at a fixed input-level convention. Screen it first with a gain sweep of about 130 renders. The project's own ear pages show the judge over-weights treble and drive, and among S, FT and D_fix it agrees with the ear at only +0.04 (F44–F47, F54 in `docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`). So every finalist below should end on a short blind page for the one listener, not on a judge score alone.

## Thirteen methods ranked for one Mac

The ranking weighs three things: strength of evidence, fit to the project's measured failure, and cost. That failure, recorded in `docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`, has three parts. The backing costs most of the tone reading: a linear probe reads 86 of 116 heads from the clean target, 30 from the mix and 20 from the stem (`docs/tone-info-probe.md`). Real labels move the model to a real-amp centre and no further (F41–F42). About 80% of FT's real-reamp gain is drive (F43). Tiers are **A**, proven on comparable data (real recordings with hundreds of fitted effect labels; only Yu et al. qualifies, and it is vocals); **B**, proven only on synthetic or other domains; **C**, paper or derivation only. **Every test design and pass bar below is the researchers' own proposal, untested and not a published protocol.** The evidence is thin throughout. Rows 2 and 8 rest on one vocal study, and row 6 on abstracts and snippets. No row has been tested on a guitar amp in a mix.

Power is the binding limit. BKS is one band with 8 DIs, and V-real-11 has 7 bands with a ±0.07 interval (F41). So zero-render screens should choose the candidates, and renders should go only to finalists. Costs assume 1 s per render on one process. The project's measured median is about 1.6 s (`docs/research/direct-model-constant-2026-10.md`), so scale up to 1.6× if needed. X-LANCE takes about 4.3 s a clip on MPS (F50). Only target and other-guitar audio is stored, while mixes and stems exist as uint8 96-band half log-mels. Mixes rebuild on the CPU with zero renders (F52; `docs/tone-info-probe.md`). This report builds on the earlier surveys and does not repeat them. Those cover auditory models such as HAAQI and PEAQ (`docs/research/perceptual-measures-2026-11.md`), blind reverb and delay (`docs/research/blind-reverb-delay-2026-11.md`) and amp captures (`docs/research/real-amp-chains-2026-11.md`). Star counts and push dates are from the GitHub API on 2026-11-04 ([batch 1](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=repo:csteinmetz1/st-ito+repo:adobe-research/DeepAFx-ST+repo:adobe-research/DeepAFx+repo:lylyhan/perceptual_neural_physical+repo:pcmbs/synth-proxy+repo:DDSynth-RL/DDSynthRL+repo:spotify/pedalboard+repo:sbi-dev/sbi+repo:CyberAgentAILab/cmaes+repo:CMA-ES/pycma+repo:csteinmetz1/dasp-pytorch+repo:mcomunita/nablafx&per_page=30), [batch 2](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=repo:DBraun/SynAPSE+repo:peladeaucome/DAFx_Params_Distrib+repo:SonyResearch/Fx-Encoder_PlusPlus+repo:facebookresearch/nevergrad+repo:optuna/optuna+repo:pytorch/botorch+repo:probabilists/zuko+repo:atong01/conditional-flow-matching+repo:acids-ircam/flow_synthesizer+repo:gwendal-lv/preset-gen-vae+repo:DBraun/synth-proxy+repo:ben-hayes/synth-permutations&per_page=30)).

Several methods were dropped. A neural proxy of Morgan is out: proxy gradients driving real, input-dependent DSP ended worse than the unprocessed input ([DeepAFx-ST](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.08759v1)), and 1M renders would take about 11.6 days. ST-ITO as published is out (worse than regression; about 27 min a song), and so is full SPSA or ES training (0.8–18M renders). So are unsupervised alignment and test-time adaptation. Their regression versions were shown only on image benchmarks with thousands of unlabelled targets, and their authors warn that feature alignment can hurt regression ([RSD](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/chen21u.html); [SSA](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03263v2)). Pretrained music encoders are out as the main reader: there are no attribute-regression results under 1,000 labels, the weights are CC BY-NC, and they discard level. Frequency-wise normalisation is out because it erases the EQ labels ([RFN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.12513)).

The pattern is consistent. Parameter loss is the stable base. Adding an audio-domain term on top improves sound by roughly 5–40% and usually worsens parameter error, and an audio loss alone is the worst choice. But every large win sits where the project is not: millions of renders, no input signal, synthetic targets, or a dry signal at test time. The project's failure is one of domain and identifiability, while most of these methods change the loss, the head or the optimiser. That is why rows 1–5 lead the ranking and the synth-scale methods trail.

Two caveats limit even the real-vocal evidence. Retrieval inherits its embedding's robustness to the backing, and that is exactly what failed here: the project's tone encoder failed on records, and separation cut Fx-Encoder++ retrieval from 19.9 to 7.1 R@1 ([Fx-Encoder++](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02273v1)). Prior-regularised search needs an objective that works. Through the C′ rebuilt DI the local Spearman is −0.126 (`docs/research/local-objective-2026-10.md`), so the prior can only regularise arm A, not rescue a blind song search. ST-ITO's authors say it "does not work well for … guitar tone matching" and give no reason ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)). The researchers infer four causes: a uniform prior, a peak-normalising extractor that discards the level cue for drive, an embedding not injective to style, and no amp or cab model in its chains. That explanation is inference, not a finding.

## Drive reads only past breakup, and only at a fixed level

Drive is where the project's score gains are largest (F43) and where the judge and the ear disagree most (F44–F47). On isolated guitar, level-free statistics separate tone classes well. Across 180 peak-normalised chords through five valve amps, a crest-type loudness measure and sharpness split clean, overdrive and distortion with ηp² of .81 and .76. Roughness was weaker and interacted with the chord ([Herbst 2019](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/15105454/Herbst_Distortion_and_Rock_Guitar_Harmony.pdf)). Learned regressors reach a gain MAE of 0.014–0.05, but only on 2-second notes at fixed level. Comunità's network held up only for gain above 0.5 in one cross-condition ([Comunità 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.03216)). Hinrichs found errors near 0.12 "almost exclusively" below gain 0.3, because there the distortion touches only the note attacks ([Hinrichs 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)). In Hinrichs's synthetic mixes, gain error grew only about 1.6× from the quietest backing to the loudest. At a 0 dB backing, its classifier's distortion and overdrive rows stayed near 0.97–1.0. No estimator separates input level from the drive knob. NAM records the analog level instead of estimating it ([NAM calibration](https://neural-amp-modeler.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/calibration.html)). So from a song the observable is effective drive, and asking for input gain and amp gain separately asks for something unidentifiable.

Listener evidence points the same way, though none of it varies an amp's gain. Soft clipping changed distortion ratings little, while hard clipping changed them a lot ([Tan, Moore & Zacharov 2003](https://convexoptimization.com/TOOLS/Tuan.pdf)). Excerpts at the edge of breakup exposed amp-model differences that high gain hid ([Cassidy & De Sena 2023](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2023/DAFx23_paper_40.pdf)). ESR mis-ordered two models on a high-gain amp ([Wright et al. 2020](https://acris.aalto.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/41964332/Real_time_guitar_amplifier_emulation.pdf)). The project's one listener heard +10 dB clearly on SW50R crunch and very clearly on AC20 clean, but not on PR12 clean or PR12 crunch (F45, F47). That fits a "breakup edge" hypothesis better than a clean-against-crunch rule, but it rests on one listener and few heard items.

For scoring, the one listener-validated design that keeps linear and nonlinear distortion apart is Moore and Tan's. It is a weighted sum of a linear prediction and a nonlinear one (Rnonlin), with r above 0.90 on music ([Moore et al. 2004](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=13034)). It has been read only at abstract level, it has no open code, and its weights were fitted on codecs and transducers. In sharpness-matched distorted guitar, two of three perceptual dimensions were predicted after the spectral tilt was removed ([Marui & Martens 2005](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=13202), snippet only). That is the case for row 6. Newer learned raters do not help: Audiobox Aesthetics tells its raters to penalise distortion ([Audiobox Aesthetics](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05139v1)). Roughness descriptors track listeners on instrument tones (Vassilakis log r 0.82–0.87) ([Czedik-Eysenberg & Reuter 2025](https://homepage.univie.ac.at/christoph.reuter/unterwegs/DAGA2025_Czedik-Eysenberg_Reuter_PAPER.pdf)). The best of them runs in MIRtoolbox, which is MATLAB and needs the user's yes, and Herbst found roughness unreliable on distorted chords. Mastering lowers crest, the best drive cue on isolated guitar, so row 3's sweep must pass through a limiter before any crest measure is trusted. **Untested proposals:** the effective-drive label (output energy the best linear DI-to-output filter cannot explain), the sweep's pass bar, the Rnonlin-style and tilt-removed rows, and contrasts A–C.

## Few labels teach an offset, and the mix hides the rest

The project's adaptation runs map out a trade-off. Joint training at a quarter real share cost the plugin read. A tenth lost the real gain. Real-only fine-tuning raised the plugin loss from about 5.4 to 9.3–20.4 (F39–F42). And on held-out songs nothing beat the real-amp centre. No audio paper tests adaptation for regression with fewer than 500 target labels, so the ranking leans on analogues. The best ones touch the pretrained model lightly. Weight-space interpolation of the pretrained and fine-tuned models gained 4–6 points under shift ([WiSE-FT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903)), and averaging cut multilingual ASR error from 34.73 to 21.76 WER ([Vander Eeckt & Van hamme 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.15282v2)). Linear probing then fine-tuning beat full fine-tuning by 10% out of distribution ([LP-FT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)). Output-level shifts are best fixed at the last layer ([surgical fine-tuning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.11466)). Row 1's bias-only arm is therefore also a diagnostic. If it matches FT, the real labels carry an offset and no per-track signal, and the next investment belongs in the input, not the model. Two sim-to-real results bear on the plugin-to-real gap. Making the simulation's nuisance chain more realistic helped room regression with zero real labels ([Srivastava et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.09133)). But fine-tuning on a small real set sometimes hurt cross-dataset guitar transcription ([SynthTab](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09085v4)). The search labels are noisy too. Re-searching 20–50 rows from new seeds and comparing restart spread with spread across recordings shows which knobs carry no per-recording information (an untested proposal).

On the input side, a source's own information helps far more than a new spectrogram. A harmonic comb at the target's notes lifted instrument assignment in real polyphonic music from about 0.6 to 0.9 F with oracle pitch, while CQT and mel tied ([Lordelo et al. 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000048.pdf)). Training on paired isolated and mixed versions made singer embeddings work in mixes ([Lee & Nam 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.11139)). A stem beside the mix helped beat tracking only through a learned fuser (0.762 → 0.774 F1) ([Chiu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08685)). So the project's linear mix-plus-stem null (24 against 30 heads) does not close the question for a CNN. Averaging short windows is standard in tagging ([Won et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00751)). But pooling shrinks only error that differs between windows: with correlation ρ the variance falls to ρ + (1 − ρ)/K of one window's, so a shared render-to-real bias survives it. Loudness normalisation before separation is proven, cutting a 2.5 dB drum loss to 0.4 dB ([Jeon & Lee 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.14355.pdf)), and it is already in the build. **Untested proposals:** mask-weighted pooling, residual-relative features and the probe gate of 37 heads.

## Conclusion

The field's proven tools mostly fix a different problem: a model that predicts badly from clean, plentiful, synthetic data. The project's model reads the clean target well, loses most of that in the mix, and lands on a real-amp offset when real labels arrive. The highest-value moves are therefore diagnostics that cost almost nothing and say where the per-track signal dies. Does bias-only tuning match FT? Does cross-validation pick the centre? Is ρ between windows near 1? Does an oracle comb recover heads from the mix? If all four say "offset only", no head, loss or optimiser from this survey will beat the centre on songs, and the effort belongs in reading the guitar out of the mix.

Drive needs its own treatment twice over. It carries most of the score's gain on real amps, and it is where the score and the ear part ways. Recasting drive as breakup plus effective drive, and scoring it with a term that separates nonlinear change from tilt, are one decision, not two. Both are decided cheaply by a gain sweep and the stored ear pages, before any render budget goes to proxies, flows or reward training.
