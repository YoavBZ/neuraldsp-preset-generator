# Adapt lightly, recast drive, check by ear

*Research, 2026-11-04, for the ML models and scoring review session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

No published method recovers amp or effect settings from a full commercial mix, and none has been tested on a guitar amp plugin with real recordings, so nothing below is proven on this problem. The closest real-recording evidence is one vocal study ([Yu et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.11315v1)). It scored 65 MedleyDB vocals against oracle fits, with DiffVox's 365 presets (fitted to private multitracks) as prior and retrieval pool. Its 16 listeners rated plain regression lowest, and MFCC nearest-neighbour retrieval tied the best search. Search with a preset prior left 25% less l/r MSS excess than regression (about 11% on m/s), but it ran Adam through a differentiable effects model, so it is untested gradient-free. Yu's own prior-free Adam re-run of ST-ITO (not the published CMA-ES system) was mixed against regression. The big wins elsewhere came on synthesizers with tens of thousands to ten million renders, no input signal and synthetic targets: flow heads 1.2–2.4× better than regression, proxy or PNP loss terms 7–38%, and about 18–41% out of domain from a reward step (DDSynth-RL's got worse in domain). Drive is the one topic where studies agree on a mechanism. Below breakup the distortion touches only the note attacks, every published estimator fixes the input level, and clean-versus-breakup detection is near-solved on isolated guitar. Nothing tests few-label adaptation for audio regression, or a listener-validated measure on amp-knob steps. The evidence best supports two changes. **First, a light-touch real-amp correction:** interpolate the plugin model W with its fine-tune FT (no training), or tune only W's bias, head or first block (minutes per arm). Testing costs about 1.0–1.4k renders (16–24 min). **Second, recast drive** as a breakup class plus one effective-drive value at a fixed input-level convention, screened by a gain sweep of about 130 renders. The judge weighs treble well above the ear, and drive above it on the clean amp only. Among S, FT and D_fix it agrees with the ear at +0.04 [−0.29, +0.39] (F44–F45, F54 in `docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`). So every finalist should end on a short blind page for the one listener.

## Thirteen methods ranked for one Mac

The ranking weighs evidence strength, fit to the project's measured failure and cost. That failure (`docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`) has three parts. The backing costs most of the tone reading: a linear probe reads 86 of 116 heads from the isolated target render, 30 from the mix and 20 from the stem (`docs/tone-info-probe.md`). Real labels move the model to a real-amp centre, with no further gain shown at this power (F41–F42), though a per-track factory pick using the true DI beats S by −0.118 (F42's audit). And about 80% of FT's real-reamp gain is drive (F43). Tier **A** is proven on comparable data (real recordings scored against hundreds of fitted presets); only Yu's kNN arms qualify (MFCC for, AFx-Rep against), and they are vocals. Tier **B** is proven only on synthetic or other domains, or with a method the project cannot run. Tier **C** is paper or derivation only. **Every test design and pass bar below is the researchers' own proposal, untested and not a published protocol.** The evidence is thin throughout. Row 2's tier-A arm and row 8 rest on one vocal study, and row 6 on snippets. No row has been tested on a guitar amp in a mix.

Power is the binding limit. BKS is one band with 8 DIs, and V-real-11 has 7 bands with a ±0.07 interval (F41). So zero-render screens choose the candidates, and renders go only to finalists. Costs assume 1 s per render on one process; the measured median is about 1.6 s (`docs/research/direct-model-constant-2026-10.md`). Counts are arithmetic and leave out each process's priming render. X-LANCE takes about 4.3 s a clip on MPS (F50). Target and other-guitar audio is stored. Mixes and stems are kept as uint8 96-band half log-mels, except the 770 rebuilt mixes and stems F52 kept as audio, and mixes rebuild on the CPU with zero renders (F52). Terms: FS and NS are plugin-render test sets (new settings on seen performances; two held-out songs); BKS is one band's real reamps; GOAT renders real DIs through NAM captures; V-real-11 is 11 parts in 7 bands of held-out real songs; S is one setting per amp and class; top-1 is a model's single answer; E-cov is oracle best-of-K over its samples. This report builds on, and does not repeat, the earlier surveys of auditory models (`docs/research/perceptual-measures-2026-11.md`), blind reverb and delay (`docs/research/blind-reverb-delay-2026-11.md`) and amp captures (`docs/research/real-amp-chains-2026-11.md`). Star counts and push dates are from the GitHub API on 2026-11-04 ([batch 1](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=repo:csteinmetz1/st-ito+repo:adobe-research/DeepAFx-ST+repo:adobe-research/DeepAFx+repo:lylyhan/perceptual_neural_physical+repo:pcmbs/synth-proxy+repo:DDSynth-RL/DDSynthRL+repo:spotify/pedalboard+repo:sbi-dev/sbi+repo:CyberAgentAILab/cmaes+repo:CMA-ES/pycma+repo:csteinmetz1/dasp-pytorch+repo:mcomunita/nablafx&per_page=30), [batch 2](https://api.github.com/search/repositories?q=repo:DBraun/SynAPSE+repo:peladeaucome/DAFx_Params_Distrib+repo:SonyResearch/Fx-Encoder_PlusPlus+repo:facebookresearch/nevergrad+repo:optuna/optuna+repo:pytorch/botorch+repo:probabilists/zuko+repo:atong01/conditional-flow-matching+repo:acids-ircam/flow_synthesizer+repo:gwendal-lv/preset-gen-vae+repo:DBraun/synth-proxy+repo:ben-hayes/synth-permutations&per_page=30)). **Every download or new install named below is the ML session's and the user's call:** AFx-Rep's 1.16 GB weights, gfx_classifier, MoSQITo, TLlib, cmaes, Optuna, TorchCFM, zuko, sbi and Basic Pitch.

Several methods were dropped. A neural proxy of Morgan is out. In DeepAFx-ST the half-hybrid proxy ended worse than the unprocessed input on MSD in the synthetic test (though better there on STFT and spectral centroid), and on RMS and LUFS in the realistic one (though better there on MSD and spectral centroid) ([DeepAFx-ST](https://arxiv.org/html/2207.08759v1), Tables 2–3). Pretrained music encoders are out as the main reader, since no attribute-regression result exists under 1,000 labels and they discard level (their CC BY-NC weights are allowed by §5). The pattern is consistent. Parameter loss is the stable base. An audio-domain term added on top improves sound by roughly 5–40% and usually worsens parameter error. An audio loss alone is the worst choice. But every large win sits where the project is not: tens of thousands to millions of renders, no input signal, synthetic targets, a differentiable effect model or a dry signal at test time. The project's failure is one of domain and identifiability, while most of these methods change the loss, the head or the optimiser. That is why rows 1–5 lead and the synth-scale methods trail.

Two caveats limit even the real-vocal evidence. Retrieval inherits its embedding's robustness to the backing, which is what failed here: the project's tone encoder failed on records, and separation cut Fx-Encoder++ retrieval from 19.9 to 7.1 R@1 ([Fx-Encoder++](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02273v1)). And prior-regularised search needs a working objective, while Yu's ran on gradients. Through the C′ rebuilt DI the local Spearman is −0.126 (`docs/research/local-objective-2026-10.md`), so the prior can at best regularise arm A's gradient-free search, not rescue a blind song search. ST-ITO's authors say it "does not work well for … guitar tone matching" and give no reason ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)). The researchers infer four causes: a uniform prior, a peak-normalising extractor that discards level, an embedding not injective to style, and no amp or cab model in its chains. That is inference, not a finding.

## Drive reads only past breakup, and only at a fixed level

Drive is where the project's score gains are largest (F43). The judge weighs treble well above the ear, and drive above it on the clean amp only (F44–F45). On isolated guitar, level-free statistics separate tone classes well. Across 180 peak-normalised chords through five valve amps, ANSI S3.4 loudness split clean, overdrive and distortion at ηp² .81, and MIRtoolbox's spectral centroid ("sharpness") at .76. Roughness was weaker and interacted with the chord ([Herbst 2019](https://pure.hud.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/15105454/Herbst_Distortion_and_Rock_Guitar_Harmony.pdf)). On peak-normalised files that loudness acts like a crest measure (the researchers' inference). Learned regressors reach a gain MAE of 0.014–0.05, but only on 2-second notes at fixed level, and Comunità's held up only for gain above 0.5 in one cross-condition ([Comunità 2021](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2012.03216)). Hinrichs found errors near 0.12 "almost exclusively" below gain 0.3, where the distortion touches only the note attacks ([Hinrichs 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)). In its synthetic mixes, gain error grew at most about 1.5× from the quietest backing to the loudest (read from Fig. 7a), and at a 0 dB backing the distortion and overdrive rows stayed near 0.97–1.0. No estimator separates input level from the drive knob, and NAM records the analog level rather than estimating it ([NAM calibration](https://neural-amp-modeler.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorials/calibration.html)). So from a song the observable is effective drive.

Listener evidence points the same way, though none of it varies an amp's gain. Soft clipping changed distortion ratings little and hard clipping a lot ([Tan, Moore & Zacharov 2003](https://convexoptimization.com/TOOLS/Tuan.pdf)). Excerpts at the edge of breakup exposed amp-model differences that high gain hid ([Cassidy & De Sena 2023](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2023/DAFx23_paper_40.pdf)), and ESR mis-ordered two models on a high-gain amp ([Wright et al. 2020](https://acris.aalto.fi/ws/portalfiles/portal/41964332/Real_time_guitar_amplifier_emulation.pdf)). The project's listener heard +10 dB clearly on SW50R crunch and very clearly on AC20 clean, but not on PR12 clean or PR12 crunch (F45, F47). That fits a "breakup edge" hypothesis better than a clean-against-crunch rule, on 4 items and one listener.

For scoring, the one listener-validated design that keeps linear and nonlinear distortion apart is Moore and Tan's weighted sum of a linear prediction and a nonlinear one (Rnonlin). It reached r above 0.90 on music and 0.85 on speech, with weights fitted on artificial distortions (response ripples, peak clipping) and real transducers ([Moore et al. 2004](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=13034), snippet only; no open code). In sharpness-matched distorted guitar, two of three perceptual dimensions were predicted after the spectral tilt was removed ([Marui & Martens 2005](https://aes2.org/publications/elibrary-page/?id=13202), snippet only). That is the case for row 6. Audiobox Aesthetics tells its raters to penalise distortion, so it does not help ([Audiobox Aesthetics](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05139v1)). Vassilakis log roughness tracks listeners on instrument tones at r 0.82–0.87 ([Czedik-Eysenberg & Reuter 2025](https://homepage.univie.ac.at/christoph.reuter/unterwegs/DAGA2025_Czedik-Eysenberg_Reuter_PAPER.pdf)). But it runs in MIRtoolbox, which is MATLAB and needs the user's yes, and Herbst found roughness unreliable on distorted chords. Mastering lowers crest, which the researchers infer is a main drive cue, so row 3's sweep passes through a limiter. **Untested proposals:** the effective-drive label (output energy the best linear DI-to-output filter cannot explain), the sweep's pass bar, the Rnonlin-style and tilt-removed rows, and contrasts A–C.

## Few labels show only an offset so far, and the mix hides the rest

The project's adaptation runs map out a trade-off. Joint training at a quarter real share cost the plugin read, and a tenth lost the real gain. Real-only fine-tuning raised the plugin loss from about 5.4 to 9.3–20.4 (F39–F42). On held-out songs nothing was shown to beat the real-amp centre at this power. No audio paper tests adaptation for regression with fewer than 500 target labels, so the ranking leans on analogues, and the best ones touch the pretrained model lightly. Weight-space interpolation gained 4–6 points under shift ([WiSE-FT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.01903)). Averaging cut multilingual ASR error from 34.73 to 21.76 WER ([Vander Eeckt & Van hamme 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2210.15282v2)). Linear probing before fine-tuning beat full fine-tuning by 10% out of distribution ([LP-FT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054)). Output-level shifts are best fixed at the last layer and input-level shifts at the first block. On Camelyon17, a natural shift the paper calls "closer to an input-level shift", first-layer tuning reached 95.6 against 92.3 for full fine-tuning and 90.1 for the last layer ([surgical fine-tuning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.11466)). The plugin-to-real gap may be partly input-side, because a real amp, cab and room colour the spectrum, so row 1 tries the first block beside the bias and head. Even if bias-only matches FT, per-track headroom exists: F42's factory pick beats S by −0.118, and F54 heard FT and D_fix far above S on two clean parts (picked after the answers). A more realistic nuisance chain helped room regression with zero real labels ([Srivastava et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.09133)). But fine-tuning on a small real set sometimes hurt cross-dataset guitar transcription ([SynthTab](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.09085v4)).

On the input side, a source's own information helps far more than a new spectrogram. A harmonic comb at the target's notes lifted instrument assignment in real polyphonic music from about 0.6 to 0.9 F with oracle pitch, while CQT and mel tied ([Lordelo et al. 2021](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2021/paper/000048.pdf)). Paired isolated and mixed training made singer embeddings work across synthetic mixes, though not better than mix-only training on real pop ([Lee & Nam 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1906.11139)). A stem beside the mix helped beat tracking only through a learned fuser (0.762 → 0.774 F1) ([Chiu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08685)). So the project's linear mix-plus-stem null (24 against 30 heads) does not close the question for a CNN. Averaging short windows is standard in tagging ([Won et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00751)). But pooling shrinks only error that differs between windows: with equal variances and a common correlation ρ, the variance falls to ρ + (1 − ρ)/K of one window's, so a shared render-to-real bias survives it. Loudness normalisation before separation is proven, cutting a 2.5 dB drum loss to 0.4 dB ([Jeon & Lee 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.14355.pdf)), and it is already in the build. **Untested proposals:** mask-weighted pooling, residual-relative features and the 37-head heuristic.

## Conclusion

The field's proven tools mostly fix a different problem: a model that predicts badly from clean, plentiful, synthetic data. The project's model reads the isolated target render well, loses most of that in the mix, and lands near a real-amp offset when real labels arrive. The highest-value moves are diagnostics that cost almost nothing and show where the per-track signal is lost. Does bias-only tuning match FT, or does the first block do better? Does cross-validation pick the centre? Is ρ between windows near 1? Does an oracle comb recover heads from the mix? If all four point to an offset, no head, loss or optimiser from this survey is likely to beat the centre on songs at this power, and the effort belongs in reading the guitar out of the mix. The per-track factory pick and F54's two clean parts say the prize is there.

Drive needs its own treatment twice over. It carries most of the score's gain on real amps, and the judge weighs it above the ear on the clean amp. Recasting drive as breakup plus effective drive, and scoring it with a term that separates nonlinear change from tilt, are one decision, not two. Both are decided cheaply by a gain sweep and the stored ear pages, before any render budget goes to proxies, flows or reward training.
