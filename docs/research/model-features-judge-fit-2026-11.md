# Fix the heads before the backbone

*Research, 2026-11-05, for the ML models and scoring review session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

*Tags: **[M]** measured and published; **[R]** measured in this repo; **[I]** inferred; **[J]** judgement; **[none]** no evidence found. Every test design and pass bar below is the researchers' own untested proposal, not a published protocol. This report builds on the methods report, [`docs/research/proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md`](proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md) (PR #379), and does not repeat it. Every download or install named here is the ML session's and the user's call: EfficientAT and AFx-Rep weights, HAAQI, GstPEAQ, kadtk, choix, POT or ott-jax, and TabPFN v2, which fetches its weights on first use. The list is not exhaustive.*

The model family is about right. What needs work is the heads, how invariant the inputs are, and the judge, not the backbone [J]. No published work shows a bigger, pretrained-transformer or time-aware network fixing what fails here: a plugin-to-stem domain gap (AUC 0.997), a backing that hides most of what the clean target carries, and 520 labels on a 0.25 lattice with 16–32% search noise [R]. **For A**, keep a small CNN, with categorical heads binned on the label lattice and conditioned on the amp and switches. The one backbone worth screening is a frozen EfficientAT mn10 (MIT) with a reduced-rank ridge, decided by zero-shot transfer from plugin rows [J]. **For B**, the clean target already reads 86–87 of 116 heads, so the picks change what the input throws away, not its resolution: a drive branch of onset-split statistics and flatness, and a band-limited long-term spectrum for tone and cab. **For C**, keep J0, and report its `tonal` term and a cross-fitted split-half term beside it to lessen its 1/d² flattening. Add at most one more term, with one fitted weight [J]. Nothing song-only is validated [none]; an unbiased MMD on AFx-Rep windows is the least-bad bet [J]. **For D**, count every candidate, nest selection by band, read one primary contrast, and give each finalist about 27–45 independent bands [I]. The plan should run zero-render screens first, then retrains that take minutes, then about 1,340 renders (about 36 min) per finalist [R]. Flows, simulation-based inference and fine-tuning big encoders wait [J].

**How every bar below reads [J].** Knob reads use `learn/reads_v3.py`: fold-demeaned, recording-level r on held GOAT, paired against the named comparator. ## A. A small CNN with categorical heads fits 520 lattice labels

The evidence does not point at the architecture, and neither does the failure [J]. On IDMT-SMT's unprocessed guitar notes and chords, a two-layer CNN given the true effect class reads drive-pedal gain at 0.040 MAE, averaged over 8 set-ups ([Comunità et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1)) [M]. Depth and recurrence add little across datasets (see "What not to try") [M]. Transformers win only at scale. From scratch on AudioSet's 22k-clip balanced set, AST scored 0.148 mAP against 0.347 when pretrained ([Gong et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.01778)), and its synth-matching win over a CNN came at 1M renders, the only size trained ([Bruford et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16643v1)) [M]. W reads GOAT tone at +0.222 with no real rows [R], so the gap is in transfer, not in fit [I].

The heads are where the measured problem sits [J]. Real labels lie on a lattice of S + k·0.25, yet the mixture's sigma floor is 0.0025 (`learn/full_space.py:434`) [R]. So a component on a lattice point can earn about +5 nats per knob from the grid alone, while the switch, mic and amp terms cannot go below 0 [I]. Mics take 75% of the real loss at FT's start [R]. Binned heads put every head in one unit, so a fixed weight per family means the same thing everywhere, and tuned fixed weights did as well as MGDA and PCGrad ([Xin et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11379)) [M]. On DX7, one-hot coding of discrete knobs (up to 32 levels) cut an MLP's MFCC error from 17.1 to 12.2, though plain numeric heads scored 16.1, so it supports discretising more weakly than it first looks ([Le Vaillant et al.](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/43332/1/DAFx20in21_paper_7.pdf)) [M, I]. Conditioning the settings on the effect class raised estimation accuracy from 44.6% for a multitask net to 60.7% ([Comunità](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1)) [M]. Coarse grids biased Comunità's gain estimates toward grid values [M], but our grid is the labels' own resolution [I].

Richer joint heads lose on the single answer the product returns. On a many-to-one EQ task with 40k tracks, a flow head scored 13.5 dB SI-SDR against 14.8 dB for a deterministic head, and won only when an oracle picked the best of N samples ([Peladeau et al. 2025](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2025/DAFx25_paper_19.pdf)) [M]. The product has no picker, so a joint density waits for a song-only judge (section C) [I]. Simulation-based inference offers no shortcut: RoPE, its closest analogue to FT, is shown only for 1–4 parameters with clean labels ([Wehenkel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.08719v2)), and unsupervised alignment fails under prior shift ([Elsemüller et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04949v1)) [M], which our labels show (22% outside the plugin labels' 5–95% range, against 8.5%) [R].

Pretrained encoders are the one backbone change with a mechanism for the domain gap, and the evidence for them is thin [J]. No paper reads amp or drive settings from frozen embeddings [none]. In MUSDB probes, eight encoders sit within about one MAE point of each other on distortion (5.2–6.4) and EQ (21.5–22.7) ([RLAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28127)) [M], where a constant guess on a uniform knob scores 25 [I]. Frozen embeddings also keep a direction that separates real sound effects from generated twins (linear probes 0.72–0.90; [Doppelganger](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04337)) [M], a synthetic domain far from plugin renders [I]. So what matters is whether the knob directions sit apart from the domain direction, which costs zero renders to measure [I]. EfficientAT leads, on weaker evidence than it seems [J]. It keeps level through fixed constants on its log-mels ([preprocess.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fschmid56/EfficientAT/main/models/preprocess.py)), and its family ranked synth sweeps best, but its final representation concatenates hand-crafted mel features whose removal from mn04 "led to a significant performance drop" ([Combes et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07635)) [M]. Part of its lead is a mel summary, much like T0's own baseline [I]. The readout should match the labels' effective dimension of 2–6 [R]: ridge or PLS onto label components, with kNN, a GP or TabPFN v2 beside [J]. Label noise caps any reader's r near 0.75–0.81, so today's 0.08–0.26 is mostly lost signal [I].

## B. The mel already holds the information; it lacks invariance

The tone probe has already answered the information question [R]. A linear reader gets 86 of 116 heads from the isolated target, 30 from the mix and 20 from the stem, and a finer STFT gets 87 (`docs/tone-info-probe.md`) [R]. "Readable" means reliably above the constant, and only modestly: the 55 readable knobs gain a median 3.1 points of range against the constant's 17-point error [R]. So added resolution (CQT, finer mels, scattering) is unlikely to help tone or cab [I]. The failure is in transfer. W's skill rests on long-term colour, which separates plugin from real at AUC 0.98–1.00 per fold, with the domains 35 dB apart at 8–12 kHz [R].

The label and the shift live in the same place [I]. In scene classification, the recording device shows up in the per-band means: softened centring of those means (λ 0.9) raised unseen-device accuracy from 43.4% to 61.5% but cut the seen device from 82.2% to 73.8%; full centring gave 60.7% and 72.7% ([Primus & Widmer](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.11764)) [M]. Our recording chain is the label (EQ, cab, mic), so per-clip band normalisation, PCEN and Freq-MixStyle belong only on a drive branch [I]. The cheapest move that should keep tone drops the bands and floor cells where the shift concentrates: a long-term spectrum limited to about 80 Hz–5 kHz, with the floor raised [I]. It may cost treble and presence, so the screen checks tone knob by knob [I].

Drive needs its own inputs. Low-gain distortion shows at the note onsets: in Hinrichs's tests, errors near 0.12 fall "almost exclusively" below gain 0.3 ([Hinrichs et al. 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [M]. A global mean and std blur attack and sustain, so statistics split at the onsets are the plausible fix, not a sequence model [I]. No published ablation shows a TCN, CRNN or Conformer beating pooled statistics for effect settings [none]. Against a physical non-linearity index on crunch, flatness alone reaches Spearman +0.32 (not yet tested), while dynamics plus flatness (+0.20) does not beat mel mean and std (paired difference 0.00 [−0.21, +0.20]) [R]. Hinrichs's results by representation are mixed (row 4 below) [M], and a ridge cannot tell a full DCT from log-mel, so only liftering can matter [I]. Stored mels smear onsets (a 42.7 ms window) and hide modulation above 50 Hz, so roughness needs audio [I].

All five rows share one zero-render screen on the stored mels, inside FTm's folds [J]. (A) counts the heads readable on target, mix and stem, by the tone-probe protocol. (B) is the plugin-against-stem AUC. (C) is a ridge read on held GOAT, paired against the 24-PC ridge on mel mean and std. The bar: (C) clears on the row's primary group, or (B) falls below about 0.9 while (A) holds and (C) is not wholly below 0. The survivors become separate inputs for tone and cab and for drive, since "no single time-frequency representation was optimal" (Hinrichs) [M].

## C. J0 stays, and its flattening has a cheap fix

No published measure has been checked against listeners on amp or effect setting changes, or on a recording against a render [none]. The repo has checked J0 on the latter: Spearman about 0.8 on record match (F53), +0.50 [+0.31, +0.67] on F54, +0.57 on F56 and +0.49 on F57 [R]. In the closest published test, on neural models of drives, fuzzes and compressors, MR-STFT reached −0.427 overall, −0.536 on distortion and −0.277 on overdrive, behind FAD on CLAP for distortion (−0.613) and MAPE for overdrive (−0.361); no metric was fitted to the ratings ([Comunità et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14405)) [M]. MR-STFT is a cell-by-cell spectral distance like J0, so it is a control, not a fix [I].

J0's 1/d² flattening follows from its formula, which makes it fixable [I]. Its total is the mean |D| over cells that already carry about 5 dB of spread (`analysis/aligned.py:403-410`) [R]. A systematic error δ then adds in quadrature, d ≈ √(σ² + δ²), so the log ratio moves about δ²/2d² [I]. The audited table fits roughly: from d 2.73 to 3.91, where 1/d² predicts a 2.05× fall, the 6 dB bell's effect falls 2.1×, the shelf's 1.76× and the 3 dB bell's 2.7× (`docs/ml-deep-review-2026-11.md`) [I]. Every cell-by-cell distance shares this [I]. Two kinds of term should flatten less, though neither escapes, since systematic per-band differences between a recording and a render still dilute a log ratio [I]. One averages before it compares, as J0's own `tonal` does: it moves 2–7× as far for the bell, yet `knob_ceiling.judge` throws it away [R]. The other cross-fits: the product of each band's errors in halves A and B has expected value δ², with no noise floor, as in the unbiased crossnobis distance ([rsatoolbox](https://rsatoolbox.readthedocs.io/en/latest/distances.html)) [M for crossnobis]. Sensitivity is not agreement: the long-term spectrum alone, the project's most linear measure, tracked the ear worst on judge-ear (−0.10) [R]. So these terms go beside J0.

How many weights the ear pages support is a judgement [J]. At J0's R² of 0.36, clinical sample-size rules ask for 14 items for one weight and 27 for two by the least demanding criterion, the R²-difference one ([via arXiv 2510.01210](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01210)), and about 20 and 40 by Riley's shrinkage criterion in its binary-outcome form, n = p/((S − 1) ln(1 − R²/S)) at S = 0.9 ([pmsampsize](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/pmsampsize/master/R/pmsampsize_bin.R)) [I]. The rules count every item and take the largest criterion [M]. The two difference pages hold about 60 items, only about 14 of them heard, in four preset-DI cells [R]. Weighting by the heard items is our judgement, and it points to one weight, two at most [J]. J5's fitted weight was undecided on the fresh chord page and not adopted; the effect size leans against it (J5 − J0 gamma −0.224 [−0.351, −0.063], resting on offline anchors; −0.135 [−0.259, +0.031] on plugin controls alone; F47) [R]. Fit one weight by ordinal regression on the graded answers, with a prior centred on J0, and accept it only on a held-out page [J].

A zero-render battery on the stored pages can screen candidates before any weight is fitted [J]. Its sharpest check, a flip, is confounded [R]. The ear put reverb and delay above a +6 dB high shelf on PR12 clean with a single-note DI (F44–F46), and below it on AC20 clean with a chord DI (F47) [R]. The DI, amp, presets and sitting change together, and F47 says why the effects were heard less "is not separated" [R]. J0's effect-to-shelf ratios (1.30 and 1.72; 1.35 and 1.56) barely move, so J0 gets 2 of the 4 orders right [R, I]. Masking predicts the flip, since dense chords mask the tails and a shelf changes more partials [I]. That is one case for HAAQI or PEAQ's outputs, which are also expected to count small EQ steps less (`docs/research/perceptual-measures-2026-11.md`) [I]. Their best published check, on ODAQ, covers PEAQ only, on codec and separation artefacts far from knob steps (row 2) [M, J]. With four orders and a confound, the flip can drop a candidate but cannot choose between HAAQI and PEAQ [J]. The other checks: no treble step in the top three, which J0 fails (1st, 2nd and 4th of 24) and CDPAM and Zwicker loudness fail outright (F45) [R]; heard drive steps above unheard ones, which J0 passes; and a response to a known error, in split-half SDs, that does not fall 5× from d 2.73 to 6.05 [J].

No song-only judge is validated [none], and cross-content evidence exists only for retrieval. Among 500 candidates, AFx-Rep found the same 8-effect chain under different music at 68.4% top-1, but a single effect at only 3.0% ([StemFX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15634)) [M]. Within one song, FAD is biased upward by about (tr Σ_X + tr Σ_Y)/N ([Chung et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15602)) [M]. A song gives 40–60 windows against embeddings of hundreds of dimensions, which leaves the covariance rank-deficient, so FAD is out [I]. KAD, an unbiased MMD, has no such bias, but no result below N = 100 has been published [M].

## D. Ten candidates need nested folds and 27–45 bands

Selection noise is as large as the effects being chosen [I]. On null data, tuning inside the scoring cross-validation biased the error estimate by −12.2 points, while nested cross-validation was nearly unbiased ([Varma & Simon 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1397873/)) [M]. With about 10 candidates, the winner's expected excess is about 1.54 × SE × √(1 − ρ), where ρ is the correlation between their contrasts [I]. F41's FT − S interval (±0.073 on 6 df) gives an SE of about 0.038, so the excess is 0.058 at ρ = 0 and 0.041 at ρ = 0.5, 1.9–2.6× FT − S itself (+0.022) [I]. If that percentile interval under-covers, as the deep review found, the SE nears 0.05 and the excess 0.077, 3.5× [I]. So declare the candidate count first, dropped arms included; choose settings inside the training folds; score the choose-then-fit procedure by leaving out one band, or one GOAT player (76% of GOAT's labels are one player's) [R]; and compare every candidate against FTm on the same items [J].

On fresh data, read one primary contrast and at most two secondaries in a fixed order, each only if the one before clears, which needs no alpha adjustment ([FDA 2022](https://www.fda.gov/media/162416/download)) [M]. Ten unadjusted null contrasts give a false "clear" about 40% of the time [I]. Decide with a t interval on band means, checked by a wild cluster bootstrap-t, which held about 5% rejection at 6 clusters while the pairs bootstrap "can fail" at 10 or fewer ([Cameron, Gelbach & Miller](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0344)) [M]. Below about 10 independent bands, a row is descriptive only [J]. A 0.05 gain in J0 at 80% power, one-sided at 5% (a 90% interval clearing zero), needs about 27 bands at a band SD of 0.10 and 45 at 0.13; at a 95% interval, 34 and 56 [I]. The ear screens only large gaps: 24 items detect a 75% preference with about 75% power, while 65% needs about 69 items for 80% power, more since items cluster by band [I]. Put at most S and two finalists on a page, and screen and confirm on separate pages [J]. On development sets, accept a change only when it beats the base by one standard error, the Ladder's rule ([Blum & Hardt](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.04585v1)) [M].

## Conclusion

The project's three open problems share one root: the per-band means of the long-term spectrum [I]. The tone and cab labels live there, the domain gap lives there, and J0 flattens because it compares cells before averaging them into those means [R, I]. So invariance has to be built per group of controls, since whatever strips colour helps drive and harms tone [I]. It also makes the domain AUC a poor gate on its own. A reader can transfer at an AUC near 1 if its knob directions sit apart from the domain direction, and fail at a lower AUC if they do not, so every screen here pairs the AUC with a transfer read [I].

The judge and the model now limit each other [J]. A distributional head is no use until something song-only can pick among its samples, and a song-only judge cannot be validated while the ear pages confound effects with the DI [J]. Until both exist, the right product is one honest answer from a small network with categorical heads [J]. Later decisions will turn on J0 gains near 0.05, which only about 27–45 fresh bands can resolve [I]. So the next real cost is bands and listening, not renders or models [I].
