# Fix the heads before the backbone

*Research, 2026-11-05, for the ML models and scoring review session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

*Tags: **[M]** measured and published; **[R]** measured in this repo; **[I]** inferred; **[J]** judgement; **[none]** no evidence found. Every test design and pass bar below is the researchers' own untested proposal, not a published protocol. This report builds on the methods report, [`docs/research/proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md`](proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md) (PR #379), and does not repeat it. Every download or install named here (EfficientAT and AFx-Rep weights, HAAQI, GstPEAQ, kadtk) is the ML session's and the user's call.*

The model family is about right. What needs work is the heads, how invariant the inputs are, and the judge, not the backbone [J]. No published work shows a bigger, pretrained-transformer or time-aware network fixing what fails here: a plugin-to-stem domain gap (AUC 0.997), a backing that hides most of what the clean target carries, and 520 labels on a 0.25 lattice with 16–32% search noise [R]. **For A**, keep a small CNN, with categorical heads binned on the label lattice and conditioned on the amp and switches. The one backbone worth screening is a frozen EfficientAT mn10 (MIT) with a reduced-rank ridge, decided by zero-shot transfer from plugin rows. **For B**, the clean target already reads 86–87 of 116 heads, so the picks change what the input throws away, not its resolution: a drive branch of onset-split statistics and flatness, and a band-limited long-term spectrum for tone and cab. **For C**, keep J0, and report its `tonal` term and a cross-fitted split-half term beside it to work around its 1/d² flattening. Add at most one more term, with one fitted weight. Nothing song-only is validated; an unbiased MMD on AFx-Rep windows is the least-bad bet. **For D**, count every candidate, nest selection by band, read one primary contrast, and give each finalist 25–45 independent bands. The plan should run zero-render screens first, then retrains that take minutes, then about 670 renders per survivor. Flows, simulation-based inference and fine-tuning big encoders wait.

## A. A small CNN with categorical heads fits 520 lattice labels

The evidence does not point at the architecture, and neither does the failure. On isolated guitar, a CNN with two conv layers of 6 and 12 filters reads drive-pedal gain at 0.040 MAE ([Comunità et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1)) [M]. Across datasets, depth helps only a little (ResNet6 0.830 to ResNet18 0.876 micro-F1 on IDMT effect recognition), and a CRNN trailed the ResNet18 at 0.856 ([SFXlearner README](https://github.com/fisheggg/SFXlearner)) [M, README only]. Transformers win only at scale. From scratch on about 20k AudioSet clips, AST scored 0.148 mAP against 0.347 when ImageNet-pretrained ([Gong et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.01778)), and its 3× win over a CNN at synth matching took 1M renders ([Bruford et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16643v1)) [M]. The current net holds only about 0.22M conv weights of its 1.07M [I, from `learn/full_space.py`], and W already reads GOAT tone at +0.222 with no real rows [R]. The gap is in transfer, not in fit [I].

The heads are where the measured problem sits. Real labels lie on the search's 0.25 lattice, yet the mixture's sigma floor is 0.0025 (`learn/full_space.py:434`). So a component placed on a lattice point can earn about +5 nats per knob from the grid alone, while the switch, mic and amp terms cannot go below 0 [I]. Mics take 75% of the real loss at FT's start (`docs/ml-deep-review-2026-11.md`) [R]. Binned heads put every head in one unit, so a fixed weight per family means the same thing everywhere, and tuned fixed weights match GradNorm and PCGrad ([Xin et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2209.11379)) [M]. One-hot heads beat regression on DX7 presets ([Le Vaillant et al.](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/43332/1/DAFx20in21_paper_7.pdf)), and conditioning settings on the effect class raised estimation accuracy from 44.6% to 60.7% ([Comunità](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1)) [M]. Coarse training grids biased Comunità's nets toward grid values [M], but here the grid is the labels' own resolution [I].

Richer joint heads lose on the single answer the product returns. On a many-to-one EQ task with 40k tracks, a flow head scored 13.5 dB SI-SDR against 14.8 dB for a deterministic head. It won only when an oracle picked the best of N samples ([Peladeau et al. 2025](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2025/DAFx25_paper_19.pdf)) [M]. The product has no picker, so a joint density waits for a song-only judge (section C) [I]. Simulation-based inference offers no shortcut. RoPE, its closest analogue to FT, is shown only for 1–4 parameters with clean labels ([Wehenkel et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.08719v2)), and unsupervised alignment fails under prior shift ([Elsemüller et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04949v1)) [M]. Our labels show such a shift: 22% fall outside the plugin labels' 5–95% range, against 8.5% [R].

Pretrained encoders are the one backbone change with a mechanism for the domain gap, and the evidence for them is thin. No paper reads amp or drive settings from frozen embeddings [none]. In MUSDB probes, eight encoders sit within about one MAE point of each other on distortion (5.2–6.4) and EQ (21.5–22.7) ([RLAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28127)) [M]. A constant guess on a uniform knob scores 25, so on EQ all of them look near a constant [I]. Frozen embeddings also keep a direction that separates real from synthetic audio (linear probes 0.72–0.90; [Doppelganger](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04337)) [M]. So the question is whether the knob directions sit apart from it, which can be measured at zero renders [I]. EfficientAT leads. It keeps level through a fixed affine step on its log-mels ([preprocess.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fschmid56/EfficientAT/main/models/preprocess.py)), and it was the strongest family at ranking synth parameter sweeps ([Combes et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07635)) [M]. The readout should match the labels' effective dimension of 2–6 [R]: ridge or PLS onto the labels' principal components, with kNN, a GP or TabPFN v2 as extra rows [J]. Label noise caps any reader's r near 0.75–0.81, so today's 0.08–0.26 is mostly lost signal, not the ceiling [I].

## B. The mel already holds the information; it lacks invariance

The tone probe has already answered the information question. A linear reader gets 86 of 116 heads from the isolated target, 30 from the mix and 20 from the stem, and a finer STFT gets 87 (`docs/tone-info-probe.md`) [R]. So representations that only add resolution (CQT, finer mels, scattering) are unlikely to help tone or cab [I]; in music tagging, mel and STFT differed by at most 0.007 AUC ([Choi et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/1709.01922v3)) [M]. The failure is in transfer. W's skill rests on long-term colour, and the long-term spectrum separates plugin from real at AUC 0.98–1.00 per fold, with the two domains 35 dB apart at 8–12 kHz [R].

The label and the shift live in the same place. In scene classification, the recording device shows up in the per-band means. Centring them raised unseen-device accuracy from 43.4% to 61.5%, but cut the seen device from 82.2% to 73.8% ([frequency-wise normalisation study](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.11764)) [M]. For us that recording chain is the label (EQ, cab, mic), so per-clip band normalisation, PCEN and Freq-MixStyle belong only on a drive branch [I]. The cheapest move that should keep tone drops the bands and floor cells where the shift concentrates: a long-term spectrum limited to about 80 Hz–5 kHz, with the dB floor raised. It may cost treble and presence, so the screen must check tone knob by knob [I].

Drive needs its own inputs. Low-gain distortion shows at the note onsets: in Hinrichs's tests, errors near 0.12 fall "almost exclusively" below gain 0.3 ([Hinrichs et al. 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [M]. A global mean and std blur attack and sustain together, so statistics split at the onsets are the plausible fix, not a sequence model [I]. No published ablation shows a TCN, CRNN or Conformer beating pooled statistics for effect settings [none], and five pooling functions moved tagging F1 only between 45.3 and 50.0 ([Wang et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1810.09050)) [M]. On crunch, flatness alone leads the drive read at +0.32 (not yet tested), while dynamics plus flatness does not beat mel mean and std [R]. Cepstral inputs read distortion gain best in Hinrichs's one four-way ablation [M]. But that MFCC net had about an eighth of the spectrogram net's weights, so capacity is confounded, and a ridge cannot tell a full DCT from log-mel; only liftering can matter [I]. Stored mels smear onsets (a 42.7 ms window) and hide modulation above 50 Hz, so roughness needs audio [I]. A deconvolved spectrum has a precedent in speech that needs about 10 dB SNR ([Gaubitch et al.](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/taslp/GaubitchBN13.html), abstract only) [M]. In the stem, the target sits a median 6.2 dB under the other guitars (`docs/ml-pipeline-review.md`, step 16) [R], so deconvolution can work only in single-guitar sections [I].

All five rows share one zero-render screen on the stored mels, inside FTm's folds [J]. (A) counts the heads readable on target, mix and stem, by the tone-probe protocol. (B) is the plugin-against-stem AUC. (C) is a ridge read on held GOAT through `learn/reads_v3.py`, paired against the 24-PC ridge on mel mean and std. ## C. J0 stays, and its flattening has a cheap fix

No measure has been checked against listeners on amp or effect setting changes, or on a recording against a render [none]. The closest test had listeners rate neural models of drives, fuzzes and compressors by MUSHRA. MR-STFT reached a Spearman of −0.427 overall, −0.536 on distortion and only −0.277 on overdrive ([Comunità et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.14405)) [M]. The authors fitted no metric to the ratings [M]. MR-STFT is a cell-by-cell spectral distance like J0, so it is a control, not a fix [I].

J0's 1/d² flattening follows from its formula, which makes it fixable. Its total is the mean |D| over cells that already carry about 5 dB of spread (`analysis/aligned.py:403-410`) [R]. A systematic error δ then adds in quadrature, d ≈ √(σ² + δ²), so the change in the log ratio is about δ²/2d² [I]. That fits the audited table: removing a 6 dB bell moves J0 2.1× less at d 3.91 than at 2.73, where 1/d² predicts 2.05× (`docs/ml-deep-review-2026-11.md`) [I]. Every cell-by-cell distance shares this [I]. Two kinds of term escape it. One averages before it compares, as J0's own `tonal` does: it moves 2–7× as far for the bell, yet `knob_ceiling.judge` throws it away [R]. The other is cross-fitted. The product of each band's error in half A and in half B has the systematic δ² as its expected value, with no noise floor. That is the logic of the unbiased crossnobis distance ([rsatoolbox](https://rsatoolbox.readthedocs.io/en/latest/distances.html)) [M for crossnobis]. Sensitivity is not agreement, though. The long-term spectrum alone is the project's most linear measure, and it tracked the ear worst on judge-ear (−0.10) [R]. So these terms go beside J0, not in its place.

Any added term must earn its weight on the ear pages, and they support one weight, two at most [I]. A clinical sample-size rule gives n ≥ 1 + 12.8p at an R² of 0.36, J0's level, so 14 items for one weight and 27 for two ([via arXiv 2510.01210](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.01210)) [I]. The two difference pages hold about 14 heard items in about 60 [R]. J5's weight, fitted on judge-ear, already lost to J0 on the fresh chord page (gamma −0.224 [−0.351, −0.063]; F47) [R]. Fit one weight with an ordinal (Thurstonian) model and a prior centred on J0, and accept it only if both held-out pages improve [J]. choix (MIT) fits Plackett–Luce models in Python, so MATLAB is not needed ([choix](https://github.com/lucasmaystre/choix)) [M for the licence]. Designed pages do better per item: GP preference learning tuned up to four hearing-aid parameters from 10–20 pairwise answers ([Nielsen et al.](https://www2.imm.dtu.dk/pubdb/pubs/6184-full.html), abstract only) [M].

A zero-render battery on the stored pages can sort candidates before any weight is fitted [J]. Its sharpest check is a flip. On the clean amp, the ear put reverb and delay above a +6 dB high shelf on the single-note DI (F44–F46), and below it on the chord DI (F47). J0's effect-to-shelf ratios (1.30 and 1.72; 1.35 and 1.56) do not move between the DIs, so J0 gets 2 of the 4 orders right [R, I]. A masking model predicts the flip: dense chords mask the tails, and a shelf changes more partials [I]. That is the case for HAAQI or PEAQ's outputs, which reached Pearson 0.89 on ODAQ against 0.77 for ViSQOL ([Dick et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.00742)) [M]. But ODAQ holds codec and separation artefacts, far from knob steps [J]. The other checks: the treble steps must not take the top three places (J0, CDPAM and Zwicker loudness fail); heard drive steps must outscore unheard ones (J0 passes); and the response to removing a known error, in split-half SDs, must not fall 5× from d 2.73 to 6.05 [J].

No song-only judge is validated [none]. Evidence across different content exists only for retrieval. Among 500 candidates, AFx-Rep found the same 8-effect chain under different music at 68.4% top-1, but a single effect at only 3.0% ([StemFX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15634)) [M]. Within one song, FAD is biased upward by about (tr Σ_X + tr Σ_Y)/N ([Chung et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15602)) [M]. A song gives 40–60 windows against embeddings of hundreds of dimensions, which leaves the covariance rank-deficient, so FAD is out [I]. KAD, an unbiased MMD, has no such bias, but no result below N = 100 has been published [M].

## D. Ten candidates need nested folds and 25–45 bands

Selection noise is as large as the effects being chosen [I]. On null data, tuning inside the same cross-validation that scored the model biased its error estimate by −12.2 points; nested cross-validation was nearly unbiased ([Varma & Simon 2006](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1397873/)) [M]. With about 10 candidates, the winner's expected excess is about 1.54 × SE × √(1 − ρ), where ρ is the correlation between their contrasts. On a 7-band set that is 0.04–0.08 in J0 log units, 2–3× FT − S [I, from F41–F42]. So declare the candidate count before reading, dropped arms included. Choose each candidate's settings inside the training folds. Score the whole choose-then-fit procedure by leaving out one band at a time, or one player at a time on GOAT, where 76% of labels come from one player [R]. And compare every candidate against FTm on the same items [J].

On fresh data, read one primary contrast and at most two secondaries in a fixed order, which needs no alpha adjustment ([FDA 2022](https://www.fda.gov/media/162416/download)) [M]. Ten unadjusted null contrasts give at least one false "clear" about 40% of the time [I]. Decide with a t interval on band means, and check it with a wild cluster bootstrap-t. That bootstrap held about 5% rejection at 6 clusters, while the pairs bootstrap "can fail" at 10 or fewer ([Cameron, Gelbach & Miller](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0344)) [M]. Below about 10 independent bands, a row is descriptive only. A 0.05 gain in J0 at 80% power needs about 27 bands at a band SD of 0.10, and about 45 at 0.13 [I]. BKS, a single band, can veto a candidate but never select one [J]. The ear can screen only large gaps. 24 items detect a 75% preference with about 75% power, while 65% needs about 67 items [I]; FTm against FT came out 4 of 10 (F56) [R]. Put at most S and two finalists on a page, and screen and confirm on separate pages [J]. On the development sets, accept a change only when it beats the current base by one standard error, the Ladder's rule ([Blum & Hardt](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.04585v1)) [M].

## Conclusion

The project's three open problems share one root: the per-band means of the long-term spectrum. The tone and cab labels live there, the domain gap lives there, and J0 flattens because it compares cells before averaging them into those means. So invariance has to be built per group of controls. Whatever strips colour helps drive and harms tone, which is why one shared input, and one shared fix for the gap, keep failing [I]. It also means the domain AUC is a poor gate on its own. A reader can transfer at an AUC near 1 if its knob directions sit apart from the domain direction, and fail at a lower AUC if they do not, so every screen here pairs the AUC with a transfer read [I].

The judge and the model now limit each other. A distributional head is no use until something song-only can pick among its samples, and a song-only judge cannot be validated while the ear pages confound effects with the DI [J]. Until both exist, the right product is one honest answer from a small network with categorical heads [J]. Later decisions will turn on gains near 0.05 in J0, which only 25–45 fresh bands can resolve. So the next real cost is bands and listening, not renders or models [I].
