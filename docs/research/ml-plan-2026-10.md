## 1. Where things stand

- **The goal** (the user's, 2026-10-14): from a song and its research, set every control so the
  preset sounds as close as possible to the song. Choosing the nearest factory preset is not the
  goal; factory presets are starting points (`docs/ROADMAP.md`).
- **Searching the settings works, with the true DI** (`docs/knob-ceiling-results.md`).
  - The test ran on development data: 24 parts × 3 amps in 14 bands.
  - It searched input gain, the amp's knobs and switches, both mics, and cab position and
    distance.
  - Scoring was cross-half: search on one half of the clip, score on the other.

  | Comparison | Gain (negative = closer) |
  |---|---|
  | Search from today's start S, vs S ("K_S") | **−0.253**, about 22% closer, 14/14 bands |
  | Search from the best menu preset M, vs M ("K_M") | −0.205, about 19% |
  | The amp's EQ on top | −0.079 more |
  | Best menu preset vs S | only −0.091 |

  - **The gain fits each recording:** another part's settings score worse than the menu preset.
  - **One listener chose the searched settings on 7 of 7 blind trials.**
  - **The search from M had −0.130 by render 25 of its 200;** K_S's own curve is stored but not
    yet reported.
  - **These are lower bounds:** the search is known to fall short.
- **So the menu was the limit, not the plugin.** Every earlier "a perfect DI buys only 12–17%"
  figure came from ranking the 111-preset menu.
- **The open question is the song side:** the product has no DI, only the song.
- **The render-order bug is fixed and audited** (`docs/render-order-audit.md`).
  - Reused Morgan renders carried the previous render at hiss level; `isolate=True` fixes it.
  - No past decision was overturned. Set 5's C′-over-C is now fragile.

## 2. Two ways to get settings from a song, and why both are tested

The judge can only compare a recording with a render **of the same notes**, and a song gives no
DI. There are two ways around that.

**Route 1: rebuild the DI, then search.**

- **How it works:** the C′ network estimates the guitar's DI from the separated stem. Candidate
  settings are rendered on that estimate, so the real plugin is checked against the song at every
  step.
- **For it:**
  - it keeps the judge's aligned, same-notes comparison;
  - the project's own evidence says that comparison is where the value is (next route).
- **Against it:**
  - its held-out evidence is from menu ranking on isolated amp tracks, never from stems;
  - the rebuilt DI sounds "dirty" and is unrelated to the true DI above 3 kHz;
  - on held-out crunch it only matched today's start (−0.023);
  - a search may amplify its errors.

**Route 2: predict the settings directly from the stem.**

- **How it works:** a model is trained on the project's own renders (many DIs × many settings),
  with no DI at all at song time.
- **For it:**
  - it needs no C′;
  - it can be trained in song conditions: mixed into real backings, mastered, and passed through
    the separator.
- **Against it:** the project tried this once, in the settings-regression proof of concept
  (`docs/preset-model-poc-results.md`: PR12 only, 18,843 renders, 14 players).
  - **The result:** −0.037 against a bar of about −0.105, carried by two parts.
  - **It hedged towards average settings:** its predictions spread only 0.44 as much as the
    labels.
  - **What it learned:** on held-out-band renders it learned mic type (75% against 40%), cab
    placement, the pedal knobs and the filters. **On the EQ bands and input gain it did no better
    than the median.**
  - **Why: the player moves the sound about as much as the preset does.** One preset's sound
    moves 1.9 dB per band between players, while clean presets differ by 1.45 dB. From the stem
    alone, "bright player, dark preset" looks like "dark player, bright preset".
  - **Its follow-up points to the DI.** Choosing settings from a *perfectly known* long-term tone
    curve bought about 5%; choosing by the judge's aligned comparison bought about 20%. In the
    project's words, "what carries the value is the judge's aligned, moment-by-moment comparison,
    which needs the take's DI".

**Why both are still tested.** Route 2 never had song-condition training, three amps, more
players, or an output that allows several right answers, and it is cheap to test.

- **Expect route 2 to trail route 1** unless that training adds what the proof of concept lacked.
- **If it doesn't trail, the rebuilt DI is not needed.**

The next experiment compares the two, and their combination, on the same cells against K_S.

### Step 3. The comparison: three ways to get settings from a song

**One declared plan, three arms, the same cells, the same scoring.**

**Shared by all arms:**

- **Cells:** knob ceiling's 24 parts × 3 amps in 14 bands, both directions.
  - Under decision 1(b), route 1 runs on its 7 leak-free parts, and arm B is reported on both sets.
  - These parts were picked for clean stems (≥ 1 dB SNR), so every result is optimistic until
    step 4's unfiltered parts are scored.
- **Input:** the part's htdemucs_6s stem. The songs check's 10-s, crop-aligned stems can be cut
  to halves.
  - Each arm reads only the **search half**.
  - Known mismatches: these stems come from the instrumental mix, with no vocals; bank clips are
    separated without the product's 30-s context.
  - Report with and without the songs in MUSDB18-train (the separator trained on them).
- **Output space:** knob ceiling's (`learn/knob_ceiling.py` `space(amp, start)`), on top of the
  dry class start S for that amp:
  - searched: input gain, amp knobs and switches, both mics, cab position and distance, the right
    mic's level and switch;
  - kept from S: pedals, EQ, room mics, pan, phase and the amp.
- **Score:**
  - render the arm's settings through the **measure DI** (the true DI re-equalised to the average
    guitar at −22.9 LUFS, `knob_ceiling.measure_di`);
  - the judge (`flat`, with v3 beside it) scores the **held half**, band-weighted;
  - report each arm-vs-arm difference with a band-clustered 90% interval.
- **Comparators:**
  - S;
  - **K_S** (knob ceiling's own search from S: same space, 150 renders);
  - M;
  - the C′ menu pick.
- **First, at zero renders:** read K_S's convergence at 25, 50 and 100 renders from knob ceiling's
  stored curves. It sets what to expect from short searches.

#### Arm A: rebuilt-DI search

- **What.**
  - C′ (the fold that never saw the part's band) rebuilds the DI from the stem with
    `learn/direc.py` `rebuild`, uncut, at −22.9 LUFS. **Not** `songs_check.build_di`, which uses the
    old networks and the retired 3-kHz cut.
  - `knob_search` runs from S, 150 renders per direction, scored by `learn/rebuilt_judge.py`
    `rebuilt_distance` (lag −52) against the stem.
  - Count how often its fallback fires.
- **Known-target control.**
  - 12 cells (6 parts that C′ never trained on × PR12 and SW50R).
  - The "recording" is a moved preset played through the part's own DI.
  - Run `knob_search` at 150 renders twice: once with the true DI, once with C′'s rebuild of that
    recording.
  - Gap closed = (d_start − d_found) / (d_start − d_target), on the held half.

  Read in order; the first match wins:

  | If | Then |
  |---|---|
  | The true-DI run closes ≥ 50% on fewer than 8 of 12 | The search setup is wrong. Stop |
  | The rebuilt run closes ≥ 50% on ≥ 8 of 12, or lands within 0.03 of the true-DI run | The control passes |
  | Otherwise | The rebuilt DI fails even on known targets |

- **Cost.** 21.6k search + 3.6k control + 2.7k for the C′ menu pick ≈ 28k renders.

#### Arm B: direct settings model

- **The model.** Start from the proof of concept's network body and per-fold loop (`learn/train.py`
  `build_model`, `fit`), with six changes:
  1. **keep input level:** its `Frontend` normalised every crop's loudness, so replace it, and add
     LUFS as a side input;
  2. compute features on the CPU and cache them;
  3. labels in knob ceiling's space for three amps (its `learn/pr12.py` was PR12-only);
  4. categorical heads for the mics and switches, and a **mixture-density head** for the
     continuous settings, so several right answers are allowed and it doesn't hedge;
  5. the amp and the class start as input conditions;
  6. two inputs: the stem, and the mix with the stem's agreement as a feature.

  To output one preset, take the heaviest mixture component's mean and each categorical's top
  choice.
- **The training bank.**
  - **Renders:** use `learn/render_job.py`'s worker pattern, but with `isolate=True` (it doesn't
    isolate today) and sampling in knob ceiling's space instead of `pr12.py`.
  - **DIs:**
    - every classed development part (82);
    - Guitar-TECHS P1 and P2 (P3 is held out and never read);
    - Five-guitar if approved.

    Extend `learn/di_pool.py`, which today reads only set-2 DIs and P1. Every DI goes through the
    same re-equalisation as the score (`learn/confirm_run.py` `build_di("measfix")`), so a
    label's input gain means what K_S's does.
  - **Settings:** random within the output space, on the six dry class starts (clean and crunch ×
    3 amps). Half are broad; half are jittered around the starts and around other folds' **K_S**
    optima. K_M's optima sit on another preset's pedals and EQ, so they are not used.
  - **Labels:** the settings that made each render, so no judge is needed in training. The proof
    of concept advised training on sound, not knobs, but its own follow-up capped that route.
    So: knob labels with a mixture-density head, always scored on sound, never on knob error.
  - **Augmentation after rendering** (no extra renders):
    - mix into other folds' backing tracks, at the real guitar-to-backing level (measure it first
      from the development mixes);
    - sometimes add a second guitar;
    - bus compression, limiting and codec damage;
    - htdemucs_6s on about half the mixtures.
  - **Size:** pilot about 10k renders of 10-s clips and about 5k separations (≈ 7 h CPU). Grow to
    about 30k only if the pilot passes.
- **Folds (declared and asserted in code), so no evaluated part's own DI trains the model reading
  it:**
  1. Bands in `train.k3_folds()` keep their fold 0–3, so arm B's fold f and C′'s fold f leave out
     the same bands (arm C needs both).
  2. Every other development band that gives a DI, a backing or a label (set 3, and the sets 1–2
     bands outside K3) gets fold 0–3 by a declared draw: sorted names, a new seed, i mod 4. The
     band → fold map is committed with the plan, before any training.
  3. Only never-evaluated sources may train every fold: Guitar-TECHS P1–P2, and Five-guitar.
  4. The fold-f model trains on no render, backing or K_S optimum from a fold-f band.
  5. Assert that every evaluated part's band is in folds 0–3.
- **Training.**
  - The proof of concept's "about 10 hours for 30 epochs" was all four folds: about 5 min per
    epoch per fold, at 1.3 s per batch of 64.
  - The pilot is about 0.7–2.6 h per fold, 3–10 h for four.
  - Precompute features, and time fold 0's first epoch before declaring.
  - MPS when step 1 isn't running.
- **Positive control on renders**, before anything real:
  - **Targets:** per fold, ≥ 30 planted settings on a class start, rendered through held-apart
    DIs, then mixed, mastered and separated.
  - **Scoring:** render each prediction, the planted settings (d_target) and the median of that
    fold's training labels through the measure DI, and judge each against the planted render's
    held half. S gives d_start.
  - **Pass:** gap closed ≥ 50% on ≥ 2/3 of targets, and closer than the median settings on ≥ 2/3.
  - **If it fails:** fix and re-run at most twice.
- **Controls and diagnostics** (144 renders each unless stated):
  - **shuffled input:** another band's stem, 144–432 renders. It "loses most of the gain" when
    arm B beats it by ≥ 0.05, band-weighted, in ≥ 60% of bands;
  - **a song-blind constant:** the median of other folds' K_S optima per amp and class. Knob
    ceiling found a common pull (less drive, less bass, darker) that a model could learn without
    listening;
  - **amp-track input** instead of the stem: separates the separator's loss from the model's;
  - **prediction spread against label spread** (the proof of concept's 0.44).
- **Amp for a product-like top-1** (declared): either the class's amp in `generate`, or a small
  extra head that predicts which amp's K_S distance is lowest (labels from other folds). Per-amp
  results are reported anyway.
- **Cost.** ≈ 10k bank + ≈ 1.2k for scoring, controls and diagnostics ≈ 11k renders.

#### Arm C: combined

- **What.**
  - Arm B's prediction becomes the start of arm A's search: `knob_search(budget=50)` per
    direction.
  - A separate `budget=150` run only if 50 passes, because `knob_search` splits its budget into
    fixed stages and can't be resumed.
  - Optionally seed from arm B's top few samples.
- **Cost.** 24 × 3 × 2 × 50 = 7.2k renders, plus 21.6k for the 150 variant.

#### Pass rule for each arm (*proposed*)

Held half, band-weighted, on the unfiltered set when step 4 provides it:

1. better than S by at least 0.03, **and** at least 40% of K_S's gain on the same cells (about
   −0.10);
2. better than S in ≥ 60% of bands;
3. neither clean nor crunch worse than S by more than 0.05. Crunch has only 5 bands, so this is a
   harm guard, not a test;
4. arm A only: not worse than the C′ menu pick by more than 0.01;
5. arm B only: clearly better than its shuffled control.

The standard error at 14 bands is about 0.026, so show the interval beside every figure.

## 5. Rules for every step

- **Process:** plan, review, message the product session, run, audit. Changes are dated amendments.
- **Renders:**
  - every Morgan render uses `isolate=True`;
  - trim the first 0.25 s of any isolated render used as audio (the fade-in);
  - reuse processes; never touch the licence daemon;
  - nothing runs 03:00–04:15.
- **Audio:** the renderer and the measure DI run at 48 kHz. Resample only around models that need
  44.1 kHz.
- **MPS:**
  - compute spectral losses and features on the CPU (the `torch.stft` gradient bug above 65,536
    samples);
  - one MPS job at a time;
  - clip gradients by hand.
- **Disk:**
  - keep 30 GB free, so about 35 GB is usable;
  - keep distances and short 16-bit FLACs, not WAVs;
  - arm B's pilot needs about 6–11 GB with features. It cannot share the disk with step 1's
    caches, so it renders after they are deleted.
- **Statistics:** the band is the unit. Report band-weighted results with intervals, and
  leave-one-band-out checks.
- **Held-out data** is used only in step 7, declared first. Nobody listens to sets 5 or 6 before
  that.
- **Licences:** weights and tools must be open (MIT, Apache, BSD, CC0, CC BY). Non-commercial data
  only with the user's yes.

## 7. What not to do

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Rank menu presets as the answer | The menu was the limit (knob ceiling) |
| Predict exact DAC codes | Phase-locked; it failed |
| Re-run the old contrastive encoder recipe (solo renders, 14 players, no separator) | It failed on real recordings |
| Judge a settings model by knob error | Many settings sound alike; judge the sound |
| Use `songs_check.build_di` for route 1 | It uses the old networks and the retired 3-kHz cut |
| Use non-open weights (MERT, MuQ, Fx-Encoder++, PEACE, RelFx, DeepAFx-ST) or unlicensed code (RLAT, Open-Amp, Sound2Synth) | The licence rule |
| Use ToneTwist audio or the Marshall JVM410H set as open data | Non-commercial or no-derivatives sources |
| Bulk-download NAM captures | TONE3000's terms forbid it |
| Switch optimiser without step 5's equal-budget test | No evidence that it helps |
| Treat GuitarToneAdapt as knob truth | Unsourced entries; no "crunch" label |
| Filter stems by SNR in a product-facing test | The product can't filter |

## 8. Known risks

- **The evidence so far is narrow:** development data with the true DI, one take per part, one
  listener. The song side is untested.
- **C′ has never run on a separated stem,** and the project's own evidence expects route 2 to
  trail route 1.
- **Crunch has only 5 bands** in step 3: treat crunch results as descriptive.
- **Today's starts were chosen on 24 of these 25 parts,** which makes gains against them look
  smaller.
- **The judge likes spectral extremes:** 93% of knob-ceiling searches ended with a setting at a
  range limit. Listening is the guard, and the true-DI search labels used for fine-tuning are
  partly "censored" at those limits.
- **The Mac's disk was not seen from here.** Each step confirms its files exist first.
