## Terms used below

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Part** | One guitar track of one development song. It has its DI and its amp (mic'd) track, the "recording". |
| **Band** | One artist's recordings. The unit of independence: folds and statistics are by band. ("EQ bands" means frequency bands; that is the only other use.) |
| **Judge** | `analysis/aligned.py` `aligned_distance`: compares a recording with a render **of the same take's DI**, moment by moment. `flat` is the current version; v3 (`JUDGE_V3`) is the candidate. Lower is closer. Results are log ratios: negative = closer; −0.10 ≈ 10% closer. |
| **Measure DI** | The part's true DI, re-equalised to an average guitar and set to −22.9 LUFS (`learn/knob_ceiling.py` `measure_di`). Every score here renders through it. |
| **Halves; cross-half** | The 10-s clip's halves, A = 1.0–5.5 s and B = 5.5–10 s. Cross-half means: choose or search on one half (the **search half**), score on the other (the **held half**), both ways, then average. |
| **Band-weighted** | Each band counts once, whatever its number of parts. |
| **The menu** | The 111 dry Morgan presets (108 factory + 3 templates). **Dry** = reverb, delay, tremolo, doubler, gate and spring off. |
| **S** | Today's starting preset for the part's class (clean or crunch) on a given amp, made dry. Six in all (`knob_ceiling.START`). |
| **M** | The menu preset closest to the recording on the search half, by the judge with the true DI. |
| **K_S, K_M** | Knob ceiling's searches with the true DI, from S and from M (`docs/knob-ceiling-results.md`). |
| **C, C′** | The networks that rebuild a DI from a recording. C′ is the corrected one (`docs/residual-di-results.md`). |
| **Folds** | Band folds 0–3 from `learn/train.py` `k3_folds()`. A model "for fold f" never trains on fold f's bands. Bands outside that scheme get fold −1. |
| **"The 33"** | Set 3's 33 development parts, over-used in earlier tuning. |
| **Stem** | The guitar output of the htdemucs_6s separator. |
| **Arm** | One method in step 3's comparison. |
| **Gap closed** | (d_start − d_found) / (d_start − d_target): the share of the distance from the start to a known target that a method recovers. |

## 1. Where things stand

- **The goal** (the user's, 2026-10-14): from a song and its research, set every control so the
  preset sounds as close as possible to the song. Choosing the nearest factory preset is not the
  goal; factory presets are starting points (`docs/ROADMAP.md`). Every control includes the
  pedals, EQ and effects (user, 2026-10-21): see "Coverage" in section 5.
- **Searching the settings works, with the true DI** (`docs/knob-ceiling-results.md`).
  - The test used development data: 24 parts × 3 amps, from 14 bands.
  - It searched input gain (−20 to +12 dB), the amp's knobs and switches, both mics, and cab
    position and distance.
  - Scoring was cross-half.

  | Comparison | Gain |
  |---|---|
  | K_S vs S | **−0.253**, about 22% closer, 14/14 bands |
  | K_M vs M | −0.205, about 19% |
  | The amp's EQ on top of K_M | −0.079 more |
  | M vs S | only −0.091 |

  - **The gain fits each recording:** another part's settings score worse than M.
  - **One listener chose the searched settings on 7 of 7 blind trials.**
  - **K_M had −0.130 by render 25 of its 200.** K_S's curve is stored but not yet reported.
  - **These are lower bounds:** the search is known to fall short.
- **So the menu was the limit, not the plugin.** Every earlier "a perfect DI buys only 12–17%"
  figure came from ranking the menu.
- **The open question is the song side:** the product has no DI, only the song.
- **The render-order bug is fixed and audited** (`docs/render-order-audit.md`).
  - Reused Morgan renders carried the previous render at hiss level; `isolate=True` fixes it.
  - No past decision was overturned. On set 5, C′ had beaten C; that result is now fragile.

## 2. Two ways to get settings from a song, and why both are tested

The judge can only compare a recording with a render of the same notes, and a song gives no DI.
There are two ways around that.

**Route 1: rebuild the DI, then search.**

- **How it works:** C′ estimates the DI from the stem. Candidate settings are rendered on that
  estimate, so the real plugin is checked against the song at every step.
- **For it:** it keeps the judge's same-notes comparison, which the project's own evidence says
  is where the value is (next route).
- **Against it:**
  - its held-out evidence comes from menu ranking on isolated amp tracks, never stems;
  - the rebuilt DI sounds "dirty" and is unrelated to the true DI above 3 kHz;
  - on held-out crunch it only matched S (−0.023);
  - a search may amplify its errors.

**Route 2: predict the settings directly from the stem.**

- **How it works:** a model is trained on the project's own renders (many DIs × many settings),
  with no DI at all at song time.
- **For it:**
  - it needs no C′;
  - it can be trained in song conditions: mixed into real backings, mastered, and passed through
    the separator.
- **Against it:** the project tried this once, in the settings-regression proof of concept
  (`docs/preset-model-poc-results.md`: PR12 only, 18,843 renders, 14 players).
  - **The result:** −0.037 against a bar of about −0.105, carried by two parts.
  - **It hedged towards average settings:** its predictions spread only 0.44 as much as the
    labels.
  - **What it learned:** mic type (75% against 40%), cab placement, the pedal knobs and the
    filters. **On the EQ bands and input gain it did no better than the median.**
  - **Why: the player moves the sound about as much as the preset does.** One preset's sound
    moves 1.9 dB per band between players, while clean presets differ by 1.45 dB. From the stem
    alone, "bright player, dark preset" looks like "dark player, bright preset".
  - **Its follow-up points to the DI.** Choosing settings from a *perfectly known* long-term
    tone curve bought about 5%; choosing by the judge's same-notes comparison bought about 20%.
    In the project's words, "what carries the value is the judge's aligned, moment-by-moment
    comparison, which needs the take's DI".

**Why both are still tested.** Route 2 never had song-condition training, three amps, more
players, or an output that allows several right answers, and it is cheap to test.

- **Expect route 2 to trail route 1** unless that training adds what the proof of concept lacked.
- **If it doesn't trail, the rebuilt DI is not needed.**

Step 3 compares the two, and their combination, on the same cells against K_S.

### Step 3. The comparison: three ways to get settings from a song

**One declared plan, three arms, the same cells, the same scoring.**

**Shared by all arms:**

- **Cells:** knob ceiling's 24 parts × 3 amps, from 14 bands, both directions.
  - These parts were picked for clean stems (≥ 1 dB SNR against the amp track), so every result
    is **optimistic**; step 7 tests it on unfiltered parts.
- **Input:** the part's stem. The songs check's 10-s stems line up with the clip, so they can be
  cut into halves.
  - Known mismatches: these stems come from the instrumental mix with no vocals, and bank clips
    are separated without the product's 30-s context.
  - Report results with and without the songs in MUSDB18-train (the separator trained on them).
- **Output space:** knob ceiling's (`learn/knob_ceiling.py` `space(amp, start)`), on top of S for
  that amp:
  - **searched:** input gain (−20 to +12 dB, as in knob ceiling, so the comparison with K_S is
    fair), amp knobs and switches, both mics, cab position and distance, the right mic's level
    and switch;
  - **kept from S:** pedals, EQ, room mics, pan, phase and the amp. *(2026-10-21: a first
    stage only; the product must cover every control, see "Coverage" in section 5.)*
- **Score:**
  - render the arm's settings through the **measure DI**;
  - the judge (`flat`, with v3 beside it) scores the **held half**, band-weighted;
  - report each arm-vs-arm difference with a band-clustered 90% interval.
- **Comparators:**
  - S;
  - **K_S** (the same space and 150 renders, but with the true DI);
  - M;
  - the C′ menu pick (C′'s rebuilt DI choosing among the menu).
- **First, at zero renders:** read K_S's convergence at 25, 50 and 100 renders from knob
  ceiling's stored curves (each cell's `result.json`). It shows what to expect from short
  searches.

#### Arm A: rebuilt-DI search

- **Rebuild.**
  - C′, for the fold that never saw the part's band, rebuilds the DI from the whole stem clip
    (the network needs context).
  - **Which C′ fold:** the part's fold under step 3's fold rules (arm B's section). For a part
    in `k3_folds()`, that is its K3 fold. A set-3 part is outside `k3_folds()` and was never in
    C′'s training data (`learn/di_pool.py` reads only set-2 DIs and P1), so any fold would be
    leak-free. Use the fold that fold rule 2 draws for its band, so arms A, B and C read each
    part with the same fold. Assert in code that the part's DI is not in C′'s training pool.
  - Use the pattern in `learn/residual_eval.py` `rebuild`: `learn/direc.py` `rebuild` on the CPU,
    then `learn/direc_check.py` `to_lufs` to −22.9 LUFS, with **no 3-kHz low-pass**.
  - **Do not use** `songs_check.build_di`: it loads the old networks and applies the retired
    low-pass.
- **Search.**
  - `knob_search` from S, 150 renders per direction.
  - Its objective is `learn/rebuilt_judge.py` `rebuilt_distance` (lag −52) between the stem and
    the render through the rebuilt DI, on the **search half only**.
  - `run_cell` hard-wires the true-DI judge, so this needs a variant with the objective swapped.
  - Count how often `rebuilt_distance`'s fallback (using the recording as the activity mask)
    fires.
- **Known-target control.**
  - **Cells:** 12 (6 parts C′ never trained on × PR12 and SW50R), drawn by a declared seed.
  - **The "recording":** S with five settings moved, played through the part's own DI (knob
    ceiling's "realistic control" format).
  - **Two runs** of `knob_search` at 150 renders: one scored with the measure DI, one with C′'s
    rebuild of that recording.
  - **Measures** on the held half: gap closed; shortfall = log(d_found / d_target).

  Read in order; the first match wins:

  | If | Then |
  |---|---|
  | The measure-DI run closes ≥ 50% of the gap on fewer than 8 of 12 | The search set-up is wrong: stop arms A and C (step 6, row 2) |
  | The rebuilt run closes ≥ 50% on ≥ 8 of 12, or its mean shortfall is within 0.03 of the measure-DI run's | The control passes |
  | Otherwise | The rebuilt DI fails even on known targets: arms A and C count as failed |

- **Cost.** 21.6k search + 3.6k control + 2.7k for the C′ menu pick ≈ 28k renders.

#### Arm B: direct settings model

- **The model.** Start from the proof of concept's network body and per-fold loop
  (`learn/train.py` `build_model`, `fit`) and its prediction code (`learn/evaluate.py` `predict`,
  `shuffled_sources`), with six changes:
  1. **keep input level:** its `Frontend` normalised every crop's loudness, so replace it, and add
     LUFS as a side input;
  2. compute features on the CPU and cache them before training;
  3. labels in knob ceiling's space for all three amps (its `learn/pr12.py` was PR12-only);
  4. outputs: one head per categorical setting (mics, switches), and a **mixture-density head**
     for the continuous ones. That head predicts several possible settings with weights, so
     equally good answers are not averaged into a bland middle. To output one preset, take the
     heaviest component's mean and each categorical's top choice;
  5. the amp and the class start (one of the six S) as inputs;
  6. two inputs: the stem, and the mix with how well the two agree.
- **The training bank.**
  - **Renders:** use `learn/render_job.py`'s worker pattern, but with `isolate=True` (it doesn't
    isolate today) and sampling in knob ceiling's space instead of `pr12.py`. Use 10-s clips at
    48 kHz; htdemucs runs at 44.1 kHz, so resample around it.
  - **DIs:**
    - every classed development part (82);
    - Guitar-TECHS P1 and P2 (P3 is held out and never read);
    - Five-guitar, once its DI output is checked (decision 6).

    Extend `learn/di_pool.py`, which today reads only set-2 DIs and P1. Every DI gets the
    measure-DI treatment (average-guitar equalisation, −22.9 LUFS), so a label's input gain
    means what K_S's does.
    - `confirm_run.build_di` works on crop folders, so pool DIs need an array version of the same
      steps.
  - **Settings:** random within the output space, on the six S. Half are broad; half are jittered
    around the six S and around other folds' K_S optima. K_M's optima sit on M's pedals and EQ,
    so they are not used.
  - **Labels:** the settings that made each render, so no judge is needed in training. Score the
    model on sound, never on knob error.
  - **Augmentation after rendering** (no extra renders):
    - mix into other folds' `backing_instrumental.wav` (each development crop has one), at the
      real guitar-to-backing level, measured first from the development mixes;
    - sometimes add a second guitar;
    - bus compression, limiting and codec damage;
    - htdemucs_6s (step 0's script) on about half the mixtures.
  - **Size:** a pilot of about 10k renders and about 5k separations (≈ 7 h CPU).
- **Folds (declared and asserted in code),** so no part's own DI trains the model that reads it:
  1. Bands in `train.k3_folds()` keep their fold 0–3. Then arm B's fold f and C′'s fold f leave
     out the same bands, which arm C needs.
  2. Every other development band that gives a DI, a backing or a label (set 3, and the sets 1–2
     bands outside that scheme) gets a fold 0–3 by a declared draw: sorted names, a new seed,
     i mod 4. The band → fold map is committed before any training.
  3. Only sources that are never evaluated may train every fold: Guitar-TECHS P1–P2, and
     Five-guitar.
  4. The fold-f model trains on no render, backing or K_S optimum from a fold-f band.
  5. Assert that every evaluated part's band is in folds 0–3.
- **Training.** The proof of concept's "about 10 hours for 30 epochs" was all four folds, about
  5 min per epoch per fold at 1.3 s per batch of 64.
  - The pilot is about 0.7–2.6 h per fold, 3–10 h for four.
  - Time fold 0's first epoch before declaring.
  - Use MPS only when step 1 isn't running.
- **Positive control on renders** (before anything real):
  - **Targets:** per fold, ≥ 30 planted settings on an S, rendered through held-apart DIs, then
    mixed, mastered and separated. The model reads the separated version.
  - **Scoring:** render the prediction, the planted settings and the median of that fold's
    training labels, each through the *measure* DI. Judge each against the clean planted render's
    held half.
    - d_target is the planted settings' score. It is not zero, because the planted render used
      the raw DI.
    - d_start is S's score.
  - **Pass:** gap closed ≥ 50% on ≥ 2/3 of targets, and closer than the median settings on ≥ 2/3.
  - **If it fails:** fix and re-run, at most twice.
  - **Cost:** about 0.5k renders.
- **Controls and diagnostics** (144 renders each unless stated):
  - **shuffled input:** another band's stem, 144–432 renders. Arm B passes this when it beats the
    shuffled input by ≥ 0.05, band-weighted, in ≥ 60% of bands;
  - **a song-blind constant:** the median of other folds' K_S optima per amp and class. Knob
    ceiling found a common pull (less drive, less bass, darker) that a model could learn without
    listening;
  - **amp-track input** instead of the stem, which separates the separator's loss from the
    model's;
  - **prediction spread against label spread** (the proof of concept's was 0.44).
- **Amp choice for a product-like single answer** (declared): either the amp of the class start
  `generate` would use, or a small extra head trained to predict which amp's K_S distance is
  lowest (labels from other folds). Per-amp results are reported anyway.
- **Cost.** 10k bank + 0.5k positive control + 0.6–0.9k scoring and diagnostics ≈ 11k renders.

#### Arm C: combined

- **What.**
  - Arm B's prediction becomes the start of arm A's search: `knob_search(budget=50)` per
    direction, with arm A's objective.
  - If arm C leads at the readout, a separate `budget=150` run follows. `knob_search` splits its
    budget into fixed stages, so it can't be resumed.
- **Cost.** 24 × 3 × 2 × 50 = 7.2k renders.

#### Pass rule for each arm (*proposed*)

On step 3's cells, held half, band-weighted:

1. better than S by at least 0.03, **and** at least 40% of K_S's gain on the same cells (about
   −0.10);
2. better than S in ≥ 60% of bands;
3. neither clean nor crunch worse than S by more than 0.05. Crunch has only 5 bands, so this is
   a harm guard, not a test;
4. arm A only: not worse than the C′ menu pick by more than 0.01;
5. arm B only: its positive control and its shuffled-input control both pass.

The standard error at 14 bands is about 0.026, so show the 90% interval beside every figure.

  - `knob_search`;
  - CMA-ES on the continuous settings, adapting the textbook code in `match/search.py` `refine`
    (the project avoids the `cma` package; `refine` itself is tied to `match`'s evaluator);
  - random search, as a floor;
  - TPE, with Optuna (decision 6).

  Every arm handles the mics and switches with `knob_search`'s staged alternative passes, so only
  the continuous search differs. Random search gets two seeds; `knob_search`, CMA-ES and TPE
  one each: 5 runs per cell. Each cell is one known target, so one run per seed (no directions).
- **Then:** the winner (one seed) and `knob_search` each also run at 300 renders.
- **Pass rule (*proposed*):** replace `knob_search` only if the new optimiser lands ≥ 0.03 closer
  on average and wins ≥ 8 of 12 cells at 150 renders.
- **Cost.** 12 cells × 150 × 5 runs = 9k, plus 12 × 300 × 2 = 7.2k at 300 ≈ 16k renders.

### Step 6. What follows step 3

Read in order; the first match wins.

| # | Result | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Arm B's positive control still fails after two re-runs | Arm B's pipeline is wrong | Read arms A and C on their own rules only |
| 2 | Arm A's control says the search set-up is wrong | The optimiser or the search space is wrong | Arms A and C count as failed; read arm B alone |
| 3 | At least one arm passes | The **leader** is the passing arm with the best band-weighted mean against S. A passing arm whose paired 90% interval against the leader includes 0 is tied; among tied arms, the cheapest per cell leads (B: 1 render, C: 51, A: 150) | **C leads:** run its 150-render variant (+21.6k), then hand "model guess + short search" to the product session. **B leads:** the rebuilt DI is not needed. Grow arm B's bank to about 30k (+20k); optionally fine-tune it on true-DI search labels for about 40 more parts (≈ 12k renders, reusing step 4's menus; never the evaluated parts). Then step 7. **A leads:** hand route 1 to the product session; arm B stays research |
| 4 | No arm passes | The song side is the loss | Re-run arm A from the amp track instead of the stem, on each part's best-amp cell only (chosen once per part: the amp where K_S's held-half score, averaged over both directions, is lowest): 24 × 2 × 150 ≈ 7.2k. If that passes, separation is the loss; if not, the rebuild is. Then one route at a time, each with its own stopping rule (decision 2): improve the DI, or train a learned DI-free score |

## 5. Rules for every step

- **Process:** plan, review, message the product session, run, audit. Changes are dated
  amendments.
- **Renders:**
  - every Morgan render uses `isolate=True`;
  - trim the first 0.25 s of any isolated render used as audio (the fade-in);
  - reuse processes; never touch the licence daemon.
  - *(2026-10-15: the earlier "nothing runs 03:00–04:15" rule is dropped. It was a one-night
    request from the ML session on 2026-10-10, keeping the product session's renders clear of
    an evaluation; nothing on the Mac is scheduled then.)*
- **Audio:** the renderer and the measure DI run at 48 kHz. Resample only around models that
  need 44.1 kHz.
- **MPS:**
  - compute spectral losses and features on the CPU (`torch.stft`'s gradient is wrong on MPS above
    65,536 samples);
  - one MPS job at a time;
  - clip gradients by hand.
- **Disk:**
  - keep 30 GB free;
  - keep distances and short 16-bit FLACs, not WAVs;
  - arm B's pilot needs about 6–11 GB with features, so it renders after step 1's caches are
    deleted.
- **Statistics:** the band is the unit. Report band-weighted results with intervals, and
  leave-one-band-out checks.
- **Held-out data** is used only in step 7, declared first. Nobody listens to sets 5 or 6 before
  that.
- **Licences and cost:** the project is non-commercial and open source (user, 2026-10-20), so
  anything open, under a non-commercial licence (including no-derivatives variants), with no
  licence stated, or gated behind an access request (e.g. a Zenodo or Hugging Face form) is fine
  (user: unlicensed weights 2026-10-19; everything else 2026-10-20). Unclear or conflicting
  licence terms go to the user to check. Nothing may force other users to pay: beyond the Neural
  DSP plugin it targets and Claude Code, which it runs in, the product must not need a paid tool
  or service. Free tools and services, cloud APIs included, are fine. In development, tools the
  user pays for personally (e.g. Moises) are fine; any other paid tool or service needs the
  user's yes. The user checked and accepted SAM-Audio, the Stable Audio Open VAE and
  X-LANCE/BS-RoFormer SW (2026-10-20). Moises is never used for training (pseudo-labels
  included) and is not part of any workflow, testing included: no plan may depend on it. It is
  used only when the user decides to on a given occasion; uploading Cambridge-MT or Telefunken
  audio to it is then allowed. Models trained on Cambridge-MT or Telefunken audio may ship.
- **Separators: adapt, don't build; no rented machines (user, 2026-10-20, revised the same
  day).** Fine-tuning or adapting an existing released separator (e.g. a class fine-tune of
  Mega-53, per-song solo-region adaptation, a training-free NMF from a solo region) and feature
  extraction are fine. Designing and training a new separator (e.g. a score-informed model
  trained from scratch, a new conditioned one-guitar separator) is not, nor is any major
  modelling and training effort. No rented machines (e.g. rented GPUs or cloud servers) for
  anything, so all training, fine-tunes included, runs on this Mac (MPS gradients are wrong
  as-is: loss off the device after a gradient gate, or the CPU). The free cloud APIs above stay
  allowed.
- **Training time (user, 2026-10-20; all models, separators and others).** Training that takes
  minutes is fine. Any run longer than that (e.g. a C′ fold of about 6.5 h) needs the user's
  approval before it starts. No week-long efforts.
- **Coverage (user, 2026-10-21).** The generated preset must cover every setting the plugin
  exposes (the pack manifests, Morgan and Tone King), including the pedals (compressor, both
  drives), the EQ, the time effects (doubler and spring included), the noise gate, the room
  mics, pan, phase, output gain, the amp choice and every other manifest control, not only
  input gain, the amp's knobs and the mics. Exceptions come from the manifests, not from this
  rule (e.g. the room mic types have no control in the plugin's UI).
  - The knob values of a switched-off feature all count as one setting (an off pedal at level
    min or max is the same setting), so training losses and knob scores must not count them
    as different. How (leaving them out, as `learn/song_relabel.py` does for the right mic, or
    setting them to neutral values, as `learn/knob_ceiling2.py` does for off pedals) is for
    the ML session to propose.
  - Plans already declared (part C's E, the direct model's step 3) keep their definitions
    until amended. The direct model's amendment 5 (2026-10-22) leaves the right cab's knobs out

  - This replaces the 2026-10-17 practice of keeping high-bleed parts flagged, with a

## 7. What not to do

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Rank menu presets as the answer | The menu was the limit (knob ceiling) |
| Predict exact DAC codes | Phase-locked; it failed |
| Re-run the old contrastive encoder recipe (solo renders, 14 players, no separator) | It failed on real recordings |

## 8. Known risks

- **The evidence so far is narrow:** development data with the true DI, one take per part, one
  listener. The song side is untested.
- **C′ has never run on a separated stem,** and the project's own evidence expects route 2 to
  trail route 1.
- **Crunch has only 5 bands** in step 3: treat crunch results as descriptive.
- **Today's starts were chosen on 24 of the 25 knob-ceiling parts,** which makes gains against S
  look smaller.
- **The judge likes spectral extremes:** 93% of K_M searches ended with a setting at a range
  limit. Listening is the guard, and true-DI search labels used for fine-tuning are partly stuck
  at those limits.
- **The Mac's disk was not seen from here.** Each step checks its files exist first.
- **Dirty data** (the [data-quality audit](../data-quality-audit.md), 2026-10-22): bleed in the live-room amp
  and mic tracks, and doubles of the same riff left in the training backings, which the stored
  leak check misses. The DIs are clean and the labels exact. Hence the trusted-data rule (§5).
