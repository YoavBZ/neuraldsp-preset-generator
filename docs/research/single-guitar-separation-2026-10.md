# Split guitars by pan before training anything

*Research report, 2026-10-19, for the ML session's request on extracting one target guitar from multi-guitar mixes. Research, not a declared plan: any run needs its own plan, review and ledger entry.*

Tags: **[V]** a researcher opened the source and read the claim, or it is in this repo; **[I]** inference; **[U]** search snippet or third-party claim. "Project SNR" is the repo's gain-fitted SNR (`snr_db` in [learn/stereo_mix_check.py](../../learn/stereo_mix_check.py)). It fits the gain on the estimate, so **SNR_lin = 1 + SI-SDR_lin** [V code, I algebra]. Literature SDR, SI-SDR and plain SNR are different units, and this report converts only SI-SDR exactly.

**No open, ungated, verified model separates lead from rhythm, or one electric guitar from another.** Every released prompted separator (Banquet, AudioSep, CLAPSep) selects by instrument class or timbre. The one strong prompted model that takes a time-span hint, Meta's SAM-Audio, is gated and so excluded. The 1.4 dB the team sees on multi-guitar parts is therefore the expected behaviour of any open guitar separator, not a fault in how Mega-53 is run. The best purpose-built research model for two same-timbre guitars gets its weaker guitar to about **1.6 dB in project-SNR units**, the same place Mega-53 already sits. The realistic open routes are pipelines and per-song adaptation built from existing weights, and all are untested on guitars. Ranked by expected gain per effort, they are: (1) a **pan split of Mega-53's stereo stem**, steered by the arrangement's `pan` field or the user's ear (the repo already measured **+1.55 dB** for the target's own channel at ±0.6 pan, and a blind side guess is a coin flip); (2) a **level-difference (ILD) soft mask** on that stem for partly panned guitars; (3) **centre/wide stereo models** for a centred lead against wide rhythms; (4) Mega-53's existing **electric/acoustic heads** when the other guitar is acoustic; (5) **solo-region test-time adaptation** using the arrangement's sections; (6) a **fine-tune on synthetic multi-guitar mixes** built from the team's Cambridge multitracks, which needs a rented GPU. Then come Banquet, text queries and score-informed separation. The cheapest first step is a zero-render pan screen on stage F under declared pan scenarios, scored against the repo's +2 dB gate. Everything below is a proposal: the project requires a plan, a review and a ledger entry before any run.

## Every open separator returns all the guitars together

The inventory is now close to exhaustive for open weights. ZFTurbo's MSST pretrained list has **no** BS-RoFormer, Mel-RoFormer, SCNet or MDX23C guitar checkpoint. Its only guitar-capable entry is htdemucs_6s, listed with no guitar SDR ([pretrained_models.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/docs/pretrained_models.md)); Mega-53 is published only as a release asset [V]. Mega-53 has `guitar`, `electric-guitar` and `acoustic-guitar` stems, but **no lead or rhythm stem**, no weights licence and no training-data statement ([release v1.0.21](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/releases/tag/v1.0.21)) [V]. UVR's official download list has **zero** guitar entries among 54 checkpoints ([download_checks.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TRvlvr/application_data/main/filelists/download_checks.json)) [V]. A Hugging Face search on 2026-10-19 for "guitar", "lead_guitar" and "rhythm" found only becruily's all-guitar Mel-RoFormer and two small acoustic-only fine-tunes ([HF search](https://huggingface.co/api/models?search=guitar&limit=100)) [V]. The full repo lists of the main community authors (Gabox, unwa, anvuew, Sucial, becruily) contain no lead/rhythm, electric/acoustic or multi-guitar model ([becruily](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=becruily), [pcunwa](https://huggingface.co/api/models?author=pcunwa)) [V]. MVSEP may offer a lead/rhythm model: a search snippet lists one ([MVSep](https://mvsep.com/en)) [U], but the current algorithm pages do not ([MVSEP algorithms](https://mvsep.com/en/algorithms)) [V]. Either way it is a service with no weights, like Moises.

The research literature explains why. The data the field trains on has no role labels. MoisesDB's guitar taxonomy is acoustic, clean electric and distorted electric only ([MoisesDB](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.15913)) [V]. Banquet, the only query-based music separator with released weights, therefore learns classes. Its authors found that a query from the same song or from another song made under 0.2 dB of difference in the coarse-stem setup, and that for fine stems it did not affect most stems. They say the model may be "insensitive to fine differences in timbre" ([Banquet, arXiv 2406.18747](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18747)) [V]. MERL's hierarchical separator stops at "clean guitars" and leaves "multiple instances of the same type" to future work ([Manilow et al., ISMIR 2020](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2020/paper/000105.pdf)) [V]. Even a 2026 CLAP-conditioned study lists picking one of several same-category instruments as future work ([Point-and-Play](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19445)) [V].

The one direct test of a dedicated model is sobering. **GuitarDuets** (ISMIR 2024) trained a permutation-invariant HT Demucs on real and synthetic classical guitar duets. On 7 real test duets, the stronger guitar reached **4.95 dB SDR / 3.57 dB SI-SDR** and the weaker one **1.01 dB SDR / −3.54 dB SI-SDR**, and the authors say the second guitar is often treated "as background noise" ([arXiv 2507.01172](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01172v1)) [V]. In project units those SI-SDRs are about **5.2 dB and 1.6 dB** [I]. So a purpose-built model's weaker guitar lands where Mega-53 already sits on multi-guitar parts (1.45 dB). Its stronger guitar would clear the +2 dB gate, but on classical duets, not band mixes [I]. Ground-truth note conditioning lifted the second guitar to 4.86 dB SDR on synthetic data, while estimated notes barely helped (−3.29 dB SI-SDR on real duets) [V].

The stakes for the project are clear. On fresh full mixes, perfect target separation moves arm A from **−0.028 to −0.094** against S. The remaining prize is about −0.049 over Mega-53, and **19 of stage F's 21 parts** sit with another electric guitar ([arm-a-mega53-results](../arm-a-mega53-results.md), [separation-finetune-plan](../separation-finetune-plan.md)) [V]. A 21-part arm A test resolves about −0.032, which is why a new stem needs about **+2 dB project SNR over Mega-53** before a render test is worth running [V]. Near the bottom of the scale that is a large step: 1.45 → 3.45 dB SNR is **−4.0 → +0.8 dB SI-SDR** [I].

### Why pan ranks first

Pan is the only hint that carries **instance** information and costs nothing to apply. Double-tracking means two takes panned hard left and right ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-double-track-guitars/)) [V]. Once Mega-53 has removed the non-guitar content, a per-bin level mask faces mostly guitar-against-guitar, so its usual failure (everything else on the same side) largely disappears [I]. The repo's own numbers back this up. With the target at −0.6 and the other guitars alternating ±0.6, keeping the target's channel gained **+1.55 dB** for Mega-53, with gains up to **+5.0 dB** on magilla. Mid gained +0.24 and side +0.35, and blind RMS weights gained +0.27 ([stereo check](../stereo-and-mix-check-results.md)) [V]. The pan law is a √2-scaled sine/cosine law, so at ±0.6 the opposite guitar still leaks into the target's channel at about **−9.8 dB** ([stereo_mix_check.py](../../learn/stereo_mix_check.py)) [V code, I arithmetic]. That is why +1.55 dB is probably a lower bound for hard-panned parts, and why an ILD mask should help at partial pans [I].

The weakness is the hint itself. The stem's own L/R balance pointed to the target's side on only **12 of 21** parts. The "most energy" rule made multi-guitar parts *worse* (1.16 dB against 1.45) because "the loudest side is usually another guitar" ([upgrade results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. The arrangement file supplies `pan` (`L`/`R`/`C`/null) and `doubled` per guitar, but it is web-sourced and marked with confidences ([reference/arrangement.md](../../reference/arrangement.md)) [V]. A documented side, or the user's ear on three short clips, is the realistic query [I]. `doubled: true` warns that the two channels hold two takes of the same part. Keeping one side then drops the double, which suits tone matching but changes the sound relative to the record [I].

Centre/wide models complement the channel pick for the classic lead-centre, rhythms-wide layout, where mid/side and a channel pick do nothing. They were trained to split mixes by pan, with "center" meaning "lead vocals, bass, kick, snare" ([gilliaan card](https://huggingface.co/gilliaan/CenterWide-Models)) [V]. Applying them to a guitar-only stem is out of domain, and a plain mid-minus-correlated-side DSP mask is the licence-free baseline they must beat [I]. Note one inherited flaw: any pan route after Mega-53 keeps Mega-53's missing top octave (−15 dB at 8 kHz, −36 dB at 12.7 kHz, against −6 and −18 for htdemucs) ([upgrade results](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. The LSD guard therefore stays essential.

### Why per-song adaptation ranks above training

Test-time adaptation uses the one cue that is specific to *this* guitar in *this* song: where it plays without the others. Özer and Müller fine-tuned a pretrained separator on random mixtures of one recording's piano-only and orchestra-only passages. Piano SDR rose from 4.60 to **8.95 dB** on a historical recording, with most of that recording's gain in the first ~10 iterations (on the others it came mostly after the 10th), and gains were largest where the recording was unlike the training data ([ISMIR 2022](https://archives.ismir.net/ismir2022/paper/000059.pdf)) [V]. Cantisani et al. fine-tuned on one song using a user's activity timeline, with a loss that silences sources marked inactive ([WASPAA 2021](https://hal.telecom-paris.fr/hal-03219350v3/file/UGOSA_Hal.pdf)) [V]. That loss maps directly onto the arrangement's per-section `guitars` map [I]. Few-shot separation shows the same direction: examples from the same track beat other-track examples by **0.4–1.1 dB**, and examples polluted by other sources hurt badly ([Wang et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.01273)) [V].

Three caveats keep this at rank 5. First, it needs regions where the target guitar plays without the other guitars, and stage F has not yet measured how often they exist (the planned X measure is pending) [V]. Second, in a full mix those regions still contain drums, bass and vocals, so the "clean" target comes from Mega-53's stem, a noisy pseudo-label [I]. Third, MPS training is broken in this repo: on MPS the head gradient had cosine **0.001** with the CPU's and the trunk gradient was NaN, attributed to the MPS STFT above 65,536 samples ([separation-finetune-plan](../separation-finetune-plan.md)) [V]. Adaptation therefore runs on the CPU (11.2 s per forward/backward on a 3-s chunk for the pruned model with all its weights trainable; a head-only pass was not measured), or on MPS only after moving the STFT and spectral losses to the CPU and re-checking gradients against the CPU [V measure; I fix]. A small mask network trained on the stem's CPU-computed STFT would be far cheaper than adapting Mega-53 itself [I]. NMF templates learned from the solo region are a training-free alternative, but they should discriminate only when the two tones differ [I].

### Why training ranks low despite being the real fix

A fine-tune is the only route that could match Moises without hints. The tooling exists: MSST trains arbitrary stem names, offers LoRA and `freeze_layers`, and has same-type `mixup`, loudness, polarity, channel-shuffle and pedalboard distortion augmentation ([augmentations.md](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/docs/augmentations.md), [LoRA.md](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/docs/LoRA.md)) [V]. MSST has no PIT loss, so two interchangeable rhythms need either a custom loss or a pan-ordered target (left-most first) [I]. MedleyVox is the most complete recipe to copy, with public code: PIT plus mixture consistency, deliberate same-song/different-singer mixes, and a "main vs rest" task that maps onto "target guitar vs other guitars" ([MedleyVox](https://github.com/jeonchangbin49/MedleyVox), [arXiv 2211.07302](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07302)) [V]. The cost is the blocker. MSST's `train.py` picks CUDA or the CPU, with no MPS branch ([model_utils.py](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/utils/model_utils.py)) [V]. At 11.2 s per step, 10,000 CPU steps take about 31 h, while Banquet trained for up to ~300k steps on an H100 ([Banquet](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18747v2)) [V numbers; I arithmetic]. GuitarDuets' 1.6 dB weaker-guitar result also warns that the payoff for double-tracked rhythms may be small [I].

The practical reading is that role should be used as metadata that *chooses* a pan, a region or an output, not as a query [I]. SAM-Audio's span prompt is weak alone for music (overall subjective 2.57 against 4.09 for text on music; 2.20 against 3.78 on instruments in the wild), and its benchmark filtered out "multiple visually indistinguishable instruments" ([arXiv 2512.18099](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18099v1)) [V]. So even the excluded best-in-class prompted model has not been shown to separate same-class guitars.

## On this Mac, inference is CPU-bound and training does not work on MPS

The repo's measurements are the best data available. On stage F, the pruned Mega-53 head took a median **89.8 s per 30-s context** on 4 CPU threads (peak 7.2 GB), against 14.1 s for htdemucs_6s on 2 threads ([arm-a-mega53-results](../arm-a-mega53-results.md)) [V]. MPS inference is correct (116 dB and 81 dB SNR against the CPU) but only **1.2–1.6× faster**. The bigger lever is the "fast setting" (one 10-s chunk, overlap 1): **5.2 s per 10 s on MPS** and 7.6 s on the CPU, at 25 dB SNR against the stored setting's output ([direct-model-stems-plan §1c](../direct-model-stems-plan.md)) [V]. The stored setting uses 20-s chunks with `num_overlap` 2, and the pruned stem is bit-identical to the full 53-stem model's ([separator-upgrade-plan](../separator-upgrade-plan.md)) [V]. Run one MPS job at a time. While a separation runs, allow at most 2 render shards ([arm-a-mega53-plan](../arm-a-mega53-plan.md)) [V].

Outside the repo, the picture is thin. MSST's BS-RoFormer runs its STFT on the CPU when the device is MPS ([bs_roformer.py](https://github.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/blob/main/models/bs_roformer/bs_roformer.py)) [V]. One RoFormer inference package dropped MPS and MLX outright in September 2026 ([bs-roformer-infer](https://github.com/openmirlab/bs-roformer-infer)) [V]. MLX and Core ML ports exist only for small 4-stem or vocal models, and none is guitar-capable ([mini-bs-roformer MLX](https://huggingface.co/schism-audio/mini-bs-roformer-18m-mlx)) [U]. For metrics, report project SNR with SI-SDR beside it, plus LSD and 3–10 kHz brightness as must-not-regress. When comparing with papers, convert only SI-SDR (project SNR 1.45 dB ≈ −4.0 dB SI-SDR; 9.96 dB ≈ +9.5 dB). Treat museval SDR and Banquet's plain SNR as different units. GuitarDuets' authors found SDR and SI-SDR read higher on guitar–guitar than guitar–piano mixtures, and say timbral similarity makes the metrics hard to trust ([arXiv 2507.01172](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.01172v1)) [V].

## A zero-render pan screen on stage F is the cheapest first experiment

**Proposal [I]; it needs a plan, an independent review and a ledger entry first.** It could be an amendment to separation-finetune Step 0, reusing its declared measures and statistics ([separation-finetune-plan](../separation-finetune-plan.md)) [V], but it adds new separations (about 2.4 h of CPU), which Step 0 excludes. It is a further development reuse of stage F; the ledger already reserves its 7th and 8th uses for that plan's Steps 1 and 3. Stage F suits it: every context is a unity sum of raw tracks (residual ≤ 2.4e-7), and every target has its own amp track, so any pan scenario can be rebuilt and scored with zero renders ([stereo check](../stereo-and-mix-check-results.md)) [V]. Its limits must be stated up front: all 21 targets are mono and centred, and the mixes have no bus processing or master. Pan scenarios must therefore be synthesised. For the same reason, the declared Step 3 arm A test, which runs on stage F as recorded, cannot test pan. Results will also flatter pan methods compared with real mastered mixes that carry stereo reverb, delay and chorus [I].

The readout answers four questions [I]. HP and P show whether a known side clears +2 dB. R and SP show the null and the failure mode. (f) against (a) prices the user's answer. G+mask against YT shows whether a class-perfect stem plus pan would be enough, or whether a conditioned network is still needed. As a descriptive cross-check on real mastered stereo (not ground truth, and not training data), the pipeline's output on a few of the user's songs could be compared by ear with the Moises lead/rhythm stems the user already has [I].

**Second, conditional experiment: solo-region adaptation [I].** Run it only if Step 0's track table shows enough parts with usable regions, meaning at least ~5–10 s where the other guitars sit ≥10 dB below the target and some time where they play without it (the X measure). For each eligible part, take Mega-53's stem from those regions, build random target-plus-others mixtures, adapt for 100 steps (in the piano study, gains on all but the hardest recording came mostly after the 10th iteration), and score on the 10-s crop. **The crop must lie outside the adaptation regions**, or the test leaks. Run two arms. The oracle arm uses true tracks for the regions and sets the ceiling. The realistic arm uses Mega-53's stems of those regions as pseudo-targets, standing in for user-marked sections. Compare a small CPU mask network against a CPU fine-tune of Mega-53's pruned head. The cost is roughly 20 min of CPU per part for 100 steps of the pruned model, or minutes for a small mask net [I from the 11.2 s measure]. The same +2 dB gate applies.

## Conclusion

The gap between Moises and the open world is not a missing architecture; it is missing **labels**. Every open model was trained on data that names instrument classes, so every open model answers a class question. That changes what the project should buy first. The cheapest instance-level signal, stereo position, is already in commercial mixes and already partly in the arrangement file. The repo's own measurement suggests a known side is worth about as much as the best separator upgrade so far. The real uncertainty is how often a side is known and correct, and how much stereo effects on mastered records erode it. Stage F can bound the first question, but not the second.

If the pan screen falls short of +2 dB, the next move is not a bigger network. It is to obtain role labels: per-song adaptation borrows them from the arrangement's sections, and a fine-tune borrows them from the team's multitrack filenames. GuitarDuets suggests a ceiling for double-tracked rhythms near today's 1.5 dB. So the product should probably treat "two takes of the same part" as one target, as `doubled: true` already implies, and spend separation effort on lead-versus-rhythm, where register and role give a model something to learn [I].
