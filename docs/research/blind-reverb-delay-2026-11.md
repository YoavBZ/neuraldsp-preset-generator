# Nothing proven recovers guitar reverb or delay

*Research, 2026-11-03, for the ML session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

Nothing proven exists. No tool, model or paper recovers a guitar's reverb (mix, decay) or delay (time, feedback, mix) blind from strummed chords in a real mix or a separated stem, and no published method reports a feedback-specific error (SunAFXiNet estimates feedback on solo guitar, pooled only). So there is no wheel to adopt, and nothing found would replace the masking renders F50 deferred until this survey. Published results stop at single notes in synthetic mixes, isolated tracks and whole-mix reverb. What exists are parts: an echo-time method never tried under a backing, encoders whose probes hear on/off but read reverb and delay knobs about equally poorly, and an on/off detector. Five of seven shortlisted items need a download (the ML session's and the user's call), and since only the clean targets are stored as audio, most also need the mixes rebuilt and the stems re-separated. The first test needs neither: a long-window cepstrum on the stored targets, then E0's 84 stored stems, as an arm beside E1c.

## Published evidence stops at single notes, solo tracks and whole-mix reverb

**Proven on real mixtures, for retrieval or whole-mix reverb only.** Fx-Encoder++ (Sony, ISMIR 2025) pulls one instrument's effect embedding out of a MUSDB18 mix with synthetic effects added. Its top-1 retrieval of the right setup among 500 falls from **19.9% on isolated stems to 7.1% on separated stems and 3.0% from the mix**, with no guitar and no per-effect figure; in the same table AFx-Rep falls from 14.0 to 4.6 after separation ([Fx-Encoder++](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02273v1)). A linear probe on frozen LAION-CLAP reads reverb time on MUSDB18-HQ mixes at **R² 0.77 (MAE 0.21 s)**, but one impulse response was applied to the whole mix ([arXiv 2607.03806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03806)). A guitar-only reverb is a far smaller share of a mix, so that is a ceiling, not a forecast.

**Proven only on isolated or synthetic signals.** Hinrichs et al. regress slapback time (MAE 0.038 on a 0–1 scale) and mix (0.027), and reverb room size (0.041) and mix (0.034), but the guitar holds **one note for 2 s** under virtual keys, bass and drums. Errors grow **2–3× from the quietest to the loudest backing**, feedback is never regressed, and a network trained on clean guitar fell below 40% accuracy once a kick drum alone was added ([Hinrichs 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)). RemFX detects reverb at 0.79–0.85 accuracy and delay at 0.68–0.74 on solo sources ([RemFX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16177)). A deep T60 estimator erred by **126% on music against 54% on speech**; one possible explanation its authors give is that notes "do not stop sharply" ([Callens & Cernak 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11167)), consistent with the RT60 rule's failure on chords (F48 in `docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`). Halmrast's cepstrum is demonstrated, not proven: a 134–137 ms slapback on Elvis's vocal, in unaccompanied passages, with no ground truth or statistics ([Halmrast 2019](https://www.arpjournal.com/asarpwp/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Tor-Halmrast_ARP2019.pdf)).

**Paper only, no code.** Sarroff & Michaels infer a reverb plugin's 21 settings and mix ratio from wet speech. They matched a human expert on synthetic examples but did significantly worse on natural ones, and warn that mix "might be easily confused with reverberation time" ([Sarroff & Michaels 2020](https://dafx2020.mdw.ac.at/proceedings/papers/DAFx2020_paper_5.pdf)). SunAFXiNet estimates chains of distortion, delay, chorus and reverb on solo guitar, delay feedback among the controls, and recovers the dry signal, but reports only a pooled parameter MSE of 0.034 ([DAFx24](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2024/papers/DAFx24_paper_53.pdf)). Hinrichs's code was promised but never found.

Two patterns follow. The project's loss looks typical, not a pipeline bug (inference, since it compares an AUC margin with a retrieval share): on the strong third the stem keeps 38–43% of the clean target's above-chance AUC margin (F49: 0.614 against 0.767 for reverb, 0.801 for delay), and separated stems keep 36% of Fx-Encoder++'s top-1 retrieval. And encoders do not split by effect: RLAT's probes on MUSDB stems read reverb knobs at MAE 21.2–24.1 (×100) and delay knobs at 20.8–24.0 for every encoder, about a constant mid-range guess on a uniform 0–1 knob (25; inference). Detection F1 is higher for delay than reverb in every encoder (AFx-Rep 73.6 against 53.5, Microsoft's CLAP 69.8 against 37.1, Fx-Encoder++ 68.1 against 45.9) ([RLAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28127)), as in the project's probe. Delay time is readable on the clean target by a training-free picker (43.5% within ±10% of wide delay-ons, F49) and by a learned lag head alike (46.0%, F50), and by neither on the stem (13.7%, 10.7%) or the mix. A cepstrum is worth trying because it is training-free and works on audio at a finer resolution, not because learning failed.

## Four untested ideas, and routes that need a ruling or a dry track

Four ideas in the notes are the researchers' own and **untested**. First, a cepstral readout of feedback and mix. For y = x + m·L(x), with a loop L of gain g repeating every N samples and its first repeat at unity gain, the complex cepstrum peaks only at multiples of N when |g| < 1 and |g − m| < 1, with ĉ(N) = m and ĉ(2N) = m(2g − m)/2, so g = ĉ(2N)/m + m/2. The real cepstrum is half the complex one at these lags, so the formula applied to it reads m/2 and biases g by −m/4 (g 0.75 at m 0.77 reads 0.56): double it first. The form g = ĉ(2N)/ĉ(N) + ĉ(N)/2 holds for any mix law, but ĉ(N) = m only for an additive mix; a crossfade gives m/(1 − m), which breaks the conditions above m = (1 + g)/(2 + g), 0.5 only with no feedback; the mix-law-free form stays correct past it. The Morgan delay's mix law is unknown, the guitar's own cepstrum adds at these lags, the backing shrinks every peak, and the delay's cut and tape-drive controls (`packs/morgan/manifest.json`) may smear the 2N peak. Second, with mix ratio π, Sarroff's model gives a direct-to-reverberant ratio of about 20·log10((1 − π)/π) plus a constant, so a room estimator's C50 could proxy mix after calibration on stored targets. Third, the stem's envelope autocorrelation minus a scaled copy of the residual's (mix minus stem), to cancel the shared beat; it needs stem and mix audio. Fourth, a tempo prior, which the stored set cannot test. On the wide draws Morgan's delay time is drawn log-uniform over 60–800 ms (`learn/knob_ceiling2.py`) by `full_space.draw_new`, which the build uses; factory and start draws jitter around a preset's time (E1a's p10–p90, 97–684 ms); and E1a records sync off throughout, so no draw follows tempo; the 100–1,100 ms range in `learn/every_control.py` is Tone King's. No paper reports a tempo-prior gain, and E1b's outline already lists synced delays.

Several tools cannot run blind: ST-ITO's optimiser, DiffVox, Diff-MST and Lee's mixing-graph search need a dry input, a reference or the multitrack ([DiffVox](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14735v2); [Lee 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15948v1)). Kendrick's BlindRT needs "for speech at least an hour, for music perhaps longer" ([BlindRT](https://github.com/kenders2000/BlindRT)).

**Needs the user's separator ruling:** RemFX's reverb and delay removers (96 MB each, CC-NC) as "how much did removal take" features ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/api/records/8218621)); BUDDy, which outputs sub-band decay while dereverberating speech and singing ([BUDDy](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07472v1)); and the dereverb RoFormers in `docs/research/di-recovery-2026-10.md`, where the vocal models score below doing nothing on MVSEP's "other" stem (0.98–3.41 against 4.65; anvuew's stereo model 5.40). None has been tested on guitar. Fx-Encoder++'s extractor returns an embedding, not audio: feature extraction, which the rule allows, though the user may want to confirm.

## Where the notes disagree

Fx-Encoder++'s two triples are different metrics, not a conflict: 39.4 / 20.5 / 12.2 is Recall@10 (12.2 with a text query, 11.7 with audio) and 19.9 / 7.1 / 3.0 is Recall@1 (3.0 with either), over 500 candidates. The 7.1 uses a music separator; a universal one gives 2.1. Separation keeps 36% at top-1 and 52% at top-10, so the notes' "about a third" and "about half" both hold. The repository states CC BY-NC 4.0 and GitHub's API reads NOASSERTION; either is allowed. One note treats ST-ITO's Table 2 (ρ 0.955 reverb room, 0.815 delay mix) as a probe on AFx-Rep; it is an optimiser processing a different dry input, not blind evidence. One note calls Hinrichs 2022 "proven on mixtures"; here it counts as synthetic. **Correction:** this report's first version read RLAT one column off (distortion as reverb) and used ST-ITO's figures as probe evidence; both are fixed above.

## First test: a long-window cepstrum, as an arm beside E1c

F50 found delay time unreadable on the stem and mix from the stored 10 ms log-mels and their envelope autocorrelation. This arm asks whether audio-domain, finer-resolution features recover it. Average the real cepstrum over 2.7 s frames (2^17 samples, 0.5 s hop), drop lags under 40 ms (the chord's pitch), and take the highest peak in 60–800 ms (the draw), with 40–1,500 ms secondary: its lag is the time, its height the on/off score. The log flattens the chord's spectrum, so the pitch comb that silences the product's detector (F48) should weigh less (inference). A second arm subtracts the mean cepstrum of the training songs' wide delay-offs of the same input, leave one song out; the trick improved cepstral delay estimates about twofold in underwater multipath ([arXiv 1810.11990](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.11990)).

**Readouts, by F49's conventions.** On the 402 wide delay-ons: within ±10% of the set time (±3% secondary), log2 error and Spearman, each paired against the picker on the same items, with 90% intervals from 2,000 resamples of the 7 band clusters; double and half lags counted apart. On the 770 wide draws: peak-height AUC overall and on the strong third (the top third of delay mix among wide delay-ons, against all wide delay-offs). Comparators (target, stem, mix): the picker (43.5%, 13.7%, 6.7%), the lag head (46.0%, 10.7%, 9.5%), the wide median (7.2%), E1a's prominence at the set lag (AUC 0.79, 0.58, 0.55); and the reader's strong-third stem AUC, 0.614.

**Order, by cost.** First the clean targets: stored audio, CPU minutes. The arm must not be shown worse than the picker there (its paired interval not wholly below 0), or it stops. Then E0's 84 stored X-LANCE stem waveforms (`judge-v5/separator-check/stems/xlance_g/*.npy`), free: 36 delay stems (12 at 300 ms, 24 at 500 ms), beside F48's detector, which reported nothing on any of the 36 whole stems (300 ms is the cleaner test; 500 ms is a quarter note at 120 BPM). The bar there: hits within ±10% on each set, against the S stems read at the same lags and against chance (about 8%: a log-uniform guess over 60–800 ms lands within ±10% that often); the subtraction arm takes E0's 48 S and reverb stems as its offs. Only if the delay stems clearly beat both: mixes rebuilt deterministically on CPU (`trusted_data.self_built_mix`, seeded mastering; zero renders), then the stems re-separated (about 4.3 s a clip on MPS: about 55 min for the 770 wide draws, about 3 h for all 2,640; the ML session's call). On the stem, passing means beating the picker's 13.7% with a log2 error below the prior's, which the picker missed (+0.350). No download, render or training.

If time reads on the stem, try the feedback readout next (doubled real cepstrum, true time first). If it reads only on the target, F50's verdict extends to this method on these stems. If the masking renders go ahead, storing stem and mix audio would let every row above be tested on them.

## Conclusion

The project is not behind a field that has solved this; it is working on a case the field has not studied. One option for the ML session is to stop asking one model for all seven settings: read delay time with a lag reader (the training-free picker and the learned lag head both work on the target: F49, F50), trying audio-domain ones such as the cepstrum on the stem; try encoders mainly for on/off; and leave feedback at the prior on the stem, where F49's reader does not beat it (the mix was not audited) and no published method reports a feedback-specific error.
