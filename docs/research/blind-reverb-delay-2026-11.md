# Nothing proven recovers guitar reverb or delay

*Research, 2026-11-03, for the ML session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

Nothing proven exists. No tool, model or paper recovers a guitar's reverb (mix, decay) or delay (time, feedback, mix) blind from strummed chords in a real mix or a separated stem. No published method estimates delay feedback blind from any audio at all. So there is no working wheel to adopt. Published results stop at single held notes in synthetic mixes, isolated tracks, and reverb applied to a whole mix, and they lose accuracy to backing and separation much as the project does. What exists are parts: a signal-processing method for echo time that has never been tried under a backing, three pretrained encoders that read reverb well but only on isolated audio or whole mixes, and a detector that gives on/off only. The shortlist below ranks seven items. Five need a download (four of 0.8–1.6 GB), which is the ML session's and the user's call. The first test needs nothing new: a long-window cepstrum on the stored delay clips, scored against the 44% and 14% time hit rates and the CNN's 0.61 stem AUC, in CPU minutes.

## Published evidence stops at single notes, solo tracks and whole-mix reverb

**Proven on real mixtures.** Two results use real songs, and neither reads a guitar's settings. Fx-Encoder++ (Sony, ISMIR 2025) pulls one instrument's effect embedding out of a MUSDB18 mix after synthetic effects are added. Its top-1 retrieval of the right effect setup among 500 falls from **19.9% on isolated stems to 7.1% on separated stems and 3.0% from the mix**, with no guitar and no per-effect figure ([Fx-Encoder++](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02273v1)). A linear probe on frozen LAION-CLAP reads reverb time on MUSDB18-HQ mixes at **R² 0.77 (MAE 0.21 s)**, but one impulse response was applied to the whole mix ([arXiv 2607.03806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03806)). A guitar-only reverb is a far smaller share of a mix, so that figure is a ceiling, not a forecast.

**Proven only on isolated or synthetic signals.** Hinrichs et al. regress slapback time (MAE 0.038 on a 0–1 scale) and mix (0.027), and reverb room size (0.041) and mix (0.034). But the guitar holds **one note for 2 s** under virtual keys, bass and drums. Errors grow **2–3× from the quietest to the loudest backing**, feedback is never regressed, and a network trained on clean guitar fell below 40% accuracy once a kick drum alone was added ([Hinrichs 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)). AFx-Rep embeddings recover reverb room size at ρ 0.955 and delay mix at 0.815 on single-effect renders ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)). RemFX detects reverb at 0.78–0.85 accuracy and delay at 0.68–0.74 on solo sources ([RemFX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16177)). Cepstrum and autocorrelation found a 134–137 ms slapback on Elvis's Sun records, but only in unaccompanied passages and with no error statistics ([Halmrast 2019](https://www.arpjournal.com/asarpwp/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Tor-Halmrast_ARP2019.pdf)). Room-acoustic estimators explain the project's random RT60 rule: a deep T60 estimator erred by **126% on music against 54% on speech**, because notes "do not stop sharply" ([Callens & Cernak 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11167)). Clarity read from music is confounded by "the natural reverberance of the excitation signals" ([Kendrick et al. 2008](https://salford-repository.worktribe.com/output/1465753/monaural-room-acoustic-parameters-from-music-and-speech)). A ringing chord looks like reverb.

**Paper only, no code.** Sarroff & Michaels infer a reverb plugin's 21 settings and its mix ratio from wet speech alone, matching a human expert by ear; only their renderer is public, and they warn that mix "might be easily confused with reverberation time" ([Sarroff & Michaels 2020](https://dafx2020.mdw.ac.at/proceedings/papers/DAFx2020_paper_5.pdf)). SunAFXiNet estimates delay and reverb chains on solo guitar, naming the last effect's type with 0.695 accuracy ([DAFx24](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2024/papers/DAFx24_paper_53.pdf)). Hinrichs's code was promised but never found.

Two patterns follow. The project's losses are typical, not a pipeline bug: separation costs Fx-Encoder++ about two-thirds of its top-1 retrieval. And encoders split by effect: in RLAT's table, all seven encoders read reverb at MAE 5.4–6.4 (×100) but delay at 21–24 ([RLAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28127)). That is near what a constant guess scores on a uniform 0–1 knob (inference; RLAT's knob distribution was not read). So encoders suit reverb and delay on/off and mix, while delay time belongs to signal processing.

## Four untested ideas, and routes that need a ruling or a dry track

Four ideas in the notes are the researchers' own and are **untested**. First, a cepstral readout of feedback and mix. Model the delay as dry plus m times a loop of gain g and N samples; the complex cepstrum then peaks only at multiples of N, with ĉ(N) = m and ĉ(2N) = m(2g − m)/2, so g = ĉ(2N)/m + m/2. The guitar's own cepstrum adds at the same lags, the backing shrinks every peak (mix reads low), and filtered repeats smear the 2N peak. The Morgan delay has high-cut, low-cut and tape-drive controls (`packs/morgan/manifest.json`); whether they sit in the loop is unknown. Second, a reverb-mix proxy from Sarroff's equation: with mix ratio π, the direct-to-reverberant ratio is about 20·log10((1 − π)/π) plus a constant, so a room estimator's C50 could stand in for mix after calibration on stored renders. Third, a differential autocorrelation: the stem's envelope autocorrelation minus a scaled copy of the residual's (mix minus stem), to cancel the shared beat and keep the guitar's echo. Fourth, a tempo prior, for which no paper reports a gain. The project has a tempo estimator (`analysis/features.py`) and note-to-ms conversion (`packs/timing.py`), but it writes delays free-running (`learn/full_space.py`), and the samplers I read draw time on a log scale (`learn/every_control.py`, `learn/knob_ceiling2.py`). If the 2,600 mixes were built that way, their delay times ignore tempo, and the stored set cannot show a gain. Check that first.

Several tools cannot run blind: ST-ITO's optimiser, DiffVox, Diff-MST and Lee's mixing-graph search need a dry input, a reference or the multitrack ([DiffVox](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.14735v2); [Lee 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15948v1)). Kendrick's BlindRT needs minutes of audio per estimate ([BlindRT](https://github.com/kenders2000/BlindRT)).

**Needs the user's separator ruling:** RemFX's reverb and delay removers (96 MB each, CC-NC), used as "how much did removal take" features ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/api/records/8218621)); BUDDy, which outputs sub-band decay while dereverberating speech and singing ([BUDDy](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.07472v1)); and the dereverb RoFormers in `docs/research/di-recovery-2026-10.md`. None has been tested on guitar. Fx-Encoder++'s extractor returns an embedding, not audio, so it reads as feature extraction, which the rule allows; the user may still want to confirm.

## Where the notes disagree

The two Fx-Encoder++ triples are **different metrics, not a conflict**. 39.4 / 20.5 / 12.2 is Recall@10 (12.2 with a text query, 11.7 with an audio one); 19.9 / 7.1 / 3.0 is Recall@1; both rank 500 candidates on MUSDB18 ([Fx-Encoder++](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02273v1)). The loss looks larger at top-1 (separation keeps 36%, the mix 15%) than at top-10 (52% and 31%), so one note's "about half" and another's "about a third" are both right. The notes do not say which query gave the top-1 3.0. The repository states CC BY-NC 4.0 while GitHub's API reads NOASSERTION (an unrecognised file); the weights' own terms were not read. Every reading is allowed. One note calls Hinrichs 2022 and Fx-Encoder++ "proven on mixtures"; this report counts Hinrichs as synthetic (virtual instruments, single notes) and Fx-Encoder++ as real mixtures for retrieval only. Hinrichs's HT-Demucs follow-up appears as 2023 and 2024, probably one paper; it copies the effect rather than reading knobs ([Hinrichs 2024](https://asmp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s13636-024-00330-0)).

## First test: a long-window cepstrum on the stored delay clips

Use the stored delay-on and delay-off clips in all three versions: clean target, X-LANCE stem and mix. Average the real cepstrum over 2.7 s frames (2^17 samples, 0.5 s hop), drop lags under 40 ms, which hold the chord's pitch, and take the highest peak in 40–1,500 ms. Its lag is the time and its height the on/off score. The log step flattens the guitar's own spectrum, so the comb of pitch peaks that makes the existing detector abstain on chords (`analysis/features.py`) should weigh less (inference). Add an arm that first subtracts the mean cepstrum of delay-off clips of the same version, fitted on one half and scored on the other; this trick improved cepstral delay estimates about twofold in underwater multipath ([arXiv 1810.11990](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.11990)). Add the differential autocorrelation as a third arm.

**Metric and bar.** Time hit rate on delay-on clips, with the existing picker's tolerance (the notes propose ±3%), counting double and half errors apart; and peak-height AUC on the louder-settings split. To pass, beat **44% hits on clean targets, 14% on stems, and 0.61 stem AUC** with a bootstrap interval that excludes it. Report the mix too, since it keeps the echo intact. Cost: CPU minutes; no download, render or training.

If it clears the stem bar, try the cepstral feedback readout next, with the true time first. If it clears only the clean bar, masking is the loss and a better time reader alone will not fix it; the levers in `docs/research/effect-detection-2026-10.md` (the wet-target separator fine-tune and audibility-aware labels) remain the route. If the ML session and the user approve one download, make it AFx-Rep (1.16 GB) for reverb, against the same 0.61 stem bar.

## Conclusion

The project is not behind a field that has solved this; it is working on a case the field has not studied. Every published estimator sees the guitar alone or reverb on the whole mix, never one guitar's time effects inside a dense, strummed, mastered song. The useful change is to stop asking one model for all seven settings. Read reverb with encoders, read delay time with signal processing, and leave feedback at the prior until something beats it, because nothing published has.
