# Cut new riffs from Guitar-TECHS; classify parts with Basic Pitch

Evidence tags: **[V]** verified at the source (a researcher read the dataset record, file headers, zip listing, paper, LICENSE or PyPI page in this session, or it is in this repo's committed docs); **[M]** measured (audio downloaded and analysed numerically, never listened to); **[I]** inferred (reasoning from verified facts); **[U]** unverified (prior knowledge or a search-engine snippet only). Grid codes: styles **S1** strummed chords, **S2** arpeggios / fingerpicked, **S3** single-note lines, **S4** power chords / riffs, **S5** bends / vibrato leads; registers **L** low (open position), **M** mid (frets 5–12), **H** high (fret 12 and up).

**Bottom line.** Guitar-TECHS, the dataset the project already ships from, is also the best source for the missing riff cells: it is CC BY 4.0, its DI is split before the amp, and it holds about **295 minutes of DI** from three players and three guitars, far more than the two P1 clips in use today. It covers strummed chords, single-note lines and bend/vibrato material in all three registers, with per-string MIDI that lets the project pick register exactly, plus 12 original solo excerpts that may supply arpeggio material. Two more CC BY 4.0 academic sets (EGFxSet Clean and EG-IPT) are truly DI but contain isolated notes only, and four small CC0 Freesound uploads add genuine dry riffs, including the only open **power-chord** material found (unfa's 7-minute drop-D jam, which still needs an ear check). **Power chords and riffs above open position (S4 M/H) are the one real gap**: no permissive, verified-dry source exists, so the cheapest fix is for the maintainer to record and release a few CC0 riffs. For Q2, no open tool has published accuracy on separated guitar stems, so nothing here is battle-tested on this task. The best-supported chain is **Spotify Basic Pitch** (Apache-2.0 code and weights, 17K parameters, CoreML on macOS, GuitarSet note F1 0.79), with chords-versus-single-notes decided by counting onset-anchored pitch classes and register taken from note-pitch percentiles. It can be checked in an afternoon on the project's 61 development parts, whose htdemucs_6s stems and isolated amp tracks already sit on disk, with GuitarSet's solo/comp labels used for threshold tuning only, because Basic Pitch was trained on GuitarSet.

## Guitar-TECHS holds 295 DI minutes; the shipped riffs use about 20 seconds of them

The project's two riffs come from player P1's chord and A-major scale files ([riffs.json](../../samples/riffs/riffs.json)) [V]. The full record contains much more. Its licence field is `cc-by-4.0` and the project site says "All data is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 License" ([Zenodo 14963133](https://zenodo.org/records/14963133), [guitar-techs.github.io](https://guitar-techs.github.io/)) [V]. The DI is genuinely dry: each guitar went "through a signal splitter, allowing us to send the signal to an audio interface for direct digital collection (i.e. 'direct input') and an amplifier that was recorded using a microphone" ([arXiv 2501.03720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03720)) [V]. Neither the splitter type (passive or active) nor the interface input (Hi-Z or line) is stated [V absence].

The three players use three distinct guitars, all 22-fret and in standard tuning, with tone and volume at maximum ([arXiv 2501.03720](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03720)) [V]. **P1** plays an Ibanez PF-300 (Les Paul type) on the bridge pickup with 0.011–0.050 **flat-wound** strings. **P2** plays an EVH Wolfgang (Super Strat type) on the **neck pickup only**, with 0.009–0.042 strings. **P3** plays a Sire T7 (Telecaster type) with both pickups selected. P1 and P2 recorded chords, scales and techniques; P3 recorded only the 12 musical excerpts [V]. P1's flat-wounds and P2's neck pickup point to darker DIs and P3's Tele to the brightest [I], which matters because the shipped riffs are all P1.

The paper and record state "48,000 Hz and a bit depth of 32-bit floating point", but the files disagree: the first DI header in every zip is **24-bit integer PCM**, mono 48 kHz for P1 chords, notes and scales, **2-channel 44.1 kHz** for P1 techniques, and 2-channel 48 kHz for all P2 and P3 zips ([Zenodo 14963133 files](https://zenodo.org/records/14963133)) [V]. The 2-channel files are probably duplicated mono and should be checked and folded before shipping [I]. The record also warns the signals "may exhibit ≤100ms misalignment" against the MIDI, which matters when cutting by MIDI timestamps [V]. The MIDI is six tracks at 9600 ticks per quarter note [V]; if the tracks map to strings, fret equals MIDI note minus that string's open pitch, which gives exact register selection [I]. The files sit at `P{n}_{category}/audio/directinput/directinput_<name>.wav` across nine zips totalling about 4 GB, with `__MACOSX` junk inside [V]. A sister set, EGSet12 (CC BY 4.0, 12 solos on a Sire T7), is amp-mic only and therefore unusable ([Zenodo 11406378](https://zenodo.org/records/11406378)) [V]. Nobody has yet described the 12 P3 excerpts piece by piece; they must be auditioned, because they are the only permissive, dry, musical multi-note performances found [V absence; I].

## Recommended chain: onset-anchored pitch-class counting, with untested thresholds

The recommended chain is one engine and two small pieces of arithmetic, all **[I] and untested** unless marked. Take the htdemucs_6s guitar stem for the part's time range (and, on a full mix, also the 4-stem "other" residual, following the approach in [arXiv 2510.05756](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05756)). Run Basic Pitch with `--save-model-outputs`, frequency limited to about **80–1400 Hz** (E2 is 82.4 Hz), `melodia_trick=False` and `onset_thresh` raised to about 0.5–0.6; the code exposes these parameters and the default `min_note_len` of 11 frames, about 120 ms ([note_creation.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/note_creation.py), [constants.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/spotify/basic-pitch/main/basic_pitch/constants.py)) [V]. Turning off onset-less note creation matters because organ and keys bleed is mostly sustained without plucks [I]. Next, drop any note whose onset coincides within about 30 ms with a stronger note 12, 19 or 24 semitones below, which removes harmonic and distortion ghosts, then count **pitch classes**, not pitches.

For **(a) chords versus single notes**, compute per excerpt the fraction of active frames with three or more pitch classes (`p3`), the median number of onsets landing within 40 ms of each other (`onset_cluster`, since a strum spreads strings over roughly 10–50 ms), and the share of two-pitch-class frames a fifth or fourth apart (`p_power`). Starting rules: chords if `p3 > 0.35` or `onset_cluster ≥ 3`; power-chord or dyad riff if mostly fifth-apart dyads with `onset_cluster ≈ 2`; single notes if at least 70% of active frames hold one pitch class with `onset_cluster ≈ 1`; otherwise "mixed", which should play both riff types rather than guess. Arpeggios look polyphonic by frame but monophonic by onset, so a disagreement between the two features should map to the S2 arpeggio riff. Basic Pitch scores 0.79 overall against 0.84 on the mono half, which implies the chord half scores lower and chord notes get missed [V; I], biasing counts downward; that is tolerable for a binary decision.

For **(b) register**, report a pitch range and treat the fret bucket as a heuristic, because the same pitch is playable in several positions [I]. For single-note parts, take duration-weighted 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of note MIDI pitch: median below about G3 (55) means low, about G3–D5 (55–74) means mid, and median above E5 (76) or 90th percentile above A5 (81) means high. For chords, use the lowest note per strum (below about A2 suggests open position; B2–A3 without open-string pitches suggests a barre at frets 5–12; a lowest note at or above G3 suggests high triads) and the top voice. Percentiles absorb occasional octave errors, and Basic Pitch's harmonic stacking with a sub-harmonic was designed to avoid octave mistakes; removing that stacking cut GuitarSet Fno from 0.79 to 0.65 ([arXiv 2203.09893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)) [V]. EGDB-PG's per-pitch results show accuracy is weakest at the highest pitches under amp tones ([arXiv 2504.07406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07406)) [V, figure caption]. On parts already classed single-note, torchcrepe's periodicity-weighted median with a ±12-semitone consistency check against Basic Pitch catches octave slips [I].

The failure mode that is most likely to dominate is untested and unquantified anywhere: at about 1 dB SNR a rhythm guitar under a lead leaks into the "guitar" stem and makes the lead look like chords [I]. The separator results already show the stem is 5 dB cleaner on single-guitar parts than on multi-guitar ones [V], so the classifier should be expected to work on the 7 single-guitar parts first and fail on the 54 multi-guitar ones [I]. Other known risks are a distorted single note's +19 twelfth posing as a power chord, double-stops in leads reading as dyads, and keys bleed shifting register estimates [I]. **Explicitly untested:** every threshold above, Basic Pitch on any separated stem, Basic Pitch on crunch tones, the bleed-rejection value of onset gating and of YourMT3+ instrument tags, and Apple-silicon runtimes for anything except the published Intel figure.

## The cheapest check runs on stems already on disk

The cheapest confirmation reuses existing material and needs no new separation. The separator test stored htdemucs_6s stems for **61 development parts**, each with its isolated amp track, under `~/ndsp-presets/learn/sep2/` ([separator-upgrade-results.md](../separator-upgrade-results.md)) [V]. Step one is to label each part's role by ear on the amp track (chords, single notes, power-chord riff, arpeggio, mixed), which takes about an hour; the earlier part-locator labels cannot stand in, since only 2 of 136 parts were ever labelled rhythm ([part-locator-results.md](../part-locator-results.md)) [V]. Step two runs the chain on both the amp track and the stem of the same 10-s crop. The amp track's Basic Pitch register serves as the reference for (b), and comparing stem against amp track isolates the damage done by separation, reported separately for the 7 single-guitar and 54 multi-guitar parts. Runtime is trivial: 61 ten-second crops twice is about 20 minutes of audio, roughly a minute of CPU at the published rate [I]. Following project practice, declare it in a committed `docs/<topic>-plan.md` with its pass gate (for example, at least 80% class agreement with the ear labels and at least 80% register-bucket agreement between stem and amp track, both illustrative [I]), and enter the gate in [dev-test-ledger.json](../dev-test-ledger.json), before any result exists [V practice].

GuitarSet supplies a second, larger labelled set at almost no cost: 360 excerpts of about 30 s from 6 players, each in a solo and a comping version, with pitch-contour annotations ([guitarset.weebly.com](http://guitarset.weebly.com/), [Zenodo 3371780](https://zenodo.org/records/3371780)) [V]. The 0.68 GB `audio_mono-pickup_mix.zip` is about 3 hours of audio, around 10 CPU minutes for Basic Pitch [I]. Its solo/comp labels test (a) and its annotations give exact register for (b). Two cautions limit what it proves: it is acoustic guitar, and **Basic Pitch was trained on GuitarSet** ([arXiv 2203.09893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09893)) [V], so scores there are in-domain and optimistic. Use GuitarSet to set thresholds and the development stems to judge them [I]. A final optional step targets the dominant failure mode directly: mix Guitar-TECHS chord DI under P3 solo DI at about 1 dB, run both through a project crunch preset, and check whether the lead still classifies as single notes; MIDI gives exact labels and register, and the material is CC BY so the test clips could even be committed [I].

## Conclusion

The riff problem is mostly a curation job rather than a sourcing job: the licence-clean, truly dry material already sits inside the dataset the project ships from, and the shortfall is musical phrasing, not legal access. That shifts the remaining work to listening (P3's 12 excerpts and unfa's jam decide whether S2 and S4 need new recordings) and to one small CC0 recording session for power chords. Register-matched riffs only pay off if the page can tell which register and style a part uses, and here the honest finding is that no open tool has been measured on the input the product actually sees. Basic Pitch is a sound, licence-clean bet, but the decisive number is the single-guitar versus multi-guitar split on the project's own stems; if the classifier holds on the 7 single-guitar parts and collapses on the 54 multi-guitar ones, the right product move is to stop guessing on multi-guitar songs and play both a chord riff and a line riff in the matching register.
