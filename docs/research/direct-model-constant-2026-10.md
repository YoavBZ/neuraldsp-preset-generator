# Decode the median before adding more data

*Research report, 2026-10-21, for the ML session's direct model after the pilot read worse than the per-class constant. Research, not a declared plan: any run needs its own plan, review and ledger entry.*

The direct model's best chance to beat the per-class median on mean absolute knob error (E) is to change how it reads out its answer. That costs no renders and no training. E is minimised by the median of the model's predicted spread for each knob. D_stem instead reports the mean of its single heaviest mixture component, chosen once for all knobs together, and it picks its checkpoint by likelihood rather than by E. In the researchers' toy simulation of the project's label mix, that readout alone costs 0.004–0.009 of E, and it loses to the constant by about 0.009 when the audio carries no cue [Sim]. That is most of the pilot's gap of **+0.0128 [−0.0013, +0.0269] on R_in**. So the first moves take minutes of CPU on the stored pilot checkpoints: read each knob's median, average the predictions of the two seeds and the two stored halves, and pull each knob toward the constant by a weight fitted on the early-stopping set. That last step cannot score worse than the constant on the set it is fitted to. Two more zero-render readouts, nearest neighbours in the network's own features and a per-knob identifiability table, show whether the network holds knob information that a better readout could use. If a gap remains, one retrain of about 17 minutes per seed on MPS aims the head at the metric: a per-knob absolute-error (median) or binned output, written as "the constant plus a learned offset" and early-stopped on E. Larger routes rest on indirect evidence and cost hours to days, mostly in separation, so they come after the cheap checks. They include frozen AFx-Rep features, pretraining on clean renders and several settings per riff. The exception is timing: embedding extraction is cheapest if it is added to the X-LANCE stem stage before that stage runs. The researchers found no published paper that reports a constant baseline for effect-knob estimation, or that tests amp-sim knobs from separated stems. So every number specific to this project is either a project record, a toy simulation, or a published result carried over by inference, and the tags below say which.

*Tags. **[V]** published, and read at source by the researchers. **[S]** published, but seen only in a search summary or abstract summary. **[P]** the project's own record or code. **[I]** the researchers' or this writer's inference or arithmetic, not a published result. **[V, by absence]** the researchers looked and found nothing. **[Sim]** the researchers' own numpy toy simulation: two short scripts, [topmean.py](direct-model-constant-sims/topmean.py) and [post.py](direct-model-constant-sims/post.py), re-run on 2026-10-21 with identical output.*

## The readout, not just the data, may keep D_stem above the median

**Where things stand.** The constant guesses each continuous setting as the median of the training labels for its amp and base class. It scores **E = 0.203 on R_in and 0.201 on R_out**, against 0.216 and 0.219 for D_stem ([pilot results](../direct-model-stems-pilot-results.md)) [P]. The two seeds disagree sharply: +0.003 and +0.023 against the constant on R_in. The learning curve projects the full 20,311 examples to −0.020 [−0.047, +0.007] if the log-linear trend holds, and to −0.004 if it slows as part C's did [P]. Step 3 stops the model unless its band-clustered interval against the constant sits entirely below zero, after about 19–22 hours of separation and 5–7.4 GPU hours ([plan](../direct-model-stems-plan.md)) [P]. One fact sets the bar. R_in's labels come from the same generator as training: 40% jitter around factory presets, 20% around the start preset, 40% uniform ([pretrain_set.py](../../learn/pretrain_set.py)) [P]. So there is no label shift, and the per-class median is already close to the best possible guess for an input that carries no information [I]. To beat it, the model has to use the audio, and has to report that use in the form E scores.

**Four choices in the current recipe pull against E.** First, the reported answer is the mean of the highest-weight component of a 4-component Gaussian mixture, and that component is chosen jointly for all knobs (`top_error` in [pretrain_set.py](../../learn/pretrain_set.py)) [P]. Second, early stopping and the checkpoint choice use the total likelihood loss (knob NLL plus switch and mic cross-entropy), not E ([direct_stems.py](../../learn/direct_stems.py)) [P]. Third, the knob loss itself is a Gaussian-mixture negative log-likelihood ([song_model.py](../../learn/song_model.py)) [P]. Fourth, step 3's declared answer, the "pooled mixture's top component" over three seeds, picks one component from one seed, so it never averages the seeds [P for the code, I for the reading]. Statistics is clear on the first point. Under absolute error, the best single answer is the median of the predictive distribution. Gneiting shows how badly a mismatch can go. In his simulation, a forecaster who always says 0.05 beats one who issues the true conditional mean under absolute error (0.96 against 0.97), while the forecaster who issues the matching summary scores 0.86 ([Gneiting 2011](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0912.0902)) [V]. On the third point, Gaussian likelihood training down-weights poorly fitted, high-variance points. Across the paper's 12 UCI datasets, plain MSE matched or beat NLL on every one (concrete RMSE 4.96 against 6.08; carbon tied), and β-NLL with β = 0.5 landed between the two on 10 of the 12 (on yacht it was worse than both) ([Seitzer et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.09168)) [V]. Forecasting practice has the same lesson. Under MAE, the conditional median of intermittent demand can be "a flat zero", so the fix is to forecast a full distribution and then extract the summary the error measure rewards ([Petropoulos et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03854)) [S].

**The toy puts the readout's cost at most of the gap.** The researchers drew labels from the project's mixture (8 knobs, 12 factory centres, a start centre, σ 0.1 jitter clipped to [0, 1], and 40% uniform). They then computed a Bayes posterior given a noisy audio "cue" for each knob, approximated by weighting 40,000 prior draws, and compared readouts [Sim]:

| Cue noise (sd) | Posterior median | Top-component mean | Constant | Top minus median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 0.111 | 0.115 | 0.218 | +0.004 |
| 0.4 | 0.167 | 0.174 | 0.215 | +0.0075 |
| 0.8 | 0.196 | 0.205 | 0.210 | +0.009 |
| no cue | 0.215 | 0.225 | 0.215 | +0.009 |

The script's sd 0.1 row is left out: there the weights rest on only a few dozen effective draws, too few to separate the readouts (it even puts the top component 0.003 ahead) [Sim]. With no cue, the posterior median *is* the constant, and the top-component readout is **0.009 worse than the constant** [Sim]. A second toy fits a 4-component mixture to uninformative labels, which is what an MDN learns when the input says nothing. Over 20 repeats, the top component's mean scored 0.2148 against 0.2088 for the per-knob median: +0.006, with a range of +0.0013 to +0.0106. The fitted mixture's own per-knob median scored 0.2090, almost exactly the plain median, which is what A1 below relies on [Sim]. These toys idealise the posterior, and the real network's posterior is learned and may be miscalibrated. They also understate one cost: when two components carry similar weight, a small change in the input flips *every* knob at once between cluster centres [I]. No published audio paper measures top-component against median readout under MAE [V, by absence].

**Losing to the constant does not mean the model knows nothing.** In the same toy, an unbiased but noisy model (prediction = truth + noise with sd 0.3) scores 0.2023, against the constant's 0.2038. Yet a 50/50 blend of the two scores **0.155** [Sim]. The blend weight was tuned on the same draw, so this figure is optimistic. Murphy's decomposition of skill against a climatological constant, MSSS = r² − (r − s_f/s_o)² − (bias/σ_o)², gives the reason ([climpred docs](https://climpred.readthedocs.io/en/v2.1.6/api/climpred.metrics._msess_murphy.html)) [V for the formula]. Here r is the correlation with the truth and s_f/s_o is the ratio of prediction spread to label spread. Take an unbiased model whose predictions spread as widely as the labels. Its squared-error skill is 2r − 1, so it needs a correlation above 0.5 per knob just to tie. Recalibrating it toward the constant leaves r², which is never negative [I, arithmetic on the formula]. The earlier arm B, a sibling network, had per-setting correlations of 0.1–0.6 and a prediction spread about a third of the label spread ([arm B results](../song-settings-results.md)) [P]. D_stem's own r and spread have not been read. They decide whether pulling toward the constant, or away from it, recovers skill.

**The literature explains why this task is harder, not how to beat a constant.** Published guitar-effect estimators report MAE of 0.02–0.16 on a unit scale: distortion gain 0.05, tone 0.10 and edge 0.14 ([Jürgens et al. 2020](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2020/proceedings/papers/DAFx2020_paper_2.pdf)) [V]; gain 0.040 and tone 0.038 across 13 drive pedals ([Comunità et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1)) [V]; and 0.0885 on unseen dry guitar tracks ([Okita & Katayose 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22276)) [V]. None reports a mean or constant baseline [V, by absence]. If labels were uniform, the implied constant would score 0.25, so those models are 1.5–12× better [I]. But they work on one isolated instrument at a normalised level, often with sources shared between training and test. The only study inside a mix, which used synthetic single-note mixes, saw errors grow **two- to three-fold** from the quietest backing to the loudest ([Hinrichs et al. 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [V]. Separation also cut an effect encoder's retrieval accuracy by about two-thirds, from R@1 19.9 on true stems to about 7.1 on separated ones ([Fx-Encoder++](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02273v1)) [V]. Two more patterns stand out. The lowest published knob errors came from tiny networks trained from scratch: two convolution layers of 6 and 12 maps, or a 32-unit MLP ([Comunità](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1); [Hinrichs](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf); [Jürgens](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2020/proceedings/papers/DAFx2020_paper_2.pdf)) [V]. And every guitar estimator in the sources reviewed uses bounded regression with MSE or L1; none uses a mixture-density head [V, by absence]. Model size is therefore not the lever. The domain (stems, bleed, unknown level) and the head are [I].

## Six zero-render checks run in under an hour on the stored models

All six run on the two stored D_stem seeds and the stored Mega-53 features. The sets are the early-stopping set (998 examples), R_in (1,407) and R_out (672). The checks need no renders and no GPU, so they can run beside the build [I]. The code shows one more free gain. Each example's E is currently the *average of two scores*, one per stored 4.5-s half, and the seed-averaged E is the average of the seeds' scores (`evaluate` in [pretrain_set.py](../../learn/pretrain_set.py); [direct_stems.py](../../learn/direct_stems.py)) [P]. No prediction is ever averaged.

| Check | What to do | What it guarantees or tests | Evidence | Mac cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1. Median readout** | Per example and knob, find the median of the 4-component mixture (about 30 bisection steps), clip to [0, 1], and report the mixture mean beside it | Reports the summary E rewards | Gneiting [V]; toys show 0.004–0.009 lost to the current readout [Sim] | Minutes, CPU |
| **A2. Average predictions** | Average the readouts over both seeds and both halves (use the mean, not a median of medians) | Under absolute error, the average's E is never above the members' mean E, for every example and knob. Averaging today's readouts therefore cannot do worse than today's +0.013 on R_in, and averaging A1's readouts cannot do worse than A1. It gains only where members fall on both sides of the label [I, triangle inequality] | Krogh & Vedelsby show the squared-error version ([NeurIPS 1994](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/1994/hash/b8c37e33defde51cf91e1e03e51657da-Abstract.html)) [S] | Minutes, CPU |
| **A3. Pull toward the constant** | Per knob, amp and class, output c + b·(θ̂ − c), with b in [0, 1] fitted by absolute error on the early-stopping set. b ∈ {0, 1} is per-knob gating | b = 0 is the constant, so on the fitting set it can only tie or win [I] | Murphy decomposition [V]; shrinkage after Copas lowers prediction error, but "often worked poorly in individual datasets" ([Taguri](https://www.ism.ac.jp/editsec/aism/pdf/s10463-022-00829-3.pdf); [Leiden thesis](https://scholarlypublications.universiteitleiden.nl/access/item%3A3494477/view)) [S]; toy blend 0.155 against 0.204 [Sim] | Seconds, CPU |
| **A4. Nearest neighbours in the trunk** | Predict each knob as the median label of the k nearest training rows (same amp and class) in D_stem's pooled features; pick k on the early-stopping set | With k equal to every row, it *is* the constant. It separates "the head is wrong" from "the features carry nothing" [I] | A kNN component inside a network gave TabR "the best average performance among tabular DL models" ([TabR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.14338)) [V] | Minutes, CPU |
| **A5. Identifiability table** | Per knob and class: ΔE against the constant with band intervals, posterior contraction (1 − predicted variance / label variance), z-scores or PIT, r and spread ratio, and mixture-weight entropy | Names the knobs that carry signal; the rest go to the constant | Contraction and z-scores as identifiability checks ([Schad et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.12765)) [S]; weak knobs in guitar papers ([Jürgens](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2020/proceedings/papers/DAFx2020_paper_2.pdf), [Hinrichs](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [V]; Okita fixed a hard knob to a constant ([Okita & Katayose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22276)) [V] | Minutes, CPU |
| **A6. Fall back when unsure** | Use the constant wherever the mixture's spread (or the disagreement between seeds) is above a threshold set on the early-stopping set; plot E against coverage | The optimal reject rule thresholds the conditional variance ([Zaoui et al.](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2020/file/e8219d4c93f6c55c6b10fe6bfe997c6c-Paper.pdf)) [S] | SelectiveNet cut its own MSE on UCI Concrete from 38.45 to 27.94 by rejecting 30% of cases ([Geifman & El-Yaniv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.09192)) [V] | Minutes, CPU |

**A2 can only help or tie, A1 should help, and both cost nothing.** A2's guarantee holds for every example, not only on average. So averaging four predictions per example (2 seeds × 2 halves) cannot do worse than the reading the pilot reported [I]. A1 has no such guarantee for a learned and possibly miscalibrated mixture, but both theory and the toys point the same way. **A3 is the strongest guarantee, and the easiest to fool.** It matches the constant on its fitting set by construction. With about 160 examples per amp-and-class cell and one weight per knob, the optimism is small [I]. The catch is where it is fitted. The early-stopping examples are whole sections of the *training* bands ([plan](../direct-model-stems-plan.md)) [P]. The model looks better there than on new bands, so weights fitted there will trust it too much on R_in [I]. Two guards help. A cross-fit that leaves one of V_sep's four bands out at a time shows how far in-band weights overstate the gain. But V_sep is R_in, so that cross-fit stays a descriptive check and never chooses the readout (next paragraph). And report the unconstrained b too: a value above 1 means the predictions are spread too narrowly for their correlation, as arm B's may have been, and pulling toward the constant cannot help [I].

**Use the readouts without spending step 3's test.** R_in's examples are also step 3's R_in, re-separated with X-LANCE ([plan, amendment 2](../direct-model-stems-plan.md)) [P]. Tuning any choice on R_in would contaminate step 3's early stop. The clean order is to declare in advance the rule that picks the readout, for example A1 + A2 always, plus A3 with weights fitted on the early-stopping set only. Then read R_in and R_out once, and propose that readout for step 3 as a reviewed plan amendment before step 3 is read. Whether to spend R_in this way is for the ML session and the user to decide [I]. The results then point in different directions [I]:

| If the readouts show | It means | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| kNN beats both D_stem and the constant | The features hold knob information that the head throws away | The one-retrain changes in the next section |
| kNN ties the constant only at large k, and the identifiability table shows almost no contraction | The stem features carry little knob information, so no head will fix E | Only the larger routes can help |
| The pre-declared readout (A1–A3, weights from the early-stopping set) brings D_stem below the constant on R_in and R_out | The readout was the main problem | Propose a reviewed amendment that makes it step 3's answer and early stop before anything else is spent; report the V_sep cross-fit beside it, not to choose |

The honest expectation is that these steps close the gap to about zero. Clearing an interval below zero needs real per-knob signal [I]. Test-time averaging over more crops is a further option, but the evidence is mixed: it "can change many correct predictions into incorrect predictions" ([Shanmugam et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2021/html/Shanmugam_Better_Aggregation_in_Test-Time_Augmentation_ICCV_2021_paper.html)) [S]. The two stored halves already give it for free.

## One 17-minute retrain per seed aims the head at the metric

The pilot's runs took **11–19 minutes each on MPS** at 170–180 examples a second, on 4,997 training examples ([pilot results](../direct-model-stems-pilot-results.md)) [P]. Every change below reuses the stored features and needs zero renders. The user's rule is per run: training that takes minutes is fine, and any longer run needs the user's approval before it starts ([ML plan](ml-plan-2026-10.md)) [P]. A pilot-size run takes minutes. Whether several seeds in a row count as one longer effort is the user's call [I]. Full-size runs take about 50–75 minutes each, since step 3's six runs are budgeted at 5–7.4 GPU hours [P], so ask first. The MPS device is also shared: the rules allow one MPS job at a time, and the X-LANCE stem stage keeps an MPS worker busy while it runs, so these retrains go before or after that stage ([ML plan](ml-plan-2026-10.md); [plan](../direct-model-stems-plan.md)) [P]. The user's earlier ruling waives approval only for runs already in an approved plan [P], and a changed head is not one of them [I].

| Change | What it fixes | Evidence | Mac cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1. Residual median head.** Output θ̂ = clip(median[amp, class] + Δ(x)), with Δ's last layer set to zero at the start and trained with absolute error (or the pinball loss at τ = 0.5, whose best answer is the median). Keep the mixture head as an extra output only if samples are needed | Trains the summary E scores. At step 0 the network *is* the constant, so every move away from it has to be earned [I] | Every guitar estimator in the sources reviewed uses bounded MSE or L1 regression ([Comunità](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1), [Hinrichs](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [V]. One network can learn every quantile with the pinball loss ([Tagasovska & Lopez-Paz](https://papers.NeurIPS.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/73c03186765e199c116224b68adc5fa0-Paper.pdf)) [V]. No paper tests a residual from a constant [V, by absence] | ~17 min per seed |
| **B2. Early stop on E.** Choose the epoch and checkpoint by median-read E on the early-stopping set, and raise the 40-epoch cap | Stops the likelihood from sharpening σ on easy examples while E stalls [I] | 3 of the pilot's 8 runs had their best epoch within 4 of the cap ([pilot results](../direct-model-stems-pilot-results.md)) [P] | A per-epoch median-read E, and a higher `MAX_EPOCHS` with its `--epochs` check ([direct_stems.py](../../learn/direct_stems.py)); 0–50% longer runs |
| **B3. Binned knob heads.** About 21 bins per knob with label smoothing and cross-entropy; read out the interpolated median bin | Gives a full per-knob distribution with no mixture collapse, and its median directly [I] | One-hot encoding of discrete numerical parameters cut DX7 MFCC error from 17.1 to 12.2, and numerical MAE from 0.119 to 0.103, at about 30k presets ([Le Vaillant et al. 2021](https://orbi.umons.ac.be/bitstream/20.500.12907/43332/1/DAFx20in21_paper_7.pdf)) [V]. Cross-entropy over bins "significantly improves performance" for value functions ([Farebrother et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03950)) [V] | ~17 min per seed |
| **B4. β-NLL (β = 0.5)**, if the mixture head stays | Restores the point fit that plain NLL loses | [Seitzer et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.09168) suggest β = 0.5 as "a good starting point" [V] | ~17 min per seed |
| **B5. Cepstral input arm.** A DCT of the stored log-mels, MFCC-like | Robustness to backing bleed [I] | MFCCs gave the lowest distortion error in mixes, and noise raised MFCC error by only 0.01–0.03, against 0.05–0.15 for spectrograms ([Hinrichs](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [V] | ~17 min per seed |
| **B6. Gradient-boosted trees.** One model per knob with an absolute-error objective, fed D_stem's pooled embedding plus descriptors derived from the mels; then stack {constant, CNN median, trees} with non-negative weights that sum to 1 | A second opinion that needs no GPU. Stacking with the constant keeps A3's guarantee [I] | Trees "remain state-of-the-art on medium-sized data (~10K samples)" ([Grinsztajn et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.08815v1)) [S]. Jürgens fed MFCC time-slopes to a 32-unit network [V]. A boosted model plus a log-mel CNN beat each alone in DCASE 2017 ([UPF](https://repositori.upf.edu/items/75745356-588d-453f-8d93-417871bba71d)) [S] | 10–30 min, CPU |
| **B7. More seeds** | Lowers variance through A2 | The pilot's seeds read +0.003 and +0.023 [P] | ~17 min each; 5 seeds is about 1.5 h in all, so ask the user whether that counts as one longer effort |

**B1 and B2 belong in one arm.** Together they replace the three mismatches (summary, loss and stopping rule) with the functional E scores, and they keep the constant as the starting point. This is the guitar literature's recipe with one addition, the residual. It has no published test, so its value here is an inference [I]. The closest published idea is SerumRNN's network, which sees the current and target audio and predicts the change between them ([Mitcheltree & Koike](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03876)) [V]. Decoding discrete structure first and continuous values second also lowered unseen-source parameter L1, from 0.168 to 0.125 for singing and from 0.213 to 0.161 for drums ([Lee et al. 2023](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2303.08610)) [V]. That supports keeping mic and switch heads separate from the knob head.

**B3 is the alternative arm if B1 fails, and its counter-evidence is about grids, not bins.** Jürgens's five-class gain classifier reached only 66% accuracy, and the authors switched to regression ([Jürgens](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2020/proceedings/papers/DAFx2020_paper_2.pdf)) [V]. Comunità's networks trained on a coarse grid of settings were biased toward the grid values ([Comunità](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.03216v1)) [V]. Both are about coarse *training labels*. Fine bins over continuous labels, read out as an interpolated median, avoid both problems [I]. The smallest published comparison of bins and regression used about 30k presets, so evidence at 5k is missing [V, by absence].

**B6 is worth running beside the GPU work because it separates two failures.** Suppose trees on D_stem's embedding beat the CNN's own head. Then the features hold information that the head loses [I]. One limit applies: the project keeps no audio, so pitch-based or crest-factor descriptors would need the stems re-made. Only descriptors computable from the stored 96-band log-mels are free [I]. All the tree evidence comes from tabular data or classification. No audio regression benchmark compares trees with a small CNN at 5k–20k examples [V, by absence].

## Bigger routes change the features and cost hours to days

These routes change what the model sees, not how it reads out. The project keeps no audio, and separation costs **4.3–4.9 s per example** ([pilot results](../direct-model-stems-pilot-results.md)) [P]. So any new input means re-making stems or rendering new examples. No published work tests any of these routes on amp-sim knobs or on separated stems [V, by absence].

| Route | Best evidence | Mac cost | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1. Frozen AFx-Rep embeddings** (mid and side, 512-d each) plus LUFS and amp/class conditioning, with a small MLP or median head. EfficientAT mn10 and LAION-CLAP serve as cheap controls, and Fx-Encoder++ as a non-commercial extra row | Single-knob search tracked distortion drive at ρ 0.944 (MSE 0.005) and MetalTone "dist" at ρ 0.862, against CLAP's 0.509 on MetalTone (on drive, CLAP reached ρ 0.852) ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)) [V]. Frozen probes on 3,600 labels gave parameter MAE ×100 of 17.7 for AFx-Rep, 18.8 for Fx-Encoder++ and 19.7 for PANN ([RLAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28127)) [V] | About 0.2–0.4 s per half on CPU, so 0.4–0.8 s per example: about 8–19% of the separation time, or 12–27% of the stage's wall time if run in series; about 189 MB stored for the stage's ~23k examples [I, estimates]; the head trains in minutes | Small margins in the probe ("smaller than for chain estimation"); the authors say ST-ITO "does not work well" for guitar tone matching [V]; never tested on stems |

| **C4. A small contrastive encoder trained by the project** (Open-Amp recipe: same settings through different riffs as positives) | A 113k-parameter encoder plus an MLP beat a supervised 760k-parameter CNN on its own pedal dataset, 87.9% against 86.9% ([Open-Amp](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.14972)) [V] | MPS time unmeasured, probably hours [I] | That gain is for classification. | **C5. Sound-domain routes**: a proxy audio loss, RL on rendered rewards, or search through the plugin after prediction | Better sound, flat or worse knob error (see below) [V] | Hours of MPS, or 1–24 min per song per amp for search [I] | The wrong lever for E |

**C1 has a deadline.** Amendment 2 re-separates every pilot, V_sep and remaining example with X-LANCE, in a stage that takes about 19–22 hours ([plan](../direct-model-stems-plan.md)) [P]. If embedding extraction runs inside that stage, it adds an estimated 0.4–0.8 s per example on CPU (two halves) against 4.3–4.9 s of separation [I]. If it does not, a later trial of C1 costs a second full separation pass [I]. Three practical points matter. **The spectrogram must run on the CPU:** AFx-Rep computes it with torchlibrosa, whose STFT is a 2,048-tap `Conv1d` ([torchlibrosa](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/qiuqiangkong/torchlibrosa/master/torchlibrosa/stft.py)) [V], and a conv1d of that size has already failed on MPS in this project ([direc.py](../../learn/direc.py), the comment in `_real_stft`) [P]. **Level must be added back:** the official extractor peak-normalises each clip, which throws away level, a drive cue ([st-ito utils](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/csteinmetz1/st-ito/main/st_ito/utils.py)) [V], so the project's exact LUFS should be concatenated to the embedding [I]. **The released config, not the paper, sets the front end:** hop 1024 and 128 mels from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, which match the weights ([config](https://huggingface.co/csteinmetz1/afx-rep/resolve/main/config.yaml)) [V]. A kill rule keeps C1 cheap: if no frozen-embedding row beats the constant by its interval at 5k, frozen features alone will not rescue step 3 [I]. Because this changes build code mid-run, it needs a plan amendment, a code review and the user's yes [I].

**C2 ranks above C3 and C4 for E, because it trains the target directly.** Contrastive pretraining helps retrieval and classification a lot, but its measured gain on parameter MAE was tiny ([RLAT](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.28127)) [V]. Supervised pretraining on clean renders uses free labels and the exact regression target. One possible shortcut is unchecked. Arm B's bank stores 10k clean renders as FLAC with their full settings (`N_BANK` in [song_bank.py](../../learn/song_bank.py)) [P]. Its labels are drawn differently: half uniform, half σ 0.1 jitter around S or a K_S optimum, against the direct model's 40/20/40 mix [P]. If its amps and label space match, it is a zero-render pretraining set, with that prior difference to allow for [I].

**C5 helps sound, not E, and an E gate would stop it.** The evidence runs one way. Adding a proxy audio term to a parameter loss improved Mel error by 8–17% in-domain, while numerical L1 rose from 0.074 to 0.082 on Dexed and from 0.117 to 0.129 on Diva ([Combes et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07635v1)) [V]. For a dynamics processor, a learned estimator had parameter MSE 0.05, while Bayesian optimisation reached 0.23 and pattern search 0.36, even though both searches matched the target's features far better (0.41 and 0.29 against 0.90) ([Sun et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19645v1)) [V]. Search gains on guitar also used the true dry signal ([Okita & Katayose](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22276)) [V]. On songs the project's dry estimate is C′, which ranks the true target below the start preset on 7 of 12 known-target cells ([arm B plan](../song-settings-plan.md)) [P]. These routes matter once the gate moves from E to judged renders, not before [I].

**For songs rather than R_in, the training prior can be corrected without retraining.** The generator's label distribution is known exactly. So the network's per-knob posterior can be re-weighted toward a factory-like prior, by taking importance-weighted samples and then the weighted median [I]. Balanced MSE shows this kind of correction moves accuracy between dense and rare label regions: few-shot MAE fell from 26.33 to 23.05, while many-shot MAE rose from 7.23 to 7.58 ([Ren et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.16427)) [V]. A constant built only from factory and start draws would also be a fairer bar for deployment, since the 40% uniform share pulls today's median toward 0.5 [I]. Neither change affects R_in, which has no prior shift [I].

## Conclusion

The step-3 gate, as declared, scores a readout that theory says is the wrong one for its own metric, and its "ensemble" picks one seed's component instead of averaging. So MODEL STOP could fire on a model that a matched readout would pass. The fix is a declared change to the readout, not to the model, and it has to be made before step 3 is read. The shrinkage view also reframes the goal. Matching the constant is guaranteed by construction on any set the weights are fitted to. The real question is whether a gain fitted on in-band examples survives on new bands. That is a question about signal, which the kNN and identifiability readouts answer in minutes, before any further separation hours are committed.

A gain on E is not the product's goal, either. The routes the literature finds best for sound (search, audio losses, rendered rewards) tend to raise knob error, and a model can drop below the median on E while sounding no better. Once D_stem clears the constant, the next gate should be judged renders, with E as a side readout. Until then, E is the right target only because it is the gate the plan declared.
