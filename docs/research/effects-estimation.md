# Test effect candidates through the song's masking

*Research, 2026-11-12, for the ML session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

*Tags: **[M]** measured and published ("abstract" or "summary" when only those were read); **[R]** measured in this repo; **[I]** inferred; **[none]** no evidence found. Every test design, pass bar and recipe credited to the researchers is an untested proposal. This report builds on `docs/research/blind-reverb-delay-2026-11.md` (PR #371; its first test ran as F51–F52 in `docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`, and its notes folder is no longer on disk), `docs/research/effect-detection-2026-10.md`, `docs/research/proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md`, `docs/research/model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md` and `docs/research/efficientat-pretrained-reuse-2026-11.md`, and does not repeat them.*

**What is proven.** On solo guitar, telling whether tremolo, chorus, delay or reverb is on is close to solved: per-effect F1 is 0.94–0.999 on real DI phrases from guitars the model never heard ([Rossi et al., DAFx25](https://dafx25.dii.univpm.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/DAFx25_paper_16.pdf)) [M]. One group has regressed tremolo rate and depth under a backing, on one held note in synthetic mixes, at errors of 0.03–0.07 on a 0–1 knob ([Hinrichs et al. 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [M]. Searching a plugin chain against a reference of other content reads reverb size and delay mix on isolated sources ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)) [M]. In this repo, picking the amp by rendering stock DIs was checked on self-built mixes against the unit's amp track: better than the model's own amp head by score on cap1 and GOAT (pooled undecided), and narrowly by ear (F81, F83) [R]. **What is not.** No published method reports per-knob accuracy for one instrument's reverb, delay or modulation in a real song or a separated stem; on mixtures there is only whole-mix reverb and chain retrieval [none, M]. Nothing detects a spring reverb, a doubler, stereo width or tempo sync, and no music wet/dry estimator exists. There is no peer-reviewed measurement of guitar tail survival and no published fine-tune recipe for keeping tails [none]. Separators train on wet stems, about 75% of the wet energy survives in X-LANCE's stem, and F49–F52 put the loss on the backing's masking [M, R]. **Where it stands.** The ML session declared its effects route, with R4 (the song research's effects on the dry answer) as floor and default, and ran T0, a zero-render matching test on E1e's 770 draws (F85) [R]. On the clean target, matching reads every effect's on/off and most knobs; on the stem only reverb and delay on/off survive, weakly (AUC 0.57, 0.58), and no knob does [R]. Delay time went to R3, and T0's stem matching hit 13% within 10%, below F52's direct cepstrum (28.4%) [R]. This report's first test is now declared as T1: render-and-compare for delay time, each candidate added to the song's residual (mix minus stem) and re-separated, so candidate and song pass through the same channel (`docs/full-controls-plan.md`) [R]. It costs 3,484 renders counting primings and 1,742 X-LANCE separations, with no training or download [R]. Three zero-render readers outside T0 could follow [I]. Doubler on/off can be tested in mono on stored labels, though whether T0's draws carry it remains in conflict; spread, width and pan wait for stereo [R].

## Solo readers work, and none has met a real song

Detection results come from real audio and carry across guitars and datasets [M]. Rossi's CNN, scored leave-one-guitar-out on 400 DI improvisations, found tremolo the hardest effect: its F1 fell from 0.999 to 0.963 once the rate was drawn over 2–10 Hz, and to 0.937 with the chain order shuffled too [M]. Guo & McFee's model, trained on GuitarSet and tested on IDMT-SMT-Guitar, scores 0.93–0.99 per time or modulation effect, but at fixed settings, so that is a ceiling ([DAFx23](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2023/DAFx23_paper_30.pdf)) [M]. Hinrichs's CNN classifies 97.4% of IDMT-SMT's solo notes and 90.0% in its synthetic mixes [M].

Regression under masking is thinner. Hinrichs's tremolo errors rise about 1.5× from the quietest to the loudest backing (Fig. 7c, MFCC input), and the rate error rises by 0.10–0.15 more once the backing leaves its trained pitches [M]. A real song changes pitch all the time [I]. Delay feedback was never regressed: below 0.3 it was "very difficult to distinguish" from slapback, and listeners agreed [M]. For reverb decay, the best published single-channel figure on music comes from averaging the cepstra of long blocks into an impulse response: EDT correlation 0.981 and T30 error 22%, against 53% for fitting free decays, on minutes of whole-reverberated music with no backing ([Adrian & Bitzer 2014](https://new.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2014/HTML/papers/1569924725.pdf)) [M]. The repo's learned reader does not read decay even on the clean target (F49, Spearman 0.02), and no cepstral-mean estimate has been tried there [R, none]. No music DRR or wet/dry estimator has appeared since 2024 [none]. On mixtures, a CLAP probe reads RT60 at R² 0.77, but with one impulse response over the whole mix ([arXiv 2607.03806](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03806)) [M]. The repo's tremolo probe (target 0.72, mix 0.55, stem 0.52; `docs/research/effect-detection-2026-10.md`) is still the only stem-level evidence for modulation [R].

## New estimators report style, not knobs, and skip mixtures

The 2024–26 work moved to chain planners and relative encoders, judged by style-transfer distance rather than knob error, so none gives per-effect figures to set beside F49–F52 [I]. DeepAFx-ST covers only EQ and compression, and in a blind test it scored worse than leaving the input alone ([LLM2Fx-Tools](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01559), Table 3) [M]. TAPE names the plugin well (72.5–78.7%) but its configuration rarely: at best 25.6–39.0% when trained for it, at a cost to plugin accuracy ([TAPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40759), Table 4) [M]. Mixtures cost even trained encoders: StemFX's retrieval of an 8-effect chain fell from 86.8% to 48.0% when the stereo mix replaced the stems and FiLM conditioning was dropped too, and a single effect is retrieved only 3.0–16.8% of the time ([StemFX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15634)) [M]. No 2025–26 estimator reads tremolo or spring reverb, and doubler, width and pan appear only as a widener, panner or imager with no per-effect figure [M, I]. The differentiable libraries ([GRAFX](https://github.com/sh-lee97/grafx), [dasp-pytorch](https://github.com/csteinmetz1/dasp-pytorch), [NablAFx](https://github.com/mcomunita/nablafx); Apache-2.0 or MIT) need a dry signal or a reference pair [M], so here they could only stand in for the plugin, which already renders [I].

## Separators keep tails by design, and the backing buries them

The effects route reads X-LANCE `gtr_mss`, BS-RoFormer SW's frozen guitar output, whose training data are undisclosed ([config](https://huggingface.co/spaces/chenxie95/xlance-msr/raw/main/configs/gtr_mss.yaml); [X-LANCE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09042v1)) [M]. Music separators with documented data train on stems as they sit in the mix: MedleyDB's stems carry their effects ([MedleyDB](https://medleydb.weebly.com/description.html)), and MSRBench says MUSDB18-HQ's and MoisesDB's "already contain production effects" ([MSRBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10995v1)) [M]. Dereverb and restoration models, by contrast, train on dry targets [M]. So a separator is asked to keep a source's own tails [I]. There is no peer-reviewed measurement of a guitar's tail survival [none]. A community stress test found Demucs and Spleeter keep added vocal reverb and delay ([StressingSourceSeparation](https://github.com/ivanarasch/StressingSourceSeparation)) [M, not peer-reviewed], and the repo's check found about 75% of the wet energy in X-LANCE's stem, the rest in the backing's sustained-instrument stem [R].

SDR cannot see this. Dropping a tail that holds 5% of a stem's energy costs a perfect stem only 13 dB, above the guitar SDRs near 9 dB that leaderboards report [I]. The one published wet-target training recipe is CrowdioSet's: SCNet trained from scratch with reverberant targets. Under noisy-and-reverberant (noisyrev) evaluation its reverb-trained models score 3.55–3.63 dB on "other" against 1.43–1.79 for clean- or noisy-trained ones; both pairs isolate reverb training. Under clean evaluation they lose a little (5.98 and 5.88 against 6.69), and it has no guitar stem ([CrowdioSet](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27828v1), Table 2) [M]. Dry-target restoration aims at the wrong signal for reading effects [I]. Fine-tuning tools exist ([MSST](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/utils/settings.py)) [M], but no published fine-tune recipe targets tails [none]. The project's own wet-target fine-tune shrank its stems to a median −33 LUFS and lost to stock on 9 of 11 real parts (D_F5ft, `docs/trusted-data-t0-design.md`) [R]. The researchers' tail recipe (a dB-domain loss, an anti-shrink term, extra weight where wet minus dry dominates; untested) would attack the quarter of the energy the separator moves, not the masking that dominates [I].

## Render-and-compare already crosses content; the matched channel is untested

The project's amp pick compared clean stock-DI renders (other content, no backing) with the separated stem, and it worked. On self-built mixes, judged against the unit's amp track, it beat always-sw50r on cap1 by −0.049, and the model's own amp head on cap1 and GOAT, though the pooled read against that head is undecided (−0.022 [−0.044, +0.001]) (F81) [R]. By ear it was heard closer than the model's pick, 0.67 (p 0.041), but dropping any one of its 9 "above" cards makes that undecided (F83) [R]. On the clean amp track the pick gained more (cap1 −0.081), so separation costs about half (F81) [R]. ST-ITO also works across content, and optimising on RLAT's content-invariant embedding beats AFx-Rep within an instrument (MR-STFT 0.575 against 0.663) while the two tie on the same content [M]. So matched content was not required for the amp pick, nor, on the clean guitar, for effects (T0, F85) [R, I]. No published render-and-compare has been tried on a mixture or a stem for reverb, delay or tremolo [none]. Whether passing candidates through the song's own channel (residual plus re-separation) helps is what T1's A − B contrast checks [R, I].

The features split by effect. Decay statistics need pauses, which music lacks [M]. A spectrogram loss is insensitive to the rate of amplitude modulation, while joint time-frequency scattering has a single minimum there, in a synthetic test without masking ([Vahidi et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.10183)) [M]. Music's own modulation spectrum peaks near 2 Hz ([Ding et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/059683.full.pdf)) [M, abstract], right where synced tremolo rates and beat-length delays sit, so a reader must contrast the stem with the song's own rhythm [I]. Rates and times make rugged search landscapes (Okita fixed the chorus rate), so a grid is safer [M, I]. False cepstral peaks reach the top quartile's height on 21% of F52's delay-offs [R]; to the extent the backing makes them, the same masking should put them in both candidate and song, where they cancel [I].

The doubler is not like the other stereo controls. It moves F67's DI-referenced mono measure more than any other effect (median +4.82 dB, at least +1.41 on every row), and it is on in 767 of E1a's targets, as reverb-like sound the label ignores [R]. So its on/off can be tested in mono on stored labels, though F67's measure is DI-referenced: it shows mono doesn't block the doubler, not that a song-only reader recovers it [I]. F18 lists what a mono input cannot read: doubler spread, delay width, ping-pong, cab pans and cab stereo [R]. Whether T0's draws carry the doubler is in conflict (next section) [R].

## No dataset labels a song's guitar effects

No public set gives graded tremolo, feedback, delay-mix, decay or pre-delay settings over real playing, and none pairs a real song with each guitar's effect settings [none]. Real hardware comes at a few settings: EGFxSet's DL4 echo at "120 bpm" (500 ms as a quarter note [I]), CR-60 spring emulation and CE-3 chorus, SpringSet's real tank, and ToneTwist's tremolo pedal at 5 settings, enough for a coarse rate check [M, I]. Rossi's DI chains draw the tremolo rate over 2–10 Hz, but the file names hold presence only [M]. Hinrichs's mix set was promised but never found [none]. For real mixes, MedleyDB's raw-and-stem pairs and MSRBench's 250 guitar clips with dry and processed stems allow fitted pseudo-labels [M, I]. MixologyDB has published one example mix of 114 announced, with per-track reverb sends, and it draws partly on Weathervane sessions [M]. The user declined a paid Weathervane membership (`docs/research/di-source-inventory-2026-10.md`), while MedleyDB's 24 Weathervane songs sit in held-out set 6 (`docs/validation-set6.md`); whether MixologyDB's annotations are usable is the user's call [R]. Nothing labels a doubler setting, a width or tempo sync [none], and E1a's 2,640 examples all record sync off [R]. So external sets serve as presence and realism checks under the project's backings; labels must come from its own renders, and each download is the ML session's and the user's call [I, R].

## Conclusion

Effect readers that work have heard the source alone, and render-and-compare has worked across content but has never passed its candidates through the song's own channel [M, R]. T0 showed both halves in this repo: matching finds most settings on the clean guitar, and the separated stem loses nearly all of them (F85) [R]. The song's own backing, the masking every tail had to survive, is the one asset no published method has used, and T1 now tests it on delay time [R, I]. A pass would make the same search worth trying on delay mix and tremolo rate, at about 26 renders per item per knob counting primings; a fail would narrow the route without closing it [I]. Doubler on/off is a separate cheap mono test either way [I]. What follows T1, against the main line, stays with the ML session and the user (F52; `docs/full-controls-plan.md`) [R].
