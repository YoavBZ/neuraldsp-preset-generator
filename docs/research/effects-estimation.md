# Render effect candidates through the song's masking

*Research, 2026-11-12, for the ML session; not a declared plan. Web sources only: nothing was downloaded, installed or run.*

*Tags: **[M]** measured and published ("abstract" or "summary" when only those were read); **[R]** measured in this repo; **[I]** inferred; **[none]** no evidence found. Every test design, pass bar and recipe credited to the researchers is an untested proposal. This report builds on `docs/research/blind-reverb-delay-2026-11.md` (PR #371; its first test ran as F51–F52 in `docs/pipeline-fix-plan.md`, and its notes folder is no longer on disk), `docs/research/effect-detection-2026-10.md`, `docs/research/proven-methods-preset-recovery-2026-11.md`, `docs/research/model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md` and `docs/research/efficientat-pretrained-reuse-2026-11.md`, and does not repeat them.*

**What is proven.** On solo guitar, telling whether tremolo, chorus, delay or reverb is on is close to solved: per-effect F1 is 0.94–0.999 on real DI phrases from guitars the model never heard ([Rossi et al., DAFx25](https://dafx25.dii.univpm.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/DAFx25_paper_16.pdf)) [M]. Tremolo rate and depth have been regressed under a backing only once, on one held note in synthetic mixes, at errors of 0.03–0.07 on a 0–1 knob ([Hinrichs et al. 2022](https://www.tnt.uni-hannover.de/papers/data/1571/EURASIP_2022.pdf)) [M]. A search over a plugin chain, against a reference with different content, reads reverb size and delay mix on isolated sources ([ST-ITO](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.21233)) [M]. In this repo the same idea works for the amp: render stock DIs, take the nearest, and the pick beats the model's own by score and by ear (F80–F83) [R]. **What is not.** No published method reads any reverb, delay or modulation setting from a real song or a separated stem [none]. None detects a spring reverb, a doubler, a stereo width or a tempo-synced delay, no music wet/dry estimator exists, and no separator has been measured or fine-tuned for keeping tails [none]. The 2024–26 estimators report style distances or retrieval, not per-effect knob accuracy on mixtures, and none reads tremolo [M]. Separators train on wet stems and so keep tails by design [M, I]. About 75% of the wet energy survives in X-LANCE's stem, and F49–F52 put the loss on the backing's masking [R]. **What the evidence best supports.** First, extend the amp's render-and-compare to the time effects, but add each candidate to the song's own residual (mix minus stem) and re-separate it, so candidate and song pass through the same masking; search rates and times on a grid (untested proposal) [I]. Second, put every knob that nothing reads on a draw-aware prior, and keep on/off from the reader [R, I]. Doubler, width and pan wait for stereo audio, because every stored mix is mono [R]. The cheapest decisive test runs the first move on delay time, the knob the clean target reads best, on F52's 134 strong-third stems. It costs about 1,700 renders and 2.1 h of X-LANCE, with no training and no download.

## Solo readers work, and none has met a real song

Detection results come from real audio and carry across guitars and datasets [M]. Rossi's CNN, scored leave-one-guitar-out on 400 DI improvisations, found tremolo the hardest effect: its F1 fell from 0.999 to 0.963 once the rate was drawn over 2–10 Hz, and to 0.937 with the chain order shuffled too [M]. Guo & McFee's model, trained on GuitarSet and tested on IDMT-SMT-Guitar, scores 0.93–0.99 per time or modulation effect, but at fixed settings, so that is a ceiling ([DAFx23](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/2023/DAFx23_paper_30.pdf)) [M]. Hinrichs's CNN classifies 97.4% of IDMT-SMT's solo notes and 90.0% in its synthetic mixes [M].

Regression under masking is thinner. Hinrichs's tremolo errors rise about 1.5× from the quietest to the loudest backing, and the rate error rises by 0.10–0.15 more once the backing leaves the pitches it was trained on [M]. A real song changes pitch all the time [I]. Delay feedback was never regressed: below 0.3 it was "very difficult to distinguish" from slapback, and listeners agreed [M]. For reverb decay, the best published single-channel figure on music comes from averaging the cepstra of blocks of 6 s or more into a minimum-phase impulse response [M]. It reached an early-decay-time (EDT) correlation of 0.981 and a T30 error of 22% on music with whole-signal reverb and no backing, against 53% for fitting free decays ([Adrian & Bitzer 2014](https://new.eurasip.org/Proceedings/Eusipco/Eusipco2014/HTML/papers/1569924725.pdf)) [M]. That is the same family as the repo's delay cepstrum, yet decay does not read even on the clean target (F49, Spearman 0.02) [R]. No music DRR or wet/dry estimator has appeared since 2024 [none]; the new room estimators target speech or arrays [M, summary]. The repo's tremolo probe (target 0.72, mix 0.55, stem 0.52; `docs/research/effect-detection-2026-10.md`) is still the only stem-level evidence for modulation [R].

## New estimators report style, not knobs, and skip mixtures

The 2024–26 work moved to chain planners and relative encoders, judged by style-transfer distance rather than knob error, so none gives per-effect figures to set beside F49–F52 [I]. DeepAFx-ST covers only EQ and compression [M], and in a blind test it scored a worse audio-feature distance than leaving the input alone ([LLM2Fx-Tools](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01559), Table 3) [M]. TAPE names the plugin 72.5–78.7% of the time, but its exact configuration almost never ([TAPE](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.40759)) [M]. Mixtures cost even trained encoders: StemFX's retrieval of an 8-effect chain fell from 86.8% to 48.0% when the stereo mix replaced the stems, and a single effect is retrieved only 3.0–16.8% of the time ([StemFX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15634)) [M]. No 2025–26 estimator reads tremolo or spring reverb; TAPE's encoder saw modulation plugins, but its decoder has no modulation stage [M, I]. Doubler, width and pan appear only as a widener, panner or imager with no per-effect figure [M]. The differentiable libraries, [GRAFX](https://github.com/sh-lee97/grafx) (Apache-2.0), [dasp-pytorch](https://github.com/csteinmetz1/dasp-pytorch) (Apache-2.0, no push since 2023-12) and [NablAFx](https://github.com/mcomunita/nablafx) (MIT), are decoders that need a dry signal or a reference pair [M]. Here they could only be the synthesis half of a search, a role the plugin already fills [I].

## Separators keep tails by design, and the backing buries them

The effects route reads X-LANCE `gtr_mss`, which is BS-RoFormer SW's frozen guitar output ([config](https://huggingface.co/spaces/chenxie95/xlance-msr/raw/main/configs/gtr_mss.yaml); [X-LANCE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09042v1)) [M]. SW's training data are undisclosed [R]. Every separator whose data are documented trains on stems as they sit in the mix, with whatever effects the mix gave them ([MedleyDB](https://medleydb.weebly.com/description.html); [MSRBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10995v1)) [M], so it is asked to keep a source's own tails [I]. No paper measures how much of a guitar's tail survives [none]. A community stress test found that Demucs and Spleeter keep added vocal reverb and delay ([StressingSourceSeparation](https://github.com/ivanarasch/StressingSourceSeparation)) [M, not peer-reviewed]. The repo's check found about 75% of the wet energy in X-LANCE's stem, with the rest in the backing's sustained-instrument stem [R].

SDR cannot see this. Dropping a tail that holds 5% of a stem's energy costs a perfect stem only 13 dB, above the guitar SDRs near 9 dB that leaderboards report [I]. Dry-target restoration aims at the wrong signal for reading effects [I], and on guitar its baseline sits at −19.4 dB SI-SNR ([MSRBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.10995v1)) [M]. Fine-tuning tools exist ([MSST](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ZFTurbo/Music-Source-Separation-Training/main/utils/settings.py)) [M], but no published recipe keeps tails [none]. The project's own wet-target fine-tune shrank its stems to a median −33 LUFS and was worse than stock on 9 of 11 real parts (D_F5ft, `docs/trusted-data-t0-design.md`) [R]. The researchers' tail recipe (untested proposal) has three parts: a dB-domain loss, an anti-shrink term, and extra weight where wet minus dry dominates. It would attack the quarter of the energy the separator moves, not the masking that dominates [I].

## Render-and-compare works only where content and masking match

The project's amp choice is the one render-and-compare checked by ear on real songs. Stock DIs, scored by nearness in the model's pooled features, beat always-sw50r on cap1 by −0.049, and the pick was heard closer to the record than the model's own amp pick (0.67, p 0.041) [R]. On the clean amp track it recovered about 40% of the headroom, so the stem was again the bottleneck (F80) [R]. Published render-and-compare for effects runs only on isolated sources [M], and none has been tried on a mixture or a stem for reverb, delay or tremolo [none]. Content invariance matters more as the content departs: optimising on RLAT's content-invariant embedding beat AFx-Rep within an instrument (MR-STFT 0.575 against 0.663) and across instruments (0.647 against 0.832), while the two tie on the same content [M].

The features split by effect. Decay statistics need pauses, which music lacks [M]. A spectrogram loss is insensitive to the rate of amplitude modulation, while joint time-frequency scattering has a single minimum at that rate, in a synthetic test without masking ([Vahidi et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.10183)) [M]. Music's own modulation spectrum peaks near 2 Hz, and rock's at 2–3 Hz ([Ding et al.](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/059683.full.pdf)) [M, abstract]. Synced tremolo rates and beat-length delays sit right there, so any reader must contrast the stem against the song's own rhythm [I]. Rates and times make rugged search landscapes, which is why Okita fixed the chorus rate; a grid is the safer search [M, I]. The masking-channel comparison is the researchers' answer to all of this (untested proposal). It costs about 1.6 s of rendering and 4.3 s of separation per candidate [R]. Because the candidate meets the song's own backing, false cepstral peaks (21% of F52's delay-offs reach the top quartile's height [R]) should, to the extent the backing makes them, appear on both sides and cancel [I].

## No dataset labels a song's guitar effects

No public set gives graded tremolo, feedback, delay-mix, decay or pre-delay settings over real playing, and none pairs a real song with each guitar's effect settings [none]. Real hardware exists only at single settings. EGFxSet records a Line 6 DL4 echo at one setting, "120 bpm" (a quarter note at 120 BPM is 500 ms [I]), a CR-60 spring emulation and a CE-3 chorus ([EGFxSet](https://zenodo.org/records/7044411)) [M], and SpringSet has a real spring tank ([SpringSet](https://zenodo.org/records/3746119)) [M]. Rossi's 33 GB of DI chains draw the tremolo rate over 2–10 Hz, but the file names hold presence only [M]. Hinrichs's mix set was promised but never released [M]. For real mixes, MedleyDB's raw-and-stem pairs and MSRBench's 250 guitar clips with dry and processed stems allow fitted pseudo-labels [M, I]. MixologyDB's per-track pan values from 114 real sessions give a pan prior ([MixologyDB](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mclemcrew/MixologyDB/blob/main/README.md)) [M, I]. Nothing labels a doubler, a width or tempo sync [none], and E1a's 2,640 examples all record sync off [R]. So external sets serve as presence and hardware-realism checks under the project's own backings, while the labels must come from the project's renders [I]. Each download is the ML session's and the user's call [R].

Between the notes, three conflicts reconcile. ## The cheapest decisive first test

The test asks whether comparing candidates through the song's own masking reads delay time on the stem, where F52's cepstrum fails (untested proposal). Delay time is the right knob to try first because the clean target reads it best (57% within ±10%, F51), while the mixes and feedback read there only weakly (Spearman 0.33–0.38, F49) [R]. If render-and-compare cannot recover it on the stem with every other knob known, no time-effect knob will come from this route [I]. The effects would then settle on priors plus on/off [I].

The test respects the project facts, each checked in the repo [R]. Only the clean targets and other guitars are stored as audio, plus F52's 770 rebuilt mixes and stems (`e1e/audio/`, outside git) and E0's 84 stems (F50–F52). Mixes rebuild on CPU with zero renders (`learn/trusted_data.py`, `self_built_mix`). X-LANCE takes about 4.3 s a clip on MPS (`docs/data-cleaning-options.md`), and F52's 770 clips took about 60 min. Renders take about 1.6 s each, with no cap (`docs/research/model-features-judge-fit-2026-11.md`). Stored mixes are mono. Training longer than minutes needs the user's approval (`docs/ROADMAP.md`, 2026-10-20), and downloads are the ML session's and the user's call (`docs/research/blind-reverb-delay-2026-11.md`).

| Part | Specification |
|---|---|
| Inputs | F52's 134 strong-third wide delay-ons (the top third of delay mix), with their stored mix and stem; residual = mix − stem |
| Candidates | Each item's true preset with delay time at 13 log-spaced points from 66 to 733 ms (ratio 1.22, so each point covers its own ±10% cell of the 60–800 ms draw), rendered through one stock DI of the item's class (A1's: The Travelling Band 16 for clean, Ale Lak 09 for crunch), loudness-matched to the stem |
| Arm A (primary) | Candidate plus residual, re-separated by X-LANCE. Pick the candidate whose stem's cepstrum (F52's primary arm, 60–800 ms) correlates best with the song stem's |
| Arm B | The same candidates compared clean against the song's stem, with no re-separation, to show whether the channel is what helps |
| Arm C (free) | The stem's cepstrum minus a multiple of the residual's, fitted leaving one song out, on all 402 wide delay-ons |
| Metric | Within ±10% of the set time, and log2 error against the draw-aware prior, each paired against F52's cepstrum on the same items (28.4% on this third), with 90% intervals from 2,000 resamples of the 7 band clusters |
| Pass bar | Declared before reading: arm A beats F52's cepstrum (interval wholly above 0) and its log2 error beats the draw-aware prior's (interval wholly below 0). F52 failed this second half. A − B above 0 credits the channel |
| Cost | 1,742 renders (about 46 min at 1.6 s; less on several processes); 1,742 X-LANCE separations (about 2.1 h on MPS, which the renders can overlap); residual sums on CPU; no training; no download |
| Then | Pass: run recommendation 3. Fail: delay time stays on a prior on stems, and the effect knobs stop until stereo or real-song evidence changes the case |

Three caveats bound what it shows [I]. First, the residual still holds about a quarter of the true wet energy, but a real song's residual holds it too. Second, the candidates skip the seeded mastering the song went through. Third, setting the other knobs to their true values makes this an upper bound on context, though not on content, since the DI is generic.

## Conclusion

The field and the project fail at the same seam. Every effect reader that works has either heard the source alone or matched its content, and a song-only product can offer neither. What the song does offer, and no published method has used, is its own backing: the exact masking every tail had to survive. The first test spends that asset on the easiest knob. A pass would make the backing a tool instead of noise, and it would carry over to tremolo rate and delay mix at about 13 renders per item per knob [I]. A fail would close the question: the product's time effects would be priors plus an on/off reader at about 0.6 AUC [R], and the remaining effort would go to stereo capture, without which the doubler, width and pan cannot be attempted [R].
